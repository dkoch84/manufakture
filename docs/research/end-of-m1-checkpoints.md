# End-of-M1 checkpoints: kernel leak, recycle threshold, planegcs headroom

- Status: research, input to [ADR 0001](../adr/0001-kernel-wrapper.md) decision 4, [ADR 0002](../adr/0002-kernel-build-and-loading.md) decision 5 and [ADR 0003](../adr/0003-sketch-solver.md) decision 8
- Date: 2026-10-04
- Question: three decisions were deferred to "after M1": whether the custom libcascade build (or the occt-wasm fallback) is needed, which heap threshold recycles the kernel instance, and whether our own planegcs build is needed before sketches grow. M1 to M7 have landed, so each can now be decided on the real acceptance models instead of spike scenarios.

## Summary

| Checkpoint                          | Measured                                                                                                                                                         | Decision                                                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ADR 0001: custom build or occt-wasm | The M1 bracket leaks 0.19 to 0.21 MiB per regen. The heaviest models leak 4 to 5 MiB per full regen (M3 jig, M6 house); a recycle plus replay costs 0.4 to 2.4 s | Recycling is enough for now. Neither the custom build nor the fallback is started; both become a follow-up with a concrete trigger |
| ADR 0002: recycle threshold         | Every model's regen fits in the initial 128 MiB; at 512 MiB the heaviest model recycles after 83 full regens, at under 2 % of regen time                         | `DEFAULT_HEAP_THRESHOLD` goes from 1 GiB to **512 MiB**                                                                            |
| ADR 0003: own planegcs build        | The shipped binary solves 115 coupled, fully constrained entities and aborts at 120; the largest sketch in any acceptance model has 16 entities                  | Not needed now. The build is a follow-up, needed before any feature makes coupled sketches of about 100 entities                   |

## Method

All numbers are from Node 26.10.0 on an AMD Ryzen 5 7600X (12 logical CPUs, 62 GiB), with the pinned libcascade 3.0.2 single-threaded build and `@salusoft89/planegcs` 1.2.0, on 2026-10-04.

**The models.** The acceptance models of each milestone, built from the same commands the e2e fixtures use (`apps/web/e2e/m*-fixtures.ts`), and regenerated by a real `RegenEngine` with the real kernel service, planegcs, the text engine with the bundled Inter Bold, and the stock, wood and construction domains registered as the app's regen worker registers them:

| Model                     | Source                                                                              | Features | Bodies | What stresses the kernel                                           |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| M1 bracket                | the walkthrough of `docs/m1-acceptance.md` as commands (what `e2e/bracket.ts` does) | 5        | 1      | symmetric extrude, two counterbored holes, a fillet                |
| M2 wall shelf             | `shelfDocumentCommands` (`e2e/m2-fixtures.ts`): the shelf and drawer parts          | 10       | 5      | several bodies; the derived supports and the assembly are left out |
| M3 jig                    | `jigCommands` (`e2e/m3-fixtures.ts`)                                                | 19       | 2      | two modelled threads, a text label cut, a chamfer, slots           |
| M4 bookshelf              | the batch of `buildBookshelf` (`e2e/m4-fixtures.ts`)                                | 37       | 12     | twelve boards, dados, rabbets and pocket-screw joints              |
| M5 sign                   | `plateCommands` and `cutCommands` (`e2e/m5-fixtures.ts`)                            | 8        | 1      | a 400 x 200 mm plate, a border groove, holes, lettering cut 2 mm   |
| M6 house                  | `houseCommands` (`packages/domain-construction/src/fixtures/house.ts`)              | 24       | 28     | 11 walls of layered bodies, 794 framing members (Manifold)         |
| M7 bolt circle (12 holes) | the bolt-circle script of `e2e/script-examples.ts` as a scripted feature            | 1        | 1      | a QuickJS script driving extrude, cut and a circular pattern       |

Two stand-ins: the M5 lettering is the same word in the bundled Inter Bold as a text outline (the walkthrough imports it from an SVG of Inter Bold converted to paths), and the M7 model is the bolt circle alone (M7's other acceptance model is the M1 bracket). Every feature of every model regenerated `ok`.

**Leak per regen.** The heap probe of T0.2, as ported to `packages/kernel/src/heap-probe.ts` (`heapInUse`): run the work n times on a fresh kernel instance in a fresh Node process, then ask the allocator once, in 4 KiB blocks, how much it can still hand out before the memory must grow; bytes in use are the memory's size less that. 4 KiB blocks, because 64 KiB blocks read a leak of small pieces at only 0.70 to 0.85 of its size (T6.5a calibration); the probe is linear, so the difference between two n is the leak over the regens between them. Two kinds of regen:

- **full**: a new engine each time, disposed after, so every body is built and released (a document open, or the replay after a recycle);
- **edit**: one cold regen, then distinct edits on the same engine (`#thickness` of the bracket, `#fit_slip` of the jig, the house's south-wall window moved about 12" and back, each time by a further 1/64" so the cache never serves it), with a cache that keeps no spare entries, so replaced bodies are released at once and what stays is leaked, not cached. The engine's default cache keeps up to 64 spare entries alive; that is bounded and reused, not a leak.

Every probe ran on instance 1 (no recycle) with no shape left alive. The instance's own baseline is about 21 MiB in use.

**Growth and recycle cost.** The wasm memory's size (what the recycle policy compares with the threshold, `Kernel.heapBytes()`) after each of 700 full regens of the bracket and 200 of the house and the jig; and `KernelService.recycle()` followed by a cold replay of the model, three times each.

**planegcs headroom.** Through `packages/sketch` (our wrapper, with diagnosis on, as regen solves), each attempt in a freshly loaded planegcs instance: the T0.4 `chain` sketch (rounded units coupled by alignment constraints) fully constrained and free, unconnected rectangles closed by coincidents and kept square by horizontal and vertical constraints, and loose lines with no constraints, in steps of 5 entities (rectangles: 8 lines). Then 1,000 regen-style solves (a new system per solve, disposed after) of a 50-entity chain in one instance, followed by the largest chain that solved fresh. The sketches of every acceptance model were counted from the documents.

**Reproducing it.** `make bench-memory` (`apps/web/bench/`) runs the leak measurement for M1 to M6 (full regens, and edits where a model has one) at n = 2 and 12 and prints the table below; it fails when a model passes the switch condition of decision 1. It reproduced the numbers of the throwaway probes this note was written from to the KiB (the probe is deterministic). The growth curves, recycle timings and planegcs sweep were throwaway probes kept outside the repository, using the same functions.

## 1. Leak per regen

| Model          | Regen               | Leak per regen, n 2 to 12 | n 12 to 42 | Cold regen | Steady regen      |
| -------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------- | ---------- | ---------- | ----------------- |
| M1 bracket     | full                | 194 KiB                   | 219 KiB    | 140 ms     | 28 to 31 ms       |
| M1 bracket     | edit (`#thickness`) | 206 KiB                   | 215 KiB    |            | 31 ms             |
| M2 wall shelf  | full                | 136 KiB                   | 124 KiB    | 69 ms      | 15 to 16 ms       |
| M3 jig         | full                | 4,049 KiB                 | 4,915 KiB  | 2,281 ms   | 1,962 to 1,983 ms |
| M3 jig         | edit (`#fit_slip`)  | 3,526 KiB                 | 4,358 KiB  |            | 1,899 ms          |
| M4 bookshelf   | full                | 2,020 KiB                 | 2,004 KiB  | 470 ms     | 292 to 300 ms     |
| M5 sign        | full                | 1,998 KiB                 | 2,376 KiB  | 815 ms     | 594 to 599 ms     |
| M6 house       | full                | 3,651 KiB                 | 4,217 KiB  | 731 ms     | 534 to 550 ms     |
| M6 house       | edit (window moved) | 386 KiB                   | 391 KiB    |            | 68 to 73 ms       |
| M7 bolt circle | full                | 446 KiB                   | 368 KiB    | 182 ms     | 61 to 65 ms       |

"Cold" is the median, over the three processes, of the first regen in a fresh process; "steady" the median of the later regens in the n = 12 and n = 42 runs. The bracket's edit rebuilds all 5 features (every one reads `#thickness`), the jig's 11 (both threads among them), the house's 2 (the wall with the window and its layers).

What it says:

- **The M1 bracket leaks about 0.2 MiB per regen**, full or edit: below ADR 0001's switch condition of 1 MiB, and close to T0.3's scenario (110 to 143 KiB).
- **Later models leak more, in proportion to the B-rep work.** Threads (the jig: about 4 to 5 MiB per regen, whether full or after an edit that rebuilds them), many boards with joinery (the bookshelf: 2 MiB), a text cut with many faces (the sign: 2 to 2.4 MiB) and the house's layered walls (3.7 to 4.2 MiB per full regen). An edit that rebuilds little leaks little: moving one window of the house leaks 0.39 MiB.
- **The leak per regen grows a little with n** for the heavy models (the jig 4.0 then 4.9 MiB), as the allocator's free lists fragment; the per-regen figures from n = 2 to 12 are the lower ones.

## 2. Heap growth and what a recycle costs

The memory's size during repeated full regens, MiB, starting at 128 (Emscripten grows it by about 20 % per step, at most 96 MiB beyond what was asked, up to 4 GiB less 64 KiB):

| Regens | M1 bracket | M6 house | M3 jig |
| ------ | ---------- | -------- | ------ |
| 1      | 128.0      | 128.0    | 128.0  |
| 50     | 128.0      | 265.5    | 318.6  |
| 83     | 128.0      | 382.4    | 550.7  |
| 101    | 128.0      | 550.7    | 550.7  |
| 169    | 128.0      | 793.1    | 793.1  |
| 200    | 128.0      | 793.1    | 943.6  |
| 700    | 184.4      |          |        |

- **One regen never needs more than the initial memory.** Every model's cold regen on a fresh instance left the memory at 128 MiB; the in-use figure after two full regens is 21 to 37 MiB. The live working set of the largest model is far below any threshold under discussion.
- **The memory's size runs ahead of what is in use** by up to a growth step: the jig reached 550.7 MiB at regen 83 with about 400 MiB in use. So a threshold is crossed earlier than in-use estimates suggest.
- The earlier review's 128 to 831 MiB over 700 fine-mesh cycles was a stress loop of repeated fine tessellation, not a model regen; it is consistent with the slopes above.

A recycle (`KernelService.recycle()`: the old instance dropped, a new one made from the cached module) took 352 to 381 ms, and the replay is one cold regen: 43 to 45 ms for the bracket, 583 to 600 ms for the house, 2,036 to 2,052 ms for the jig. The engine also loses its feature cache, which the cold replay already accounts for.

## 3. The recycle threshold

Regens between recycles (the memory's size first past the threshold, from the curves above; the bracket extrapolated from its in-use slope), the overhead (recycle plus replay, over the regen time between recycles), and the memory a tab holds when it recycles:

| Threshold | M3 jig, full regens | M6 house, full regens | M1 bracket, regens (estimate) | Overhead, jig / house | Memory at recycle                           |
| --------- | ------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| 256 MiB   | 35                  | 50                    | about 1,100                   | 3.5 % / 3.6 %         | 265.5 MiB                                   |
| 512 MiB   | 83                  | 101                   | about 2,300                   | 1.5 % / 1.8 %         | 550.7 MiB                                   |
| 768 MiB   | 147                 | 169                   | about 3,500                   | 0.8 % / 1.1 %         | 793.1 MiB                                   |
| 1 GiB     | over 200            | over 200              | about 4,800                   | under 0.7 % / 0.9 %   | over 1 GiB (estimate: about 1.1 to 1.2 GiB) |

Considerations:

- **wasm32 and browsers.** The build may grow to 4 GiB less 64 KiB, but a tab holding a gigabyte of mostly leaked memory is a burden on an 8 GiB machine, and a grow that the browser refuses aborts the instance mid-operation (handled as a fatal recycle, but the operation's work is lost). Browser memory limits were not measured here.
- **Recycle cost** is small at every threshold from 512 MiB up: a recycle is at most 2.4 s for the slowest model, at an idle point.
- **Thrashing.** A threshold below one regen's needs would recycle after every batch, and a recycle between the batches of one regen restarts it (the engine retries twice, then fails the regen). Every model fits in 128 MiB, so 512 MiB leaves a margin of four times the largest model's memory.
- **Common edits leak less.** Most edits rebuild a few features: at 512 MiB the house recycles after about 1,000 to 1,300 window moves, the bracket after about 2,300 edits; the jig's thread edits are the expensive case at about 80 to 140.

**Decision: 512 MiB.** It halves the memory a session can hold (550 MiB against more than 1 GiB) for about one more percent of regen time on the heaviest models (1.5 to 1.8 % against under 1 %), and stays four times above the largest model's needs. `DEFAULT_HEAP_THRESHOLD` in `packages/kernel/src/service.ts` is now `512 * 1024 * 1024`. Revisit it when the memory bench's slowest model takes more than about 5 s to replay, or when a model's single regen needs more than about 128 MiB.

## 4. libcascade: custom build, fallback, or recycling

ADR 0001 decision 4 set two conditions for switching to the occt-wasm fallback: the custom libcascade build missing its targets by the end of M1, or the leak per regen on real M1 models exceeding 1 MiB.

- The leak condition does not hold: the M1 bracket leaks 0.19 to 0.21 MiB per regen.
- The custom build was never attempted (ADR 0002 notes there was no container engine when T0.2 ran), so the first condition holds only formally. Switching to occt-wasm because of a build nobody tried would trade a working kernel for a fork with face-only history; that was not the intent.
- What matters is whether recycling copes on the heaviest real models, and it does: 4 to 5 MiB per full regen means a recycle every 80 to 100 full regens at 512 MiB, at under 2 % of regen time.

**Decision: recycling is enough for now.** Neither the custom build nor the occt-wasm fallback is started; the custom build becomes a follow-up task (below), with this switch condition replacing the one in ADR 0001 decision 4:

> Start the custom libcascade build when any acceptance model leaks more than **8 MiB per full regen** in `make bench-memory` (at 512 MiB that is fewer than about 50 regens between recycles, about twice today's worst recycle overhead), or when the recycle overhead of any acceptance model passes 5 %. If the custom build, once attempted, misses ADR 0001's targets (no measurable leak, a fine-mesh copy of about 28 ms, init well under 482 ms in Node), fork occt-wasm's facade as decided there.

The bench enforces the leak half: it fails above 8 MiB per full regen. Today's maximum is about 4.9 MiB (the jig from n = 12 to 42). The custom build still has reasons beyond the leak (download size, mesh copy speed, and dropping `'unsafe-eval'` from the worker CSP), so it is worth doing before the condition forces it.

## 5. planegcs: the shipped binary's headroom

Largest sketch solved by `@salusoft89/planegcs` 1.2.0 (fixed 16 MiB heap) through `packages/sketch`, each attempt in a fresh instance:

| Sketch                                                    | Largest solved                             | First abort (`Aborted(OOM)`) | Solve time at the largest |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| `chain`, fully constrained (DOF 0)                        | 115 entities, 321 constraints              | 120 entities                 | 57 ms                     |
| `chain`, free (coincident, horizontal, tangent only)      | 130 entities, 260 constraints              | 135 entities                 | 29 ms                     |
| Unconnected rectangles (coincident, horizontal, vertical) | 200 lines (50 rectangles), 400 constraints | 208 lines                    | 12 ms                     |
| Loose lines, no constraints                               | 2,000 (the sweep's end)                    | none                         | 3 ms                      |

- Our wrapper reaches a little less than the T0.4 spike's 125 fully constrained entities (its diagnosis costs memory), so the abort message in `packages/sketch/src/planegcs/module.ts` ("about 125 connected entities") is slightly optimistic.
- 1,000 regen-style solves of a 50-entity sketch in one instance did not erode the headroom: the 115-entity chain still solved afterwards.

The sketches the acceptance models actually make:

| Model        | Sketches | Largest                                                                        |
| ------------ | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| M1 bracket   | 2        | the L-profile: 6 lines, 17 constraints                                         |
| M2 shelf     | 5        | 4 lines, 12 constraints each                                                   |
| M3 jig       | 8        | the thumbscrew lobes: 12 entities, 18 constraints                              |
| M4 bookshelf | 9        | the face frame: 4 lines, 16 constraints                                        |
| M5 sign      | 4        | the border groove: 16 lines and arcs, no constraints                           |
| M6 house     | 0        | walls, floors and roofs come from the construction domain's data, not sketches |
| M7           | 0        | scripts pass their profiles to the kernel directly                             |

Text and SVG artwork enter a sketch as one `outline` entity whose anchor is all the solver sees, so lettering does not count against the limit. The one place the limit already shapes the product is SVG import as editable sketch geometry, which the sketcher refuses past `MAX_SOLVER_LOAD` (`apps/web/src/sketcher/svg-import.ts`: about 110 free arcs), offering an outline instead.

**Decision: not needed now.** The largest real sketch (16 entities) is about a seventh of the limit, and neither M4 nor M6 produced the large sketches ADR 0003 expected: the construction domain works from its own plan data. Our own build becomes a follow-up task, needed before any feature makes coupled sketches of about 100 entities, or when the SVG geometry cap should be lifted.

## 6. Follow-ups

### A custom libcascade build

What it is: ADR 0001 decision 4 and ADR 0002 decision 3, unchanged in substance.

- Build libcascade 3.0.2 (OCCT 8.0.1) with `@libcascade/toolchain` in a pinned container in CI, the Emscripten version and flags recorded: a trimmed symbol list (what `packages/kernel` binds today, enumerated from its use), destructors that free (re-run T0.2's destructor audit: no empty destructor among the bound classes we use), C++ helpers for the operations with history collection, the `topology()` query and mesh extraction into packed buffers, shapes kept in a C++ arena. Try `-sDYNAMIC_EXECUTION=0`, so the worker CSP can drop `'unsafe-eval'` (it is there for the kernel glue's embind invokers, `deploy/Caddyfile`).
- Keep it a separately loaded, replaceable `.wasm` plus glue, with the recipe and helper sources in this repository (ADR 0006).
- Acceptance: `make bench-memory` shows no measurable leak (under about 64 KiB per full regen of every model, the probe's resolution at n = 2 and 12) and the bench's table recorded in the task; fine-mesh copy about 28 ms; init well under 482 ms in Node; the brotli size measured against T0.2's 1 to 4 MB estimate; every kernel golden, regen and e2e test passing unchanged.
- If it misses those targets: fork occt-wasm's facade and add edge and vertex history (ADR 0001).
- When it becomes necessary: when `make bench-memory` fails (a model past 8 MiB per full regen) or a model's recycle overhead passes 5 %. Before that it is an improvement (size, speed, CSP), best scheduled before long-running headless sessions (the agent surface plan's 1,000-batch runs), where recycles add up.

### Our own planegcs build

What it is: ADR 0003 decision 8.

- Build planegcs from pinned upstream sources (the `@salusoft89/planegcs` binding template and FreeCAD's PlaneGCS at a pinned commit) with a pinned Emscripten (upstream's 3.1.45 settings break on Emscripten 4, upstream [issue #8](https://github.com/Salusoft89/planegcs/issues/8)), `-sALLOW_MEMORY_GROWTH=1` with a `MAXIMUM_MEMORY` cap (for example 256 MiB, so a runaway sketch still aborts cleanly), `-sDYNAMIC_EXECUTION=0`; export conflict groups, and expose `initSolution` separately so a drag does not re-partition the system on every move. Separate `.wasm`, recipe in the repository (ADR 0006, LGPL).
- Acceptance: re-run T0.4's benchmark and the headroom sweep of section 5: a fully constrained coupled chain of at least 500 entities solves; drag and dimension latency at 50 entities no worse than the published build; `packages/sketch` tests pass, with the out-of-memory test driven by the new cap; then raise `MAX_SOLVER_LOAD` in the SVG import and correct the abort message's entity count.
- When it becomes necessary: before any planned feature that makes coupled sketches of about 100 entities (importing DXF or SVG as constrained geometry, sketch patterns, generated profiles), when lifting the SVG geometry cap is wanted, when solver aborts show up in use, or when the published package stops working with our toolchain.
