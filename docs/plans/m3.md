# M3 plan: 3D printing: printability checks, print features, 3MF and slicer hand-off

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-09-26
- Epic: "Can't CAD Can We?", milestone M3
- Builds on: M1 as landed (`packages/kernel`, `packages/core`, `packages/regen`, `packages/io`, `packages/sketch`, `packages/units`, `apps/web`), the [M2 plan](m2.md) (multi-body parts), [product decisions](../decisions/0000-product-decisions.md) (first domain 3D printing; Bambu Lab printers sliced with OrcaSlicer), ADRs [0004](../adr/0004-document-format.md), [0006](../adr/0006-licensing.md), [0007](../adr/0007-worker-protocol.md), and the research note [Slicer hand-off and 3MF interop](../research/slicer-handoff.md)

M3 makes manufakture good at the domain it chose first: parts for an FDM printer. It checks a part for what will print badly (overhangs, thin walls, features too small for the nozzle, a part that does not fit the bed), adds the features printed parts need (embossed and debossed text, threads, clearances for press, slip and sliding fits), and gets a multi-part, multi-colour model into OrcaSlicer or Bambu Studio intact. It is accepted when a real small printed part, a PTFE tube cutting jig, is remodelled end to end and printed on a Bambu Lab printer; the print itself is a human-only task.

This page breaks M3 into agent-sized tasks, each roughly one pull request, grounded in the code as it stands after M1 and in the M2 plan. The epic's items are split as follows:

| Epic item                                                           | Tasks          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| T3.1 Printability: overhangs, thin walls, min feature size, bed fit | T3.1a to T3.1d |
| T3.2 Print features: text emboss/deboss, threads, fit presets       | T3.2a to T3.2h |
| T3.3 3MF with several parts and colours; "open in slicer" hand-off  | T3.3a to T3.3c |
| Decisions and spikes behind them                                    | T3.0a to T3.0d |
| Acceptance: remodel and print a real part (the print is human-only) | T3.4a, T3.4b   |

Estimates are marked as estimates. Claims about the slicers come from the research note, which read their sources at their latest releases; what it marks unverified stays unverified here.

## Where M1 (and M2) leave us

What M3 builds on, from the code:

- **Most geometry facts are already exact and cheap.** `packages/kernel` `topology` (`src/types.ts` `FaceInfo`) gives every face its surface type, area, centroid, the outward normal of a plane, and for a cylinder its radius and the direction of its axis, and regen sends a part's topology with its mesh (`PartResult.topology`, regen README "Result"). Planar faces for "lay flat on this face" and the radius of every hole and boss need no new kernel call. Two cylinder facts are missing: `FaceInfo` has no point on the axis, so two coaxial faces cannot be told from two parallel ones, and nothing says whether a cylinder is a hole or a pin; `src/topology.ts` takes the axis from `cyl.Axis()` without the sign correction it applies to plane normals for reversed faces, so the direction's sign carries no meaning either. T3.1c adds both facts in a small kernel step. `Topology` holds no names; a face's name is in the mesh (`MeshData.faceNames`, an index into the reply's name table). `measure` (`src/measure.ts`) adds exact distances with witness points (`BRepExtrema_DistShapeShape`) when a check needs one.
- **Meshes carry per-face normals and face ids.** `MeshData` has outward `normals` per vertex (a `Float32Array`), `faceRanges`, `triangleFaces` and face names (kernel README, "Meshes"), so overhang shading can colour whole B-rep faces or single triangles in the viewport without a round trip. The viewport already has custom shader materials (`apps/web/src/viewport/materials.ts`).
- **Export is watertight and tolerance-controlled.** `packages/io` welds and checks every mesh (`exportMesh`, `checkManifold`), writes 3MF core with one named object per body and no transforms, colours or components, and `parse3mf` refuses components (io README, "3MF"). The optional `interop` CI job slices our 3MF with PrusaSlicer.
- **Profiles are lines, arcs and circles, one region per feature.** Kernel `profile` loops take `line`, `arc` and `circle` entities; regen builds one region per feature, and "a profile that selects several separate regions fails with `unsupported`" (regen README, "Translation"). Text needs many regions with holes, and curved glyph outlines. OCCT's `Geom_BezierCurve`, `Geom2d_BezierCurve` and `Geom_BSplineCurve` are bound in libcascade 3.0.2 (checked in its `types.d.ts`), so curved profile edges are possible.
- **Sweeps exist in the binding, not in our wrapper.** `BRepOffsetAPI_MakePipeShell` (with `SetMode` for an auxiliary spine or a fixed `gp_Ax2`), `BRepOffsetAPI_ThruSections` and `BRepBuilderAPI_MakeEdge(Geom2d_Curve, Geom_Surface)` (an edge on a cylinder, the classic way to build a helix) are all in libcascade's typings (checked). The wrapper has no sweep, loft or helix yet (kernel README, "Operations").
- **Hole standards exist.** `src/holes.ts` has clearance holes M3 to M12 and #6 to 1/2" in close, normal and loose fits (ISO 273, ASME B18.2.8) with counterbore and countersink sizes; core's hole feature records `standard { size, fit }`. There is no thread table.
- **Materials are a permanent-id table.** `packages/core/src/materials.ts` has PLA, PETG, ABS, woods and metals with cited densities; ids never change, and the table can grow without a format change.
- **The `extension` feature kind is reserved for domain features** (`print.brim` is the schema's own example), but regen treats extension features as geometry-free (`ok` with an `extension` warning). Text and threads change geometry and need core validation and kernel support, so they are core kinds, not extensions.
- **M2 brings bodies.** After T2.1a to T2.1d a part holds several named bodies with a colour and a material each (`Part.bodies`, `BodyProps { id, name?, color?, material? }`), regen reports a mesh per body, and export writes one 3MF object per body (M2 plan, T2.1d). Configurations (T2.4a, T2.4b) give "export all" per row. Assembly export (T2.3f) adds 3MF build items with transforms and leaves "colours, materials and plates in 3MF" to M3.

## Decisions that cut across tasks

1. **Print setups are document state, not features.** A document gets a top-level `print` section holding print setups (a printer, a nozzle, thresholds, and the bodies to print with their orientation), the way M5 adds a `cam` section with setups ([M5 plan](m5.md), decisions 4 to 6). A print setup changes no geometry, so it is not in the feature tree and never dirties a regen. T3.0b records this in an ADR.
2. **Print references never block modelling.** A setup names bodies (`{ part, body }`, M2 decision 1) and orientation faces (`FaceReference`). As in M5 decision 6, deleting or changing what they name is allowed; the print panel then reports `reference-lost` and offers a re-pick. Validation checks only that ids are well formed.
3. **The printer table is data with permanent ids**, like `materials.ts`: build area polygon, height, excluded areas, per-nozzle areas for two-nozzle printers, nozzle sizes and default line width, each row citing its source. The Bambu Lab rows come from OrcaSlicer 2.4.2's printer profiles ([research note, section 4](../research/slicer-handoff.md#4-bambu-lab-build-volumes)); the numbers are copied as facts with the source cited, never the AGPL profile files.
4. **Analysis runs on meshes, facts come from the B-rep.** Overhang angles, wall thickness and gaps are measured on the per-body meshes at the export tolerance (0.02 mm chordal at `normal`, io README), which is two orders of magnitude finer than what an FDM nozzle resolves. Hole radii and axes, planar normals and bed contact come from `topology`, exactly (the axis origin and the hole-or-pin flag once T3.1c has added them). Nothing about the analysis is stored in the document; only thresholds and orientation are.
5. **Overhang angles in one convention.** The UI states angles as **degrees from vertical** (the "45 degree rule" convention). OrcaSlicer's `support_threshold_angle` is a slope **from horizontal** (default 30, research note section 4); the default shown to the user is converted (60 degrees from vertical) and the conversion is documented where the setting appears.
6. **Text is sketch geometry that stays editable.** A text entity in a sketch stores its string, font, size and placement; regen turns it into closed region loops at every regen, so changing the string or a size variable changes the part. Its glyph edges have positional ids, so faces built on them are fragile, like imported faces (kernel README, "import"), and a reference to one resolves with a warning.
7. **One outline module for text and SVG.** Glyph outlines and SVG paths are both lines plus quadratic and cubic Beziers. T3.2b writes the converter from such paths to sketch region loops once, in `packages/sketch`; M5's T5.8 (SVG import) reuses it. The text sketch entity (T3.2c) is designed as a general `outline` entity with a `source`, so T5.8 can add an SVG source to the same kind rather than a second one. **Text is M3's**: T5.8 drops its "optionally text" item and keeps SVG import, and T5.8's task description should say that text moved to M3.
8. **Fonts come with a license decision.** Bundling a font needs an amendment to ADR 0006, whose allowlist does not name the SIL Open Font License. T3.0c writes it: OFL 1.1 fonts may ship, unmodified or without a Reserved Font Name (the OFL FAQ counts subsetting as modification), with their license text in the shipped notices. User-supplied fonts are stored in the document like imported files (bytes, size, SHA-256) and their embedding permissions are shown.
9. **Threads are a feature on a cylindrical face.** The picked face supplies axis, side (internal or external) and extent; the thread standard and the fit define the geometry, not the cylinder's exact radius. `modelled` threads cut and add real helical geometry; `cosmetic` threads only size the hole or shaft (for tapping, self-tapping screws or heat-set inserts) and are drawn as helix lines.
10. **Fits are variables.** Press, slip and sliding clearances are ordinary document variables (`#fit_press`, `#fit_slip`, `#fit_sliding`) inserted with defaults from the printer table, so every dimension can use them in expressions and a printed calibration coupon can replace them. No new mechanism in `packages/units`.
11. **Hand-off is a download** in M3 ([research note, section 5](../research/slicer-handoff.md#5-recommendation)). A web page cannot give a slicer a URL it can fetch without a server, so there is no custom-scheme launch until M7 can host a short-lived https URL, and then only on explicit opt-in.
12. **3MF stays standard.** Core plus the materials extension's colour groups (prefix `m`, one group per colour, `pid` and `pindex="0"` on each object) and build-item transforms, never an `Application` value that impersonates Bambu Studio or OrcaSlicer. Components and Bambu's `Metadata/model_settings.config` are added only if the spike's load matrix (T3.0a's command-line results, checked in the slicers' GUIs by T3.0d) shows they help.
13. **One format bump per schema task, landed one at a time.** T3.1a (print section), T3.2c (outline entity and fonts) and T3.2f (thread feature) each take the next free `FORMAT_VERSION` when they land, with a migration and a fixture (core README, "To change the file shape"). They serialise with each other and with every other milestone's schema tasks: M2's T2.1a, T2.4a, T2.2a and T2.3b (planned as v4 to v7), M5's T5.1b, and any schema task in the M4 and M7 plans. If M2's four land first, M3's three take the next free numbers from v8 on, interleaved with M5's and any others; nothing in M3 assumes a number.

The print section, as a first cut for T3.1a:

```ts
interface ManufaktureDocument {
  // ...as after M2
  print: PrintData; // added by T3.1a's format version; the migration adds an empty one
}

interface PrintData {
  setups: PrintSetup[];
  nextIds: Record<string, number>; // 'print' (setups), 'item', 'r'; only ever increase
}

interface PrintSetup {
  id: string; // 'print#1'
  name: string;
  printer: string; // a printer table id: 'bambu-a1-mini', 'bambu-x1c', ...
  nozzle: number; // mm, one of the printer's nozzle sizes
  thresholds?: {
    overhang?: StoredExpression; // angle from vertical
    minWall?: StoredExpression;
    minGap?: StoredExpression;
    minHole?: StoredExpression;
    teardrop?: StoredExpression; // horizontal holes above this diameter are flagged
  }; // absent: defaults from printer and nozzle
  items: PrintItem[];
}

interface PrintItem {
  id: string; // 'item#1'
  part: string; // part id
  body?: string; // body id (M2); absent: every body of the part
  orientation:
    | { kind: 'asModelled' }
    | { kind: 'layFlat'; face: FaceReference; turn?: StoredExpression } // face down on the bed, then a turn about z
    | { kind: 'rotate'; x: StoredExpression; y: StoredExpression; z: StoredExpression };
  copies?: number;
}
```

## Tasks

Every task follows the repository's rules: tests with the code, `make lint`, `make typecheck` and `make test` green, the e2e specs it names passing, kernel goldens in the `kernel-goldens` Vitest project where the kernel is involved, the package README updated where its API changes, user docs in `docs/user/` for anything a user sees, and a license check from the installed package for every new runtime dependency (ADR 0006 decision 6) with a line in the task report.

### T3.0a: Spike: 3MF fixtures and OrcaSlicer command-line matrix

**Goal.** Replace every "unverified" in the research note that M3 depends on and that a program can check with an observation, and prepare the files and checklist for the checks only a person can make (T3.0d).

**Scope.**

- 3MF fixtures under `packages/io/src/fixtures/slicers/`, each a small two-body model (two boxes, red and blue): core only (today's output); plus colour groups as in decision 12; plus one component object holding both bodies; plus `pindex` and per-triangle colours; plus a minimal `Metadata/model_settings.config` with per-object `extruder`; plus build-item transforms (a rotated and a translated body).
- A script that builds the fixtures from source (`packages/io/scripts/` or a test helper, decided in the task), so they can be regenerated and rerun per slicer release rather than edited by hand; the fixtures are committed with it.
- OrcaSlicer's command line (`--export-3mf`, `--slice 0`, research note section 5) at 2.4.2: load each fixture, write it back as a project 3MF, and record from its `Metadata/model_settings.config` the objects, parts, names and filament slot per object or part, plus whether slicing succeeds (the automatable part of the matrix, for T3.3c). Record what the command line needs to start headless (a display, profiles on first run). PrusaSlicer's command line, which the `interop` job already runs, slices each fixture too.
- Write-up: a new "Verified" section of `docs/research/slicer-handoff.md` (dated, with slicer versions and operating system) holding the command-line matrix, a provisional recommendation on components and `model_settings.config` from that evidence, and an empty GUI matrix with a step-by-step checklist for T3.0d.

**Acceptance.** The fixtures build from the script and are committed; the command-line matrix is filled for every fixture; each unverified claim in the note that the command line can settle is marked confirmed or refuted; the provisional recommendation for components and `model_settings.config` is stated with the evidence; the T3.0d checklist names every observation to report and where it goes.

**Human-only: no.** If OrcaSlicer's command line cannot run in the agent's environment, the task says so, leaves those cells for T3.0d, and still delivers the fixtures, the script and the checklist.

**Dependencies.** None. M1: `packages/io`.

**Footprint.** `packages/io/src/fixtures/slicers/*.3mf`, the script that builds them, `docs/research/slicer-handoff.md`.

**Risks.** Slicer behaviour changes between releases; the fixtures and their script stay in the repository so the matrix can be rerun per release. The command line may load a file differently from the GUI (it skips dialogs); T3.0d compares the two on the same fixtures.

### T3.0b: ADR: 3D printing architecture

**Goal.** Fix the boundaries before code: where print state lives, what runs where, and which new kinds the document gets.

**Scope.** One ADR under `docs/adr/`, the next free number when it is written, plus its index line. ADRs 0009 (sync) and 0010 (scripting sandbox) are both M7's, so M3's ADRs take the next free numbers from 0011 on. Decides: the `print` section and its setups (decision 1) and why not features or `extension`; references that never block modelling (decision 2); the printer table as data (decision 3); `packages/print` (pure TypeScript, no kernel dependency) and where analysis runs: overhang classification on the main thread per frame is cheap, thickness and gap ray casting in a lazily started print-analysis worker, a context ADR 0007 does not have yet (M5 adds a CAM worker the same way; the ADR says whether the two share one); the outline entity and fonts in the document (decisions 6 to 8); the thread feature (decision 9); fits as variables (decision 10); hand-off by download and the M7 option (decision 11); 3MF conventions (decision 12).

**Acceptance.** The ADR is accepted and indexed; T3.1a, T3.2c and T3.2f cite it.

**Dependencies.** None; it cites T3.0a's and T3.0d's results if they exist and leaves the 3MF details to T3.3a otherwise.

**Footprint.** `docs/adr/NNNN-3d-printing.md`, `docs/adr/README.md`.

**Risks.** None beyond numbering collisions with parallel plans; take the number at write time.

### T3.0c: ADR: fonts, amending ADR 0006

**Goal.** Allow a bundled font and set the rules for user fonts.

**Scope.** A new ADR (the next free number, from 0011 on as for T3.0b) that amends ADR 0006 decision 2 in part (the ADR README asks for a new record when a decision changes): the SIL Open Font License 1.1 is allowed for font files shipped with the app, loaded as data, never compiled into code. The FSF lists OFL as "a free copyleft license for fonts" ([FSF license list](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/license-list.html)), and OFL FAQ 1.3 says only the font needs to stay under OFL, bundling with software under other licenses being intended ([OFL FAQ](https://openfontlicense.org/ofl-faq/)). Rules: ship the font file unmodified, or pick a font with no Reserved Font Name, since FAQ 2.6 counts subsetting as modification; ship its license text in the notices (ADR 0006 decision 5); geometry made from a font in a user's part is the user's work (FAQ 1.13: embedding a font in a document "does not change the license of the document itself"). Choose the bundled font: one static sans-serif (no variable axes, overlaps removed) that prints well at small sizes, license read from its own files. User fonts: stored in the document, their OS/2 `fsType` embedding bits read and shown when restrictive. Coordinate with M5: T5.0c or T5.5a amends the same decision for LGPL-3.0 (OpenCAMLib); whichever ADR lands second extends the first rather than contradicting it.

**Acceptance.** The ADR is accepted and indexed; ADR 0006's inventory gains `opentype.js` (2.0.0, MIT, npm metadata read on 2026-09-26; re-read from the installed package when adopted) and the chosen font.

**Dependencies.** None.

**Footprint.** `docs/adr/NNNN-fonts.md`, `docs/adr/README.md`, the font file and its license under `packages/text/fonts/` when T3.2b adds it.

**Risks.** Some good print fonts carry a Reserved Font Name; shipping them unmodified is fine, but a later subsetting optimisation would then be a rename.

### T3.0d: Slicer GUI load checks and browser hand-off checks (human-only)

**Goal.** Settle the claims only a person at a desktop can check: what the three slicers' GUIs make of our fixtures, and how browsers and the operating system hand a `.3mf` download to a slicer.

**Human-only: yes.** It needs someone at a desktop with OrcaSlicer 2.4.2, Bambu Studio 2.8.2 and PrusaSlicer 2.9.6 installed, Chrome and Firefox, and ideally Windows, macOS and Linux. The person follows T3.0a's checklist; an agent writes up what they report.

**Scope.**

- Load each T3.0a fixture in the three slicers' GUIs and record: objects and parts in the object list, names, positions, filament slot per object or part, whether slot colours change, warnings or repair prompts, and whether slicing succeeds. Note where the GUI differs from T3.0a's command-line result for the same file.
- Hand-off: Chrome's and Firefox's behaviour on a `.3mf` download (is "Always open files of this type" offered), OrcaSlicer's `.3mf` association on Windows, and one `orcaslicer://open?file=` round trip with a file on a public static https host, to confirm section 1 of the note for M7.
- Results into the GUI matrix of the note's "Verified" section (dated, with slicer versions, operating systems and browser versions).

**Acceptance.** The GUI matrix is filled for every fixture and slicer on at least one operating system; each remaining unverified claim in the note that M3 relies on is marked confirmed or refuted; T3.0a's provisional recommendation on components and `model_settings.config` is confirmed or revised with the evidence, and a revision is filed as a follow-up to T3.3a if T3.3a has already landed.

**Dependencies.** T3.0a (fixtures and checklist).

**Footprint.** `docs/research/slicer-handoff.md`; screenshots under `docs/research/slicer-handoff/` if they help.

**Risks.** One person rarely has all three operating systems; record which were checked, and leave the rest marked unverified rather than inferred.

### T3.1a: Core: print setups (format bump)

**Goal.** The document can hold print setups: which bodies to print, on which printer and nozzle, oriented how, with which thresholds.

**Scope.**

- `packages/core/src/schema.ts`: `PrintData`, `PrintSetup`, `PrintItem` as in the first cut above; the printer id is a string checked against the printer table by T3.1b at use, not by the schema (so the table can grow and a document naming an unknown printer still loads); `FORMAT_VERSION` next free; migration adds `print: { setups: [], nextIds: {} }`; fixture of the previous version and its `FIXTURES` registration.
- `src/commands.ts`: `addPrintSetup`, `editPrintSetup`, `deletePrintSetup`, `addPrintItem`, `editPrintItem`, `deletePrintItem`, with inverses; ids from `print.nextIds`, never reused.
- `src/features.ts` and `variables`: `variableUses`, `renameVariable` and `inlineVariable` cover print expressions.
- `src/changes.ts`: `diffDocuments` reports `printChanged` separately, so regen does not treat a print edit as a part edit.
- Validation: item part ids exist; body ids well formed (M2's `bodyCreator`); references well formed. Not validated: that a body or face still exists (decision 2).
- README: the print section, its rules and why it is not a feature.

### T3.1b: `packages/print`: printers, orientation, overhangs, bed fit

**Goal.** The pure, tested functions the print workspace needs first: a printer table, the transform an orientation stands for, overhang classes per triangle and per face, and whether a set of bodies fits a printer.

**Scope.**

- New package `packages/print` (GPL-3.0-or-later, no kernel or DOM dependency).
- `printers.ts`: Bambu Lab A1 mini, A1, P1P, P1S, P2S, X1, X1 Carbon, X1E, H2S, H2D, H2D Pro and X2D from the research note's section 4 (build area polygon, height, excluded area, per-nozzle areas and heights), nozzle sizes each model is sold with (read from Bambu Lab's product pages in the task, cited per row), default line width 0.42 mm for a 0.4 mm nozzle and the minimum feature size of 25% of the nozzle (OrcaSlicer defaults, research note). A generic "custom" printer with user-entered size is out of scope unless trivial.
- `orientation.ts`: the rigid transform for `asModelled`, `layFlat` (the planar face's outward normal to -z, then the turn about z, then the lowest point to z = 0) and `rotate` (x, y, z Euler angles, fixed order, documented).
- `overhang.ts`: per triangle, its angle from vertical against the build direction, classified as ok, steep (within a warning band below the threshold), overhang, or downward-flat; triangles in the plane of the bed (within a tolerance of the lowest z) are `onBed`, never overhang. Per B-rep face, the worst class and the area in each class. Default threshold 60 degrees from vertical (OrcaSlicer's 30 from horizontal, decision 5), warning band 10 degrees (an estimate).
- Overhang means strictly steeper than the threshold: a triangle exactly at the threshold is steep, not overhang, matching OrcaSlicer, which supports surfaces whose slope from horizontal is below `support_threshold_angle`. "Exactly" allows for floating point: an angle within about 1e-6 rad of the threshold (the value is fixed and documented in the task) counts as at the threshold, so a face built at 60 degrees whose normal round-trips through the mesh slightly off is still steep. The tolerance has to cover the mesh's precision: `MeshData.normals` is a `Float32Array`, and rounding a unit normal to 32 bits moves its angle by up to about 1e-7 rad, so a 1e-9 rad tolerance would misclassify such a face.
- `bedFit.ts`: oriented, placed bodies against the printer: bounding box inside the area polygon minus exclusions and under the height; for two-nozzle printers, the caller passes which nozzles each body uses, and a body that uses both must fit the overlap of their areas (`packages/print` does not guess; T3.1d and T3.3b derive it from the bodies' colours and filament slots). Reports the overshoot per axis.
- Tests with hand-built meshes and hand-computed answers. Test meshes store their normals in a `Float32Array`, as `MeshData` does, never in 64-bit arrays, so the tolerance is exercised at the precision real meshes have.

**Acceptance.** Unit tests: a cube is all ok on its base, all onBed at the bottom; a 45 degree chamfer below a ledge is exactly 45 degrees; a face at exactly 60 degrees from vertical, its normals rounded to 32 bits (compared with the 1e-6 rad tolerance), is steep and one at 61 degrees is overhang; a downward-facing ledge is downward-flat; lay-flat on a slanted face puts that face at z = 0 facing down; a 180 mm cube fits the A1 mini and a 181 mm one does not, on x; an object inside the X1's excluded corner fails with the exclusion named; on the H2D, a body passed as using both nozzles is checked against the overlap and the same body passed as using one nozzle against that nozzle's area.

**Dependencies.** None. M1 types only (`MeshData` shape from `packages/kernel`, imported as a type).

**Footprint.** `packages/print/` (new), `pnpm-workspace.yaml` if needed, `vitest.config.ts` project entry, README.

**Risks.** Printer data drifts with slicer profile updates; each row cites a profile version so a later update is a deliberate change. Excluded areas differ by plate type on some printers (unverified); take the profile's default.

### T3.1c: Wall thickness, gaps and small features

**Goal.** Find walls too thin to print well, gaps that will close up, and holes and details below what the nozzle resolves, and say where they are.

**Scope.**

- `packages/print/src/thickness.ts`: for sample points on each triangle (centroids, plus more on large triangles), cast a ray inward along -normal against the same body's mesh and record the distance to the first opposite-facing hit: that is the local wall thickness. Outward along +normal the same gives the gap to the next face of the same body (or another body of the same setup). A bounding volume hierarchy makes it fast; use `three-mesh-bvh` (MIT, 0.9.15 published 2026-09-09, peer `three >= 0.159`, npm metadata read on 2026-09-26; re-read from the installed package) or a small own BVH if it pulls in too much for a worker, decided in the task.
- Classes per sample: thinner than the minimum feature (not printed at all: 25% of the nozzle, OrcaSlicer's `min_feature_size`), thinner than the minimum wall (default two line widths, 0.84 mm at a 0.4 mm nozzle: an estimate of "at least two perimeters", documented as such), gap narrower than the minimum gap (default 0.2 mm, an estimate, which the fit presets later inform). Per face: minimum thickness and the area below each threshold.
- Kernel step first: `FaceInfo` gives a cylinder only its radius and an axis direction, which is not enough to group faces or tell a hole from a pin. T3.1c adds two fields for cylinders to `FaceInfo` (`packages/kernel/src/types.ts`), both `null` for other surfaces, computed in `src/topology.ts`: `axisOrigin`, a point on the axis (the location of `cyl.Axis()`), and `hole`, true when the face's outward normal points toward the axis (material outside the cylinder, a hole) and false when it points away (a pin or boss). The flag is sign-corrected the way `topology.ts` already corrects plane normals: the surface normal is flipped for a reversed face and for a left-handed frame (`Direct()` false), then compared with the radial direction from the axis at a point on the face. The axis direction stays as OCCT gives it; the kernel README's topology entry says its sign carries no meaning and that `hole` is the field to read for the side. A kernel test covers a hole and a boss in one block, a mirrored copy of each, and a face OCCT stores reversed.
- Small holes and pins from `topology`: every cylindrical face with its radius, axis and side (exact, from the B-rep). Cylindrical faces that share an axis and a radius, within a tolerance, and face the same way (all `hole`, or all pins) are **one hole or pin**: a slot or cross hole that cuts a bore splits its cylinder into several faces, and the checks below report the group once, listing its faces. Axes are compared as lines, not as vectors: two directions match when the angle between the lines, ignoring sign (from `|dot|`, computed as `atan2(|a x b|, |a . b|)` so it stays accurate near zero), is at most 1e-9 rad, and two axes coincide when the distance from one face's `axisOrigin` to the other's axis line (point-to-line distance, `|(o2 - o1) x d1|` for a unit `d1`) is at most 1e-6 mm. The radius tolerance is 1e-6 mm (defaults chosen in the task and documented). Grouping looks only at the axis, the radius and the side, so two separate bores with the same axis and radius (two holes in line on either side of a gap, say) are grouped as **one** hole and reported once; the README says so, since the grouping cannot tell them apart without adjacency and the checks give the same answer for both. A hole whose diameter is below the minimum hole diameter (default two nozzle diameters, 0.8 mm at a 0.4 mm nozzle, an estimate) is flagged, and a hole exactly at the minimum is not. "Exactly" allows for floating point: a diameter within 1e-6 mm of the minimum counts as at the minimum, so a 0.8 mm hole whose radius round-trips through the kernel as 0.39999999 is not flagged.
- Horizontal holes: a hole whose axis is parallel to the bed (within 1 degree) and whose diameter is strictly above the teardrop size (default 3 mm, an estimate: below it the top of the hole bridges acceptably on a well-tuned FDM printer; editable per setup like every threshold) is flagged as needing a teardrop or support, once per hole, with the list of its faces. The comparison uses the same 1e-6 mm tolerance as the minimum hole: a diameter within 1e-6 mm of the teardrop size counts as at it and is not flagged. Its top is an overhang anyway, which T3.1b shows; the flag says what to do about it.
- Threaded holes are not holes. A **modelled** internal thread (T3.2f) cut as a helical groove from a hole at the minor diameter can leave cylindrical crest strips of the original hole (about 4.13 mm across for M5), which would otherwise read as a horizontal hole above the teardrop size. Those strips are kept or modified faces of the hole, so they inherit the hole's name (kernel README, "Naming"); they carry no thread name. T3.1c therefore works by axis: a face whose name contains the `:thread:` segment of T3.2e's naming rule (`<id>:thread:crest`, `root`, flanks and ends, also under a pattern or mirror prefix) is never a hole itself, and any cylinder group whose axis coincides, as a line within the tolerances above, with the axis of a cylindrical `:thread:` face is left out of the hole checks. So a modelled threaded hole is neither a small hole nor a teardrop candidate; its overhang still shows through T3.1b. Names are not in `Topology`: the analysis takes each face's name from the mesh (`MeshData.faceNames`, an index into the reply's name table) next to the topology. The names are all it needs, so this does not wait for T3.2f. A **cosmetic** thread adds no thread faces and keeps a cylinder at the tap drill diameter, which is found like any hole.
- The print-analysis worker (T3.0b): a Comlink worker entry in `packages/print` that takes the setup's oriented meshes (copied from the main thread; regen keeps none) and returns issue lists and per-triangle values as transferred arrays, cancellable per ADR 0007's generation rule.
- Performance target: a 200,000-triangle body analysed in under a second in the worker (an estimate to measure, not a promise); a budget test in Node.

**Acceptance.** Unit tests: a 1 mm wall measures 1.000 mm within the mesh tolerance; a 0.3 mm fin is below the minimum wall; a 0.1 mm slot between two blocks is a narrow gap; at a 0.4 mm nozzle a 0.6 mm hole is flagged, and a 0.8 mm hole (exactly the minimum), a hole of 0.8 mm minus 1e-7 mm (inside the 1e-6 mm tolerance) and a 3 mm one are not; a horizontal 3.2 mm hole gets the teardrop flag, and a horizontal 3 mm hole (exactly the teardrop size), a horizontal hole of 3 mm plus 1e-7 mm (inside the 1e-6 mm tolerance), a vertical 5 mm hole and a hole tilted 5 degrees from horizontal do not; a horizontal 4.2 mm hole split into several cylindrical faces by a slot crossing it gives one hole and one teardrop flag listing all its faces, including when their stored axis directions have opposite signs; two faces of the same radius on parallel axes 1 mm apart are two holes; a hole and a pin of the same axis and radius are not grouped; two separate bores in line with the same radius are one hole (documented behaviour). Thread rule, on a hand-built fixture of a `Topology` plus `MeshData.faceNames` slots and a name table (the topology alone has no names): a horizontal 4.13 mm hole group of two faces named after a `hole#3` feature, coaxial with a cylindrical face named `thread#5:thread:root`, is skipped (no hole, no flag); a different horizontal 4.13 mm hole group, coaxial with nothing named `:thread:` (in the same fixture, on a parallel axis 10 mm away), is reported as a hole with a teardrop flag; the same rule holds with the thread face under a pattern prefix (`pattern#6:i2/thread#5:thread:root`). Kernel tests (in the kernel's suite): `axisOrigin` lies on the axis and `hole` is right for a hole, a boss, their mirror images and a reversed face. The M1 bracket at 6 mm has no thickness issues; worker test with a transferred mesh.

**Dependencies.** T3.1b. M1: kernel `MeshData` and `Topology` types, extended here.

**Footprint.** `packages/kernel/src/{types,topology}.ts` and their tests, `packages/kernel/README.md` (the topology entry: the two new fields, and that the axis direction's sign carries no meaning), `packages/print/src/{thickness,features,worker}.ts`, tests, README.

**Risks.** Rays from triangles near sharp edges hit the neighbouring face and read as thin; offset the origin slightly inward and ignore hits within a small angle of the source face. Curved thin shells (a 0.8 mm cylinder wall) need enough samples per facet; test with the `fine` tolerance mesh. The thread rule needs at least one `:thread:` face that OCCT reports as a cylinder (a truncated crest or root), since a helical flank has no axis in `FaceInfo`; T3.2e's goldens show whether its geometry leaves one. If it does not, T3.1c skips instead any hole group with a face that shares an edge with a `:thread:` face (`EdgeInfo.faces`), and the README records which rule is in use. The new `FaceInfo` fields change a kernel type every consumer of `Topology` sees; they are additive and nullable, so existing readers are unaffected.

### T3.2g: Fit presets and the fit-test coupon

**Goal.** Clearances for press, slip and sliding fits that users can apply everywhere and calibrate for their printer.

**Scope.**

- `packages/print/src/fits.ts`: default diametral clearances per printer family and nozzle: press 0.1 mm, slip 0.2 mm, sliding 0.4 mm at a 0.4 mm nozzle. These are **placeholders** (typical community values, estimates, not measured on Bambu printers) until T3.2h replaces them. Also sizes for heat-set insert holes and self-tapping screw holes for M2 to M5 if a vendor table can be cited (marked unverified per size otherwise, like `holes.ts`).
- App: **Insert fit variables** adds `#fit_press`, `#fit_slip` and `#fit_sliding` to the variables table in one `batch` (values from the active print setup's printer and nozzle, or the defaults), without overwriting existing ones; the expression field suggests them for diameter and offset fields; the hole dialog offers **Printed fit** next to the ISO clearance fits.
- A **fit-test coupon** template: a new document with a plate of pegs of one nominal diameter and a row of holes at stepped clearances (0.0 to 0.5 mm in 0.05 mm steps), each hole labelled with its clearance as debossed text (T3.2d when available; plain numbered holes before), plus a written procedure: which fit is which (press: goes in with force and stays; slip: slides in by hand without play; sliding: moves freely).
- Docs: `docs/user/fits.md` (new).

**Acceptance.** Unit tests for the table and the insert command (idempotent, undoable). e2e: insert fit variables, use `#fit_slip` in a hole diameter, change it, regen. The coupon document regenerates and passes T3.1's checks on an A1 mini.

**Dependencies.** T3.1b. M1: variables, hole dialog. Soft: T3.2d for labels.

**Footprint.** `packages/print/src/fits.ts`, `apps/web/src/{variables,features,print}/`, a coupon template under `apps/web/src/home/` or `print/`, docs.

**Risks.** Real clearances depend on printer, nozzle, filament and slicer settings (OrcaSlicer 2.4.2's BBL process profile for 0.20 mm layers, [`fdm_process_single_0.20.json`](https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/blob/v2.4.2/resources/profiles/BBL/process/fdm_process_single_0.20.json), sets `elefant_foot_compensation` to 0.15 mm, overriding 0 in [`fdm_process_common.json`](https://github.com/OrcaSlicer/OrcaSlicer/blob/v2.4.2/resources/profiles/BBL/process/fdm_process_common.json); research note, section 4); the docs must say the defaults are starting points.

### T3.3a: io: 3MF colours, transforms and multi-part objects

**Goal.** A 3MF from manufakture arrives in OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio with its parts named, placed as oriented, and each colour on its own filament slot, and still loads everywhere else.

**Scope.**

- `packages/io/src/threemf.ts` `write3mf(objects, options)`: per object an optional `color` (`#rrggbb`); the materials namespace declared with the prefix `m`; one `m:colorgroup` per distinct colour with one `m:color`, in first-use order; `pid` and `pindex="0"` on each coloured object; no `requiredextensions` for colours (decision 12). Build items with a `transform` per item (orientation and placement), several items per object for copies. Metadata: `Application` stays `manufakture`, `Title` the document name.
- Per the spike's evidence, either or both of: one component object holding several bodies of one item (a two-colour part stays one object with two parts), and a `Metadata/model_settings.config` with per-object or per-part `extruder`. Without evidence, neither. T3.0a's command-line results are enough to start; T3.0d's GUI results, when reported, confirm the choice or become a follow-up change.
- `parse3mf` reads colour groups, object `pid`, transforms and (if written) components, so our own files round-trip; `validate3mf` checks colour references, transforms (rigid, finite) and component references.
- The PrusaSlicer `interop` test slices a coloured, transformed file.
- README "3MF": what is written now and why (with a link to the research note).

**Acceptance.** io unit tests: two bodies with two colours give two groups and two `pid`s; three bodies in two colours give two groups; transforms round-trip exactly; a component file round-trips; `validate3mf` finds a dangling `pid`, a non-rigid transform and a missing component; the T3.0a fixtures parse. The PrusaSlicer interop test passes (when installed).

**Dependencies.** T3.0a (hard: fixtures and the command-line matrix); soft T3.0d (GUI results). M2: T2.1d for per-body export with colours; soft T2.3f, which adds build-item transforms for assemblies: whichever lands second reuses the other's transform writing.

**Footprint.** `packages/io/src/threemf.ts`, `threemf.test.ts`, `interop.test.ts`, fixtures, `packages/io/README.md`.

**Risks.** Slicers may change how they map colours in a release; T3.3c's check and the fixture matrix catch it.

### T3.4a: M3 acceptance: the PTFE tube cutting jig, end to end

**Goal.** Prove M3 the way `docs/m1-acceptance.md` proved M1: one walkthrough through the UI, hand-computed values, screenshots, and a file ready to print.

**The part.** A jig for cutting 4 mm outer-diameter PTFE (Bowden) tube square to length, remodelled from the kind of jig 3D printer owners print for themselves; the owner has one in daily use, which T3.4b compares against. Described generically:

- a block about 40 x 20 x 15 mm (variables `#length`, `#width`, `#height`);
- a through bore along the length for the tube, diameter `4mm + #fit_slip`, with a 0.5 mm chamfer at the entry;
- a slot across the bore for a single-edge razor blade, width `#blade + #fit_press` (`#blade` measured by the person printing it, default 0.3 mm), deep enough to cross the bore;
- a threaded hole on the side into the bore (M5 modelled internal thread) for a thumbscrew that holds the tube; with the block flat on its base this hole is horizontal, like the bore;
- the thumbscrew as a second body in a second colour: an M5 modelled external thread with clearance `#fit_slip` and a knurled or lobed head;
- debossed text on the top, for example "PTFE 4", 0.6 mm deep;
- a print setup on an A1 mini: the block laid flat on its base, the thumbscrew laid flat on its head.

If T3.2e found modelled threads not robust enough (its risk), the side hole becomes a cosmetic thread sized for a self-tapping screw and the thumbscrew body is dropped; the rest stands.

**Scope.**

- `apps/web/e2e/m3-jig.spec.ts`: model the jig through the UI; after each feature its status and the exact volume where it can be computed by hand (block, bore, chamfer and slot exactly; text by its region area times depth; threads against T3.2e's reference within its tolerance); printability: no bed-fit issue on the A1 mini; the 45 degree entry chamfer of the bore is ok for overhangs (its steepest line is 45 degrees from vertical, below the steep band); overhangs only where expected, each listed in the doc as documented and accepted: the top of the horizontal bore (4 mm plus `#fit_slip`) and the top of the horizontal side M5 hole, including its downward-facing thread flanks and crests there. The thumbscrew stands on its head, so its thread axis is vertical: an ISO M5 flank (30 degree flank angle, the helix lead tilting the normal slightly) sits at about 59.9 degrees from vertical, just inside the steep band and within mesh facet noise of the 60 degree threshold. Its downward-facing flanks are therefore expected as **steep**, and the spec tolerates overhang-classified triangles on the thumbscrew's thread faces up to 2% of those faces' area (an estimate, set from the first measured run and recorded in the doc) rather than asserting none; any overhang elsewhere on the thumbscrew fails. Teardrop flags (T3.1c, default 3 mm size): exactly one, on the bore (about 4.2 mm, horizontal). The blade slot crosses the bore and the side hole opens into it, so the bore is several cylindrical faces; T3.1c groups them by axis and radius into one hole, and the one flag lists them all. The modelled M5 side hole is not flagged: the cylindrical crest strips its thread leaves (about 4.13 mm across, which would otherwise exceed 3 mm) keep the side hole's own name, but they lie on the same axis as the thread's `:thread:` faces, and T3.1c leaves out every hole group coaxial with thread faces (T3.1c scope); the doc says so and points at its overhang listing instead. The side hole's axis is square to the bore's and meets it, so the two are different lines and the bore's flag is unaffected. The thumbscrew and the chamfer produce no flag (vertical axis; conical face). In the cosmetic-thread fallback above, the side hole is a cylinder and is flagged too if its diameter is above 3 mm, and the doc records which. No wall below the minimum except as documented; export for printing: `validate3mf`, two objects, two colour groups, both inside the A1 mini plate; change `#fit_slip` and see bore and threads update; reload.
- `docs/m3-acceptance.md` with the walkthrough, a checks table (like M1's), budgets (regen with threads and text, analysis time in the worker), and screenshots.

**Acceptance.** The spec passes in the e2e job; the doc is complete; the exported 3MF is uploaded as a CI artifact for T3.4b and for the optional OrcaSlicer check (T3.3c).

**Dependencies.** Every agent task above (T3.1d, T3.2d, T3.2f, T3.2g, T3.3b, T3.3c and what they depend on). M2: T2.1a to T2.1d.

**Footprint.** `apps/web/e2e/m3-*.spec.ts`, `docs/m3-acceptance.md`, `docs/m3-acceptance/*.png`.

**Risks.** A long spec is slow under SwiftShader; split it into chapters sharing a fixture, as M1 split views and budgets.

## Risks across M3

- **Threads may not be robust in OCCT's WASM build.** T3.2e has an explicit fallback (cosmetic threads only) that keeps the acceptance part printable.
- **Slicer behaviour is outside our control.** The research note reads today's sources; T3.0a's fixtures and T3.3c's optional check detect changes. The download hand-off works whatever the slicers do with URLs.
- **Three format bumps**, serialised with M2's four and M5's one. If schedules collide, T3.2c and T3.2f can share one bump, landed by whichever comes first, with the other extending the same version before any release (as M2's plan suggests for its own).
- **Analysis thresholds are heuristics.** Everything but "not printed at all" (OrcaSlicer's own minimum feature) and bed fit is an estimate; the UI says "may print badly", never "will fail", and every threshold is editable per setup.
- **Not in M3**: supports, brims and other slicer-side features (`print.brim` stays an example), automatic orientation, per-face colour painting, variable layer height, custom printers beyond a simple size, a direct connection to printers, and a custom-scheme launch (M7 at the earliest, research note).
