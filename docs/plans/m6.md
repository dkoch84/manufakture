# M6 plan: construction (walls, openings, floors, roofs, framing, takeoff, plans and elevations)

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-09-26
- Epic: "Can't CAD Can We?", milestone M6
- Builds on: M1 as landed (`packages/kernel`, `packages/core`, `packages/regen`, `packages/io`, `packages/sketch`, `packages/units`, `apps/web`), the [M2 plan](m2.md) (bodies, configurations), the [M4 plan](m4.md) (domain packages, extension translators, stock catalog, `tools` input, takeoff model, nesting, drawings), the [M3](m3.md), [M5](m5.md) and [M7](m7.md) plans (schema order, naming forms), [product decisions](../decisions/0000-product-decisions.md) and ADRs [0002](../adr/0002-kernel-build-and-loading.md) (kernel memory), [0004](../adr/0004-document-format.md) (document format), [0005](../adr/0005-units.md) (units, decision 8: pitch deferred to M6), [0006](../adr/0006-licensing.md) (licensing), [0007](../adr/0007-worker-protocol.md) (workers), [0009](../adr/0009-sync-model.md) (sync remap) and [0010](../adr/0010-scripting-sandbox.md) (scripting)

M6 makes manufakture useful to someone framing a small building. A level is a named elevation; a wall is a layer stack (studs, sheathing, drywall) drawn along a path on a level; openings (doors and windows) sit in walls with headers; floors have joists; roofs are gable or hip at a pitch typed as `6/12`. A framing generator turns each wall, floor and roof into real members (plates, studs at 16" or 24" on centre, king, jack and cripple studs, headers, blocking, joists, rafters), which a lumber takeoff counts and prices, and plan and elevation drawings dimension. The milestone is accepted when a 12' x 16' shed is framed in manufakture and its lumber takeoff matches a hand calculation.

**manufakture is not an engineering tool.** It lays out framing by geometric rules the user chooses (spacing, plate count, header size). It does not compute loads, check spans, size members, or check anything against a building code, and nothing it produces is a guarantee that a structure is safe or legal. Code tables are cited in this plan as references for the reader, never as engineering data in the app. Task T6.0c makes this visible to users, and every construction output carries it.

This document has three parts: research findings with sources, the design decisions the tasks rely on, then the task breakdown. "Checked" means read on 2026-09-26 from the source linked next to it; "measured" means run on that day; estimates and unverified claims are marked as such.

## Part 1: research findings

### Framing representation: what thousands of members cost

A 12' x 16' shed has on the order of 150 framing members, and a 2,000 sq ft single-storey house on the order of 800 to 1,000 (both estimates: perimeter and interior wall length divided by the spacing, plus plates, corners, openings, joists and rafters; T6.5a counts them from scripted fixtures). A two-storey house passes a few thousand. Today every body is a B-rep in the kernel worker, meshed, named and sent to the viewport as its own three.js `Mesh` with per-face pick ids (`apps/web/src/viewport/bodies.ts`, `picking.ts`). Two throwaway probes (not committed) put numbers on the alternatives. Node 26.10 on one development workstation, one run per row, so orders of magnitude, not budgets:

| Representation                                                                            | 100 studs | 1,000 studs | 3,000 studs | 10,000 studs | Notes                                                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------- | ----------- | ----------- | ------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| libcascade 3.0.2 through `Kernel.box` + `Kernel.mesh`, released after (`packages/kernel`) | 116 ms    | 698 ms      | 1,913 ms    | not run      | 0.64 ms per stud at 3,000; 12 triangles each; no naming, history, transfer or viewport included |
| `manifold-3d` 3.5.4: `Manifold.cube` + `translate`, then `compose` + `getMesh`            | 5.5 ms    | 17.3 ms     | 76.9 ms     | 332 ms       | module init 10 ms; 1,000 studs each with a notch subtracted (a birdsmouth stand-in): 70 ms      |

Other findings:

- The kernel's heap read 128 MiB before and after 4,100 boxes were made, meshed and released. That measure moves in steps of about 20 % (M4 plan, Part 1), so it only rules out a large leak; libcascade's empty destructors (ADR 0002: `delete()` frees nothing for 2102 of 5298 classes, `TopoDS_Shape` and every builder among them) still apply, and a regen of thousands of B-rep members would bring the instance recycle (1 GiB threshold, about 0.3 to 0.4 s plus a replay) much closer. T6.5a measures it with the leak probe from the T0.2 spike (`node-child.ts leakprobe`, [T0.2 report](../spikes/T0.2-occt.md), "Memory and the leak check"; T0.3 reused it): run the pipeline N times in a fresh process, then take 64 KiB blocks from the allocator until memory would have to grow, and compare N = 10 with N = 60.
- The B-rep path's cost is in the kernel worker on every regen of a dirty wall, and would grow further with names, history and per-face picking data per member. The Manifold path is about 25 times faster in this probe, and a box with no cuts needs no geometry kernel at all: its mesh is 12 triangles computed in TypeScript.
- three.js 0.186.1 (the app's version, checked in `node_modules`) has `InstancedMesh`, `BatchedMesh` and `LOD`. The viewport uses none of them today. Studs of one length are identical meshes under different transforms, so thousands of them can be one draw call; picking then needs a per-instance id in the pick pass (the viewport encodes 24-bit ids per face today).

**What this means for us** (a hypothesis for T6.5a to confirm or overturn): framing members should not be one B-rep body each. A member is data (a stock, a length, a placement and at most a few planar cuts), computed in pure TypeScript by the framing generator; the viewport draws members instanced; takeoff and framing drawings read the member data directly. A B-rep of a member is built only on demand (STEP export, a drawing view that needs hidden lines through members, a "convert to bodies" action). Members with cuts (birdsmouths, plumb cuts, gable studs cut to the roof) are meshed by Manifold or by our own planar clipping, whichever T6.5a finds simpler at equal cost. Wall layers (sheathing, drywall), which are few, large and cut by openings, stay B-rep bodies through M4's extension translators, so they get names, references and hidden-line drawings like any body.

### manifold-3d: license, size, API

- **License**: Apache-2.0 in the package's `package.json` and the `LICENSE` file inside the 3.5.4 tarball (checked by unpacking it). ADR 0006 lists "manifold, none yet, Apache-2.0, unverified"; this is the version to verify against at adoption (ADR 0006 decision 6: read from the installed package).
- **Size** (measured from the 3.5.4 tarball): `manifold.wasm` 541,470 bytes raw, 158,724 brotli (q11), 203,834 gzip (-9); the glue `manifold.js` 82,371 raw, 17,223 brotli. The package lists runtime dependencies (`@gltf-transform/*`, `commander`, `fast-xml-parser`, `@jscadui/3mf-export` and others), but they serve its ManifoldCAD bundler and command line; `manifold.js` imports nothing but Node built-ins inside Emscripten's own environment checks (checked). Published 2026-09 (npm `time.modified` 2026-09-25); repository [elalish/manifold](https://github.com/elalish/manifold).
- **API** (checked in `manifold-encapsulated-types.d.ts`): `Module()` then `setup()`; constructors `Manifold.cube`, `cylinder`, `extrude` (from a `CrossSection`), `ofMesh`; `translate`, `rotate`, `transform(Mat4)`; booleans `add`, `subtract`, `intersect`, batch `union` / `difference`, `split`, `compose` (disjoint parts into one without a boolean); `getMesh()` returns triangle and vertex arrays with run ids; `originalID()`, `asOriginal()` and `reserveIDs()` tag meshes so a composed mesh still says which input each triangle came from (useful for per-member picking). Every object needs an explicit `delete()` ("since these cannot be garbage-collected automatically"); whether its destructors really free memory is not known and T6.5a checks it the way libcascade's were checked.
- **Fit with ADR 0006**: Apache-2.0 is allowed. It is not LGPL, so it may be bundled, but it is still loaded as its own `.wasm` asset, lazily, inside the regen worker (ADR 0007), so an app that never frames never downloads it.

### web-ifc: license, size, API

- **License**: MPL-2.0 in `package.json` and `LICENSE.md` of the 0.0.78 tarball (checked). ADR 0006 allows MPL-2.0 unless files are marked "Incompatible With Secondary Licenses" (MPL-2.0 Exhibit B). The only file in the tarball containing that phrase is `LICENSE.md` itself (the license's own definition and the Exhibit B template). In the GitHub repository ([ThatOpen/engine_web-ifc](https://github.com/ThatOpen/engine_web-ifc), shallow clone of commit `a5578a2`, 2026-09-26) the phrase again appears only in `LICENSE.md`, and source files carry the Exhibit A header ("This Source Code Form is subject to the terms of the Mozilla Public License, v. 2.0"). So web-ifc is usable under ADR 0006 decision 2, pending the re-check at adoption. MPL-2.0 is file-level copyleft: its files stay MPL and their source must be offered, which the shipped notices (ADR 0006 decision 5) and the public repository cover.
- **Bundled C++** (from `src/cpp/CMakeLists.txt`, checked): fast_float, tinynurbs, glm, earcut.hpp, CDT, spdlog, stduuid, unordered_dense. Their licenses were **not** checked (CDT is believed to be MPL-2.0: unverified); T6.6a checks each before shipping.
- **Size** (measured from the 0.0.78 tarball): `web-ifc.wasm` 1,595,268 bytes raw, 439,717 brotli, 572,045 gzip; `web-ifc-api.js` 5,909,050 raw, 209,091 brotli (it carries the schema classes). A multi-threaded `web-ifc-mt.wasm` exists and needs cross-origin isolation, which ADR 0002 rules out; `Init(locateFile, forceSingleThread)` selects the single-threaded build. Unpacked, the package is 24.8 MB (most of it typings and three builds of the API). Version 0.0.78; npm `time.modified` 2026-09-21; still 0.0.x, so its API may change between releases.
- **API** (checked in `web-ifc-api.d.ts` and `ifc-schema.d.ts`): writing is `CreateModel(settings)`, `CreateIfcEntity(model, type, ...args)`, `GetNextExpressID`, `WriteLine` / `WriteLines`, then `SaveModel(model)` returning a `Uint8Array` of IFC-SPF text. Schemas `IFC2X3`, `IFC4` and `IFC4X3` are all present. Relevant enumerations: `IfcMemberTypeEnum` has `STUD`, `RAFTER`, `PLATE`, `PURLIN`, `POST`, `BRACE` and others; `IfcBeamTypeEnum` has `JOIST` and `LINTEL`. Reading and meshing IFC (`OpenModel`, `StreamAllMeshes`) are what the library is mostly used for; M6 only writes.
- **What this means for us**: IFC export is a stretch goal (T6.6a). web-ifc as a lazily loaded, separate `.wasm` in the regen worker, used only on export, costs about 650 KB brotli on first use. The alternative is our own IFC-SPF writer in TypeScript for the small subset we emit (project, site, building, storeys, walls, members, plates, slabs, roof, openings, doors, windows, their placements, extruded solids and aggregation relations); IFC-SPF is the same ISO 10303-21 text we already write for STEP. T6.6a starts with web-ifc as the epic says and keeps web-ifc as a development-only validator (reading our file back) if the own writer turns out smaller and simpler.

**Layout practice** ([Fine Homebuilding, "Laying Out Stud Walls"](https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/framing/laying-stud-walls), checked): layout marks stud edges, so "it doesn't matter whether you make a mark at 15-1/4 in. or 16-3/4 in.", as long as "the center of the stud ends up 16 in. from the stud to either side"; this lands 4' sheet edges on stud centres. One full-height king stud each side of an opening; jack studs are "the header depth shorter than kings and form the inside of the opening"; cripples under a rough sill "fall on the stud layout"; a corner is two studs in an L, one the outside nailer for sheathing, the other extending past the intersecting wall as a drywall nailer. **Precut studs** of 92-5/8" on one bottom plate and a double top plate give a 97-1/8" wall, which leaves room for ceiling drywall and a gap at the floor (per [Fine Homebuilding](https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/framing/do-precut-studs-make-sense) and [JLC](https://www.jlconline.com/how-to/framing/precut-stud-lengths_o/) as summarised by a search tool: unverified); 104-5/8" is the common 9' equivalent (unverified).

**Stock sizes** come from M4's catalog (T4.1c): PS 20-25 dry dressed sizes (a 2x4 is 1-1/2" x 3-1/2", a 2x6 1-1/2" x 5-1/2"), and sheet goods by PS 1 / PS 2 Performance Category (M4 plan, Part 1). M6 adds entries the catalog lacks: precut stud lengths, 7/16" OSB wall sheathing, 1/2" and 5/8" gypsum board in 4' x 8' and 4' x 12' (sizes from maker data; ASTM C1396 not read: unverified), each with a source and `verified` flag.

### Roof pitch math

Derived here from the geometry, with values computed on 2026-09-26. Pitch `p/12` means `p` units of rise per 12 units of run.

- **Angle**: `θ = atan(p / 12)`. `4/12` is 18.435°, `6/12` is 26.565°, `12/12` is 45°. Rise is `run × p / 12`.
- **Common rafter**: length along the rafter per unit of run is `√(12² + p²) / 12` (1.1180 for `6/12`). The run of a gable's common rafter is half the building span less half the ridge board's thickness; an overhang adds its own run times the same factor. Plumb cuts are vertical in the building and seat cuts horizontal; on the rafter, a plumb line meets the rafter's edge at `90° − θ`.
- **Birdsmouth**: a horizontal seat of length `s` (usually the plate's width) and a vertical heel cut of `s × tan θ` remove a notch `s × sin θ` deep, measured square to the rafter's edge: 1.565" for a 3-1/2" seat at `6/12`, 28 % of a 2x6's 5-1/2".
- **Hip and valley**: with equal pitches on both sides, a hip runs at 45° in plan, so it covers `12√2 = 16.97` units of run per 12 of common run. Its angle is `atan(p / 16.97)` (19.47° at `6/12`) and its length factor `√(16.97² + p²) / 12` (exactly 1.5 at `6/12`, since 288 + 36 = 324 = 18²). Jack rafters on the same spacing shorten by `spacing × common factor` each (17.89" at 16" on centre and `6/12`), with 45° side cuts. The hip's backing or dropping, and the ridge's reduction by the hip's thickness, are geometry the generator computes from the members' faces.

### Pitch notation in `packages/units`

ADR 0005 decision 8 deferred pitch to M6, "applies to angle fields only, so `6/12` stays a division everywhere else", and the units README says the same. The detail matters, because today `6/12` in an angle field is `0.5` read in the display angle unit, so half a degree, silently (units README: a bare `INT/INT` without a unit is ordinary division, and a bare result takes the display unit). The colon is not a token today (checked in `src/lexer.ts`), so it is free. Proposed rules for T6.0b, which amends ADR 0005:

1. **`rise:run` anywhere in an angle expression** is a pitch: `6:12` is `atan(6/12)`, and both sides may be lengths or bare numbers of the same dimension (`#rise:#run`, `7.5:12`). It binds looser than `*`, `/` and `^` and tighter than `+` and `-`, so `#rise*2:12` is `(#rise*2):12`, `6:24/2` is `6:12`, and `6:12 + 2°` works; it does not chain (`1:2:3` is an error). In length or number fields it is an error ("A pitch is an angle").
2. **Slope fields** (a field option the roof and ramp features set: `expected: 'angle', slope: true`) additionally read a **top-level division of two dimensionless operands** as a pitch, so the construction user can type `6/12`. A bare number with no unit is an error there ("Ambiguous: write 30° or 30/12"), the same stance the units package takes on `4-1/2`. Anything with an angle unit (`26.57°`, `atan2(6, 12)`) is an angle as before.
3. **Formatting**: `formatAngle(rad, { unit: 'pitch', run: 12, decimals, slope })` gives `6/12`, `7.5/12` (decimals for the rise, trailing zeros dropped; no hyphenated fractions, since `7-1/2/12` could not parse back) when `slope: true` and `6:12` otherwise. The formatter cannot tell a slope field from an angle field by itself: the caller passes `slope` from the same field option it passes to evaluation (`EvaluateOptions.slope`), so display and parsing always agree. Output parses back to the displayed value. Slope fields show pitch in documents whose length display is `ft-in` or `in-fraction`, and degrees otherwise; the user can switch.
4. No document shape changes: a slope field's stored expression is its source and entry units (ADR 0004 decision 7), and which fields are slope fields is fixed by the feature's schema, which is new in M6, so no stored `6/12` changes meaning.

## Part 2: design decisions the tasks rely on

These are proposals for the construction ADR (T6.0a) to confirm or change.

1. **A domain package, `packages/domain-construction`**, pure TypeScript, depending on `core`, `units` and the M4 wood domain's stock catalog and takeoff types (T4.0a decides whether those move to a shared package; M6 follows that decision). No kernel, regen or app dependency, so the framing generator and the takeoff run in Node tests without wasm. It registers its extension types with regen exactly as M4 decision 3 describes.
2. **Construction features are `extension` features** (M4 decisions 2 and 3): `construction.wall`, `construction.opening`, `construction.floor`, `construction.roof`, each with its own `schemaVersion` and params migrations. **Levels, wall types, roof and floor types, framing settings and the header rules** are document-level domain data in `domains.construction` (M4 decision 6, T4.1a), so none of them bumps the format.
3. **Levels are named elevations**, `{ id, name, elevation (expression), height (expression, the default wall height) }`. A wall, floor or roof names its level; changing a level's elevation moves everything on it. A level is not a part studio: a building is one part studio (M2 multi-body), so every member, layer and opening shares one regen and one reference namespace.
4. **Members are data, not bodies** (to be confirmed by T6.5a). The framing generator returns `Member { id, role, stock, length, placement (a frame), cuts (up to a few half-spaces: plumb, seat, bevel, birdsmouth), group (wall, opening, floor or roof id) }`. Regen carries members next to bodies as a **member set** per feature (T6.5b) with a mesh per distinct member shape (identical studs share one) and a transform per member. Members get no B-rep, no face names and cannot be referenced by features in M6; a member is picked as a whole and identified by `<feature id>:<member id>`. A member B-rep is built on demand only (STEP export of framing, drawings needing hidden lines through members), in batches, with instance recycling in mind. **STL and 3MF export** of a building include the members: each member is its shared mesh under its transform, written as its own object in 3MF (named by member id) and appended to the triangle soup in STL, next to the layer bodies; no B-rep is needed. **Measure** on members is out of scope in M6: picking a member shows its stock, length and cuts from member data in the info panel, and the measure tool works on layer bodies only.
5. **Member ids are stable by role and layout, and marked fragile.** A stud is named by its layout slot (`s12` is the stud at the 12th layout position from the wall's start), opening members by the opening's feature id and role (construction features are extension features, so their ids are `extension#n`, M4 decision 2: `extension#7:king-l`, `extension#7:jack-r`, `extension#7:header`, `extension#7:cripple-a2`), plates by course and splice (`top1:2`). The host wall frames its openings, so a full reference to an opening member is `extension#3:extension#7:king-l` (wall, then member). Lengthening a wall adds slots at the end and keeps the others; moving an opening renames the studs it displaces. Nothing in the document refers to a member except the user's per-member overrides (decision 8), which report `lost` like any reference when their member disappears. These ids are a new naming form for M7's name parser and id remap (M7 T7.1a): opening member ids embed the opening's feature id, which the remap rewrites, so the remap must know the form (see "Schema versions").
6. **The user chooses every structural size.** Wall types name stud stock, plate count and spacing; opening header sizes come from a user-editable header rules table (`opening width up to X: header Y, jack studs N`) in `domains.construction`. New documents get **no** header rules; a new wall type gets one default header (the user's choice when creating it) and every opening shows which rule or default it used. The app ships no load-based table (Part 1: copyrighted, load-dependent, and an engineering judgement). Warnings are layout warnings only (an opening wider than any rule, a ridge below 3/12 with no ties, a birdsmouth deeper than a third of the rafter), each labelled as a rule of thumb.
7. **Wall layers are bodies.** Sheathing and drywall (and optional siding) are B-rep bodies made by the wall's translator: a box per layer along each wall segment (an extrude of the layer's rectangular profile, since there is no box feature input), mitred or butted at corners, cut by openings through M4's `tools` input (T4.2a). One extension feature therefore makes several bodies, named `<feature id>:layer/<layer id>` (the form of M2's `pattern#2:i3` instance bodies). Core validation must accept such body ids for extension creators; that changes validation, not the document shape, so it needs no format bump; T4.1a is asked to include it, otherwise T6.1b adds it.
8. **Per-member overrides are domain params, not features.** A user can delete a member, change its stock or nudge a stud (a stud moved to clear a duct) through a list of overrides in the wall's params, keyed by member id. Anything more (a hand-framed detail) is ordinary bodies in the same part studio, which the takeoff counts through M4's cut list.
9. **Construction drawings are mostly analytic.** A framing elevation of a wall is the wall's members projected onto its plane: rectangles and cut lines computed from member data, no HLR. A floor plan is a horizontal section at the level's cut height: layer bodies cut by the M4 section path (T4.4b) plus member sections computed from member data. Building elevations use HLR on layer bodies (few and large). All of it feeds `packages/drawing`'s input types (M4 decision 7), with **chained dimension strings** (overall, openings by centreline or rough opening, stud layout) and a **pitch symbol** (the right triangle marked 12 and `p`).
10. **The takeoff is a set of producers on M4's model** (M4 decision 10, T4.3a): framing members grouped by stock and length, rounded up to lengths sold through the 1D layout (T4.3c) or listed as precut studs; plates and blocking by linear feet as well; sheathing and drywall as sheets from a per-face layout with offcut reuse through the 2D packer; everything priced from the stock overrides' prices. Hardware (nails, hangers, anchors) is out of scope.
11. **Not an engineering tool**, stated in the user docs, the construction workspace, every exported drawing's title block and every takeoff export (T6.0c).

### Schema versions: coordination with M2, M3, M4, M5 and M7

- M6 is designed to need **no format bump of its own**: walls, openings, floors and roofs are extension features, and levels, types, settings and header rules are `domains.construction` data (decision 2), both added by M4's T4.1a. Pitch notation changes no stored shape (Part 1). Member ids and layer body ids change validation and name parsing, not the shape (decisions 5 and 7). Dimensions to members and chained strings need no bump either way (next bullet).
- **One possible bump: T6.4a.** Plans and framing elevations are new view sources for T4.4c's `View`. If it lacks the reference kind or `chain`, construction dimension strings are **derived, never stored**: T6.4a computes them from wall, opening and member data at every drawing regen, the view's domain params only say which strings a view shows, and a user who wants to edit one converts it to ordinary T4.4c dimensions on layer faces. If T4.4c lacks the view source, T6.4a takes the next free `FORMAT_VERSION` with a migration and a fixture.

### T6.1b Features: wall and opening

- **Goal**: walls and openings are features that regenerate their layers and framing.
- **Scope**: `construction.wall` params (`schemaVersion` 1): level, wall type, path (a sketch polyline on the level's plane, or points as expressions), height (default the level's), justification, per-end join overrides, framing setting overrides, per-member overrides; `construction.opening` params: host wall (by feature id in `dependsOn`), kind (`door`, `window`, `opening`), position along the wall (expression, from an end or a centreline), unit size and rough opening allowance or rough opening size, sill height, header (a rule, the wall type's default or explicit), swing and hand for doors (drawing only). Translators (T4.1b): layer bodies per wall segment as extruded rectangles (decision 7), joined at corners, cut by the openings through `tools` (T4.2a), named `<id>:layer/<layer>` (decision 7, with the core validation change if T4.1a did not make it); members through T6.2a into a member set (T6.5b); corner joins found from walls sharing endpoints on a level; a simple placeholder body for door and window units (optional, off by default). Naming: core's README documents the `<id>:layer/<layer>` body id form and the `<feature id>:<member id>` reference form used by per-member overrides; if M7's T7.1a has already landed, this task also adds both forms to core's name parser and id remap (`packages/core/src/{names,remap}.ts`) with a name round trip test and a remap test per form, as T7.1a requires of every M3 to M6 form landing after it (see "Schema versions"). T6.2a does the same for the member id forms it defines.
- **Acceptance**: regen tests with the real kernel: a 16' wall of the default type makes its layer bodies with exact volumes and a member set matching T6.2a's fixture; an L of two walls joins at the corner; an opening cuts sheathing and drywall (volumes exact) and reframes the wall; moving the opening changes only that wall's results; deleting the wall fails the opening with a dependency error in the tree; a stock thickness override widens the wall; references to layer faces (a later feature on the sheathing) resolve `exact` after the wall lengthens.
- **Deps**: T6.1a, T6.5b, T4.2a. Through them T4.1b, T4.1c. Soft: M7 T7.1a (if landed, this task adds its forms to the parser and remap).
- **Footprint**: `packages/domain-construction/src/features/{wall,opening}.ts`, `packages/core/src/validate.ts` (body id form, if needed), `packages/core/src/{names,remap}.ts` (only if T7.1a has landed), tests, READMEs.
- **Risks**: walls meeting at angles other than 90 degrees: the layers mitre, the framing uses the same corner styles at the angle; walls crossing without a join are refused with a message.

- **Acceptance**: regen tests: the shed floor, walls and a `6/12` gable roof regenerate together with the member counts of the T6.2b and T6.2c fixtures; changing the pitch to `4/12` re-runs only the roof and its gable studs; sheathing area of each roof plane equals the hand value.
- **Deps**: T6.1b, T6.2b, T6.2c, T6.0b.
- **Footprint**: `packages/domain-construction/src/features/{floor,roof}.ts`, tests.
- **Risks**: the order between walls and roof (the roof reads the walls' top plates) must be a graph edge through `dependsOn`, not feature order alone.

### T6.3a Takeoff: framing and sheet-goods producers, cost

- **Goal**: a correct lumber and sheet-goods takeoff with a cost estimate, on M4's takeoff model.
- **Scope**: `TakeoffRow` producers (M4 T4.3a): framing members grouped by stock and cut length, then assigned to lengths sold by the 1D layout (T4.3c) with kerf, or to precut studs where they match; plates, blocking and fascia also totalled in linear feet; sheathing, subfloor, roof sheathing and drywall counted from a per-face sheet layout (full sheets from a chosen corner, cut around openings; offcuts reused on the same face and then across faces through the 2D packer) with an optional waste percentage; per-level and per-feature subtotals; cost as quantity times the stock's price from the overrides, with a currency setting and "no price" rows flagged; the configuration row in force (T2.4a) when the building uses configurations; formatting in the document's units; the disclaimer's short text on every export (T6.0c).
- **Acceptance**: hand-computed fixtures: the T6.2a 16' wall's rows (studs as 92-5/8" precuts; plates as three 16' pieces (one bottom, two top), or, when the settings' stock lengths stop at 12', as shorter pieces from the 1D layout with the two top plates' splices at least 24" apart); the shed floor's joists and rims; 4' x 8' sheathing for a 16' x 8' wall face with a 36" x 80" door is 4 sheets with the offcut noted; drywall in 4' x 12' sheets; cost totals; a configuration row with a longer shed changes only the affected rows.

## Risks across M6

- **Engineering expectations.** Users may read a framed model as a checked one. T6.0c, the absence of any "OK" or "compliant" label, the title blocks and the docs all say otherwise; reviewers of every M6 task check the wording.
- **Performance is the central technical risk**, which is why the spike (T6.5a) is first and the representation is decided on numbers, not on preference. If members must be B-rep after all, the kernel leak (ADR 0002) makes recycling frequent on a house; the custom trimmed kernel build (ADR 0002 step 3) would then move up in priority.
- **M4 is a hard prerequisite** for everything but the spike, the pitch notation, the disclaimer and the pure generators. If M4's domain mechanism slips, those four keep moving.
- **Code facts are secondary.** The IRC figures in Part 1 came through a summarising fetch of a state adoption; the app uses none of them as data, and T6.0a re-reads them before they reach the docs.
- **Regional practice** (metric framing at 400 or 600 mm centres, different plate conventions, CLS lumber) is only partly covered: spacing and stock are settings, the catalog is regional (M4), and defaults follow North American practice because the acceptance case is imperial.
- **Not in M6**: load, span, bracing and code checks; engineered lumber sizing; trusses (other than, possibly, placeholder members later); stairs; dormers, valleys, unequal pitches, irregular roofs; foundations beyond skids and a slab outline; insulation, MEP, finishes; per-member face references; hardware takeoff.

## Open questions

- Should the takeoff also show the common estimating shortcut (one stud per foot of wall plus extras) next to the as-framed count, since lumber yards quote that way?
- Should header rules ship with a template (empty rows with the IRC section cited) to make clear where the numbers come from, or stay empty?
- Are percent slopes (`25%`) wanted in slope fields for ramps and low-slope roofs, alongside `p/12` and degrees?
- Should a building be able to span several part studios (one per level) for very large projects, or does one part studio with instanced members scale far enough? T6.5a and T6.5d answer the second half.
