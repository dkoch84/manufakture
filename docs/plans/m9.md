# M9 plan: mechanical engineering, design an electromechanical machine to a target and specify it

- Status: proposed
- Date: 2026-10-10
- Epic: "Manufakture M9: mechanical engineering"
- Builds on: M1 to M8 as landed, ADRs [0004](../adr/0004-document-format.md) (document format), [0005](../adr/0005-units.md) (units), [0006](../adr/0006-licensing.md) (licensing), [0008](../adr/0008-assembly-mate-solver.md) (assembly mate solver), [0013](../adr/0013-domain-packages.md) (domain packages), [0015](../adr/0015-construction-domain.md) (construction, for its decision 8, which this plan departs from) and [0016](../adr/0016-agent-sessions.md) (agent sessions), and one ADR written for this plan: 0017, the mechanical domain (T9.0d)

M9 lets a person design a machine that moves and carries load, size it to a stated outcome, check that it holds together, and come away with specifications someone can build from. The reference machine is a portable cable trainer in the class of the Beyond Power Voltra I: a box of about 6 kg with a spool, a motor and a battery, which pulls a cable with a programmable force from 5 to 200 lbf. Designing one means answering questions manufakture cannot answer today: which motor, at what spool radius, makes 200 lbf at the cable; how hot the windings get holding it; where the energy goes when the user pulls the cable out (the motor generates); whether the shaft, the bearings, the bolts and the housing survive; how many sessions a 98 Wh pack lasts; and what the firmware must do so a fault never yanks the handle out of someone's hand.

The milestone is general on purpose. A cable trainer exercises every piece (a drivetrain, rotating parts, a battery, power electronics, a duty cycle, structure, safety limits), and the same pieces design a winch, a motorised camera slider, a robot joint, an e-bike drive or a CNC axis. Nothing in the domain is specific to fitness equipment except one scenario and the resistance modes, which are one kind of load profile among several.

## What the maintainer asked for

From the request of 2026-10-10, in his words: the ability "to design and plan a build like the Voltra 1, a portable cable resistance workout machine that uses an electric motor and batteries to create resistance", "to calculate stresses and everything needed to make it work for a desired outcome, and to produce not only a design but specifications for electrical and mechanical things". Three things follow, and every task below serves one of them:

1. **Design to an outcome.** The user states what the machine must do (force range and step, cable speed, travel, sessions per charge, mass, size, cost) and the app tells them whether a design does it, by how much, and if not, what to change. The sizing study (T9.8a) goes further and proposes designs.
2. **Calculate what makes it work.** Torques, speeds, currents, voltages, power, energy, heat, stresses, deflections, bearing life, fatigue, margins. Every number shows how it was computed.
3. **Specify it.** A mechanical specification, an electrical specification, a purchased-parts list with the ratings each part must meet, a calculation report, a control specification for whoever writes the firmware, a hazard analysis and a verification test plan.

## Where M8 leaves us

From the code (survey of 2026-10-10):

- **Materials carry density only.** `packages/core/src/materials.ts` has nine built-in materials (`pla`, `petg`, `abs`, `pine`, `oak`, `plywood`, `mdf`, `aluminium-6061`, `steel`) with `density`, a category and a source; no stiffness, strength, fatigue or thermal properties, and no user materials. A part or body picks one by id (`Part.material`, `bodies[].material`).
- **Mass properties stop at the centre of mass.** `packages/kernel/src/measure.ts` gives volume, area, centre of mass and the bounding box. There is no inertia tensor, which a rotating design needs first.
- **Units know length, angle, feed and spindle speed.** `packages/units` has `QuantityKind = 'length' | 'angle' | 'feed' | 'spindleSpeed' | 'number'`. No mass, force, torque, speed, power, energy, voltage, current, resistance, charge, temperature or stress. ADR 0005 decision 2: every number a user types goes through `packages/units`.
- **Assemblies solve poses, not motion.** `packages/assembly` has fastened, revolute, slider, planar, cylindrical and ball mates, limits on revolute and slider, sweeps over a mate's travel with interference (#1201) and renders at a pose (#1204). No gear, belt or screw couplings, no drivers, no dynamics; `kind: 'gear'` is rejected on purpose (ADR 0008 decision 6, which names OndselSolver as the first candidate when couplings come).
- **Purchased parts exist for one family.** Drawer slides (`packages/domain-wood/src/slides/`, #1200) are a catalog with clearance models, sources and `verified: false`, counted as `hardware` lines in the cut list and served as `manufakture://tables/hardware`. Heat-set inserts, holes and threads are tables (`packages/print/src/fits.ts`, `packages/kernel/src/holes.ts`, `threads.ts`). The slide write-up already says other purchased parts "can join the catalog the same way".
- **Engineering checks exist in one place.** The wall-thickness check (`packages/regen/src/wall-check.ts`, #1210) runs after a regen, measures with a kernel op and emits `RegenWarning`s with the measured value and the minimum. Print analyses (`packages/print/src/`) run in a worker. This is the pattern T9.5a generalises.
- **The domain pattern is settled.** ADR 0013: a domain is `packages/domain-<name>` owning a namespace, its features are `extension` features with translators, settings go in `domains.<namespace>`, and data that needs references, ids or variables goes in a typed section of its own (decision 9; `print` and `cam` are examples).
- **Outputs.** `packages/drawing` makes views, dimensions and title blocks (no BOM table or balloons yet); `packages/io` writes STEP, STL, 3MF, DXF, SVG and PDF; `packages/takeoff` has rows with `each`, length, area and volume units (no mass); the review bundle (`packages/review`) summarises domain data per domain; MCP serves 18 tools and exports from `bom-csv` to `drawing-pdf`.
- **No simulation, FEA or electrical code exists anywhere.** The only physics is CAM's material-removal simulation.
- **The project has said, of construction, that it is not an engineering tool.** ADR 0015 decision 8: no loads, spans or sizing, nothing labelled safe or passing. The maintainer's request asks for the opposite in this domain, which is decision 1 below.

## Decisions that cut across tasks

1. **The mechanical domain calculates, shows its working and never certifies.** This departs from ADR 0015 decision 8 for the mechanical domain only; construction keeps its stance. Every result is a calc record (decision 4) that names the method, the inputs, the assumptions and the source of each property. Strength and life results are stated as a margin against a safety factor the user chose ("shaft at the bearing seat: von Mises 182 MPa, yield 415 MPa, factor 2.28 against your 2.0"). Performance results against the user's own targets may say "meets" or "misses" ("200 lbf: meets, 14% torque headroom"). Nothing says a machine is safe, certified or compliant with a standard. A short disclaimer, like construction's, appears in the tools, every specification and the calculation report; its wording is the maintainer's (T9.12b).
2. **One domain, `mech`, covering mechanical and electrical.** The drivetrain simulation needs the motor, the controller and the battery in one model, and ADR 0013 forbids one domain importing another, so electrical is part of `packages/domain-mech`, not a domain of its own. Formula code with no domain vocabulary (beam bending, shaft fatigue, bearing life, bolt preload, wire ampacity) goes in a shared package, `packages/calc`, that any domain may use.
3. **The `mech` section is typed, not `domains.mech`.** Requirements and load cases refer to variables, assembly instances, mate connectors and faces (an FEA load on a face), which ADR 0013 decision 3 keeps out of domain data. So `mech` is a typed document section like `print` and `cam`, validated by core's envelope and the domain, with one format bump (T9.1e). Plain settings with none of those (default safety factors, report options) may still live in `domains.mech`; ADR 0017 draws the line.
4. **Every number is a calc record.** A check, a simulation result or an FEA result produces `{ id, title, method, formula, inputs[], result, unit, limit?, margin?, assumptions[], sources[], status }`, where each input names where it came from (a requirement, a catalog field, a measured face, a material property, a simulation peak). Records are derived, recomputed from the document and never stored (ADR 0004 decision 1); the calculation report (T9.9c) is records printed in order. A status is `ok`, `warning` (below the user's factor or target) or `unknown` (an input is missing, named), never "safe".
5. **Hand calculations first, FEA second, both always available.** Classical methods (Shigley, Roark, ISO 281, VDI 2230, AGMA simplified) are fast, exact for their assumptions and explainable, and they run on every regen. FEA (T9.6) checks real geometry where the hand calculation's assumptions do not hold, on demand in a worker. Where both exist for one feature (a shaft), the report shows both and their difference.
6. **Simulation is a lumped, deterministic, time-stepped model.** One degree of freedom along the drivetrain (cable, spool, reduction, rotor), a motor model from datasheet constants (torque constant, resistance, inductance, losses), a controller with current and voltage limits, a battery with open-circuit voltage against state of charge and internal resistance, and two-node thermal models for the windings and the cells. No multibody dynamics and no electromagnetic field solving. The user is a prescribed motion or force profile (a rep), so results are repeatable and testable.
7. **Purchased parts are data with ratings, and the app ships examples, not a vendor database.** A purchased part (motor, controller, cell, bearing, belt, cable, fuse, connector) is a catalog entry with typed rating fields, a source and `verified: false` until someone checks it against a datasheet, exactly as the slide catalog does. The app ships a small set of representative entries with typical published values, and the user adds their own from a datasheet. No scraping of vendor sites and no claims that a listed part is current.
8. **Electrical means system design, not schematic capture or PCB layout.** The electrical model is components, connections and harness segments with currents, voltages and lengths, from which the app sizes wires, fuses and connectors and draws a block diagram and a wiring diagram. Schematic capture, PCB layout and circuit simulation are out of scope (a KiCad netlist export is a later phase).
9. **Firmware is specified, not written.** The control specification (T9.9d) states the resistance laws, the limits, the loop rates and sensor resolutions the design needs and the fault responses. manufakture writes no firmware and generates no code for a microcontroller.
10. **Agents get the same tools.** Every new read (requirements status, simulation, checks, FEA results, specifications) is a query in `packages/session` and an MCP tool, and every write is a core command, so an agent can design a machine by conversation under ADR 0016's review gate.

## The cable trainer, by the numbers

Why the simulation and the electrical checks are in scope, with illustrative values (T9.0b replaces them with modelled ones):

- **Torque.** 200 lbf is 890 N. On a spool of 25 mm effective radius that is 22 N·m at the shaft. Direct drive (no reduction) needs a motor with that much continuous torque; a 5:1 belt needs 4.5 N·m plus the belt's losses, and spins the motor five times faster.
- **Speed and power.** A brisk pull at 1.5 m/s turns that spool at 60 rad/s (573 rpm) and moves 1.3 kW of mechanical power.
- **The pull is the generating phase.** When the user pulls the cable out, the motor's torque opposes the motion, so the motor absorbs the user's work and generates. When the cable winds back in under load, the motor drives. At 200 lbf over a 0.6 m stroke, each pull returns about 530 J to the DC bus. A 1.7 Ah pack (the Voltra's is 57.6 V, 97.9 Wh) taking 1.1 kW is being charged at about 11C, several times what typical lithium-ion cells accept. So the design needs a braking resistor and a chopper, a pack that accepts it, or both, and the app must say so from the numbers. Nobody finds that by drawing the box.
- **Holding is the hottest case.** An isometric hold at 200 lbf moves no power at all, yet with a torque constant of 0.5 N·m/A it draws about 45 A and dissipates around 160 W in the windings (with an assumed 0.08 Ω), all of it heat. Thirty seconds of that is 4.8 kJ into a few hundred grams of copper and iron.
- **Battery life is set by losses.** Sessions per charge depend on copper, iron, controller and resistor losses plus what the regenerated energy can and cannot put back, not on the work done.
- **The 100 Wh line.** 97.9 Wh is just under the usual airline limit for carrying a battery on board, which is a requirement, not an accident. Requirements can therefore be about the parts themselves, not only about performance.

## The domain model

ADR 0017 fixes the shapes; this is the picture it starts from.

- **Requirements** (`mech.requirements`): named targets, each a quantity with a comparison and an optional tolerance: `maxForce >= 890 N`, `forceStep <= 4.45 N`, `peakCableSpeed >= 2 m/s`, `travel >= 2.5 m`, `sessionsPerCharge >= 2 (at loadCase 'max')`, `mass <= 6.5 kg`, `packEnergy < 100 Wh`, `envelope within 300 x 200 x 150 mm`. Values are `StoredExpression`s and may use variables, so "the spool radius that meets the force target" can be a variable the user tunes.
- **Load cases** (`mech.loadCases`): what the machine sees. For a cable trainer: a rep profile (force law and motion), an isometric hold, a snatch (the user yanks the slack cable), a side pull at the worst angle the fairlead allows, a drop from 1 m (static equivalent), transport. Each names its duration or repetitions, so the duty cycle is part of it.
- **Resistance modes** are load-case force laws: constant, eccentric (more force on the way back), chains (force rising with extension), isokinetic (speed limited), damper and rowing (force from speed), and a user-defined table. They are what the control specification (T9.9d) turns into firmware requirements.
- **Drivetrain** (`mech.drivetrain`): an ordered chain of stages from the source to the output, each referring to assembly instances where geometry exists: motor (catalog part), reductions (belt, gear, planetary, with ratio, efficiency and inertia), shafts and bearings (bodies and catalog bearings), the spool (body), and the cable (catalog rope). Inertias come from the kernel (T9.1c) and catalogs, and are reflected through the ratios.
- **Electrical system** (`mech.electrical`): components (pack, BMS, fuse, switch, precharge, controller, braking resistor and chopper, motor, charger input, DC-DC converter, controller board, encoder, load cell, display), connections between their terminals, and harness segments with lengths, measured between instances in the assembly or typed in.
- **Stress studies** (`mech.studies`): an FEA study names bodies, a load case, fixtures and loads by face reference, a mesh size and the material.
- **Purchased parts** live in the catalogs of `packages/domain-mech` (built-in) and in the document (the user's own entries), and appear in the BOM with every rating the design relies on.

## Tasks

Each task lands with tests (Node for packages, Playwright for app behaviour), passes `make lint`, `make typecheck` and `make test`, updates the README of every package it touches and the user docs it affects (`docs/user/mechanical.md`, new in T9.1e), and adds an ADR 0006 license row for every new dependency.

### T9.0a: Spike: FEA in the browser (volume mesher and linear static solver)

**Goal.** Know whether linear static stress analysis on manufakture bodies can run in the browser, with what, how accurately and how fast.

**Scope.** Volume meshing of an OCCT solid (candidates: Netgen, LGPL; gmsh, GPL; TetGen, AGPL; each built to wasm or found prebuilt), and a solver (candidates: an own TypeScript or Rust-to-wasm solver for quadratic tetrahedra with a preconditioned conjugate gradient; CalculiX built to wasm). Licences checked against ADR 0006 (GPL-3.0-or-later; LGPL loaded as a separate module). Benchmarks: a cantilever against beam theory, a plate with a hole against Peterson's stress concentration factor, a thick-walled cylinder against Lamé, each at three mesh densities; time and memory at 50k, 200k and 500k degrees of freedom in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (the no-root recipes in the CLI notes).

**Acceptance.** A write-up in `docs/spikes/` with a recommendation; analytical cases within 5% at the recommended density; 200k degrees of freedom solved in under 30 s in Chromium on the reference machine, or a stated reason why not and what limit replaces it. **Dependencies.** M8. **Footprint.** `spikes/T9.0a-fea/`, `docs/spikes/`. **Risks.** No mesher builds cleanly to wasm; then the fallback is a mesher in a server-side helper, which contradicts M7's "the server never computes geometry" and must go to the maintainer.

### T9.0b: Spike: electromechanical rep simulation

**Goal.** Prove that the lumped model of decision 6 gives believable numbers, and replace the illustrative values above with modelled ones.

**Scope.** One script: a rep (force law, stroke, speeds) through cable, spool, optional reduction and a PMSM model (torque constant, phase resistance, inductance, pole pairs, iron and friction losses), a controller (current limit, bus voltage, efficiency map or fixed losses), a pack (open-circuit voltage curve, internal resistance, charge acceptance) with a braking resistor, and a two-node winding thermal model. Integrator choice and step size; energy balance closes to 1%. Compare against two published motor datasheets' torque-speed and efficiency curves.

**Acceptance.** A write-up with the cable trainer's numbers at 200 lbf (torque, current, copper loss, regenerated power per pull, resistor energy, winding temperature after 10 reps and after a 30 s hold, energy per session) and the model's error against the datasheets. **Dependencies.** M8. **Footprint.** `spikes/T9.0b-sim/`, `docs/spikes/`.

### T9.0c: Research: reference machines, catalog data and standards

**Goal.** Ground the scenario and the catalogs in published facts.

**Scope.** Published specifications of cable trainers (Voltra I, Tonal, Vitruvian Trainer+ and others): force, speed, mass, battery, modes. Representative motors (direct drive outrunners and gimbal motors used in robot joints and trainers), controllers (open hardware FOC controllers), cells (high-power 18650 and 21700), bearings, timing belts, synthetic rope; which datasheet fields each needs. The standards a builder should know about, by name and scope only: fitness equipment (ISO 20957), battery safety and transport (IEC 62133, UN 38.3), electrical safety of household appliances (IEC 60335) and EMC. No standard's text is copied.

**Acceptance.** `docs/research/electromechanical.md` with sources; the scenario's requirement list (T9.11a) drafted from it; field lists handed to T9.2b to T9.2e. **Dependencies.** M8. **Footprint.** `docs/research/`.

### T9.0d: ADR 0017: the mechanical domain

**Goal.** Fix the shapes and rules every other task builds on.

### T9.1d: `packages/calc`: engineering formulas that return calc records

## Open questions

Three questions for the maintainer that change what the product does. Until they are answered, the plan assumes the default stated with each.

1. **How far the app goes in judging safety.** The plan reports stress and life as numbers and margins against a safety factor you choose, flags anything below it, and never calls a design safe or certified (decision 1). Construction keeps its "no engineering" stance. Assumed: yes. The alternative is to label results "pass" or "fail" against default factors the app picks, which is friendlier and claims more.
2. **Electrical depth.** The plan sizes the electrical system and draws block and wiring diagrams and a harness table, but does not draw circuit schematics or lay out circuit boards (decision 8). Assumed: that is enough for M9, with export of a netlist to KiCad as a later step. Say so if you want schematics in this milestone; it roughly adds a quarter to its size.
3. **Where FEA runs.** The plan runs stress analysis in the browser, so it works offline like the rest of the app. If the spike (T9.0a) finds the browser too slow or no mesher builds for it, the fallback is running it on your self-hosted sync server, which breaks "the server never computes geometry" from M7. Assumed: browser only, with a smaller model limit if needed, and the question comes back to you only if the spike fails.

## Later phases (not in M9)

- Gear, belt and screw coupling mates in assemblies, and kinematic drivers (ADR 0008 decision 6, OndselSolver).
- Dynamics beyond one degree of freedom; modal analysis and vibration; fatigue FEA; contact and nonlinear materials; thermal FEA.
- Schematic capture, PCB layout, circuit simulation; KiCad netlist export.
- Firmware code generation.
- Cost models with supplier prices and quantity breaks.
- Pneumatics and hydraulics.

## Risks across M9

- **Scope.** Forty-five tasks, the largest milestone so far. The sizing study and FEA are the most separable; if the milestone has to shrink, FEA moves to M10 first, since hand calculations cover the reference machine's shafts, bearings and bolts.
- **False confidence.** Numbers with three significant figures look authoritative. Mitigated by calc records that show assumptions and sources, `verified: false` on catalog data, the validation suite and decision 1's wording.
- **Catalog data quality.** Typical published values vary by vendor and batch; motor constants are often quoted line to line or phase to neutral without saying which. ADR 0017 fixes the conventions and the catalog editor states them next to each field.
- **Printed parts.** Strength of printed parts depends on orientation, layer adhesion and infill, which a linear isotropic model does not capture. Records for printed bodies say so and use a reduced allowable that the user can change.
- **The format bump.** One bump, serialised behind any other in flight, as before.
