# 0014: CAM architecture: a document-level cam section, a pure cam package and worker, posts as data

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-10-02

## Context

M5 turns a part into G-code for the Shapeoko: a tool library, stock and a work coordinate system (WCS), 2.5D operations (facing, profile, pocket, drill, V-carve), a toolpath preview with a material-removal simulation, a GRBL post-processor first, 3D surfacing later, and DXF/SVG export for laser and plasma ([M5 plan](../plans/m5.md)). The plan's Part 2 lists ten design decisions and a first cut of the document shape, written for this ADR to confirm or change (task T5.0c). This ADR records them before code, so the schema task (T5.1b), the package task (T5.1c), the regen stage (T5.1f), the CAM worker (T5.1g) and the operation and post tasks share one design.

The inputs:

- **The [M5 plan](../plans/m5.md)**: Part 1's research (Clipper2 ports and their licenses, OpenCAMLib and its WASM build, Kiri:Moto as a reference, G-code parsers for CI, and what Grbl 1.1, grblHAL and Carbide Motion accept), Part 2's decisions 1 to 10 and the `CamData` sketch, and the T5.0c task section.
- **[ADR 0004](0004-document-format.md)**: nothing derived is stored (decision 1); every change of shape bumps `version` with a migration and a fixture (decision 2); ids are permanent (decision 4); references are names, resolved with the vocabulary `exact`, `descendant`, `ancestor`, `ends`, `ordinal`, `fragile`, `lost`, `ambiguous` (decisions 5 and 6); numbers are `StoredExpression`s (decision 7); the cache key covers a feature's inputs and an implementation version (decision 8).
- **[ADR 0005](0005-units.md)**: millimetres and radians inside; every typed number goes through `packages/units`; its consequences ask for a time dimension before feeds and speeds exist.
- **[ADR 0006](0006-licensing.md)**: the dependency allowlist. Decision 2 admits LGPL 2.0 and 2.1 only, and only as a separately loaded, replaceable `.wasm` (decision 4); a new dependency's license is read from the installed package (decision 6).
- **[ADR 0007](0007-worker-protocol.md)** and its amendments: Comlink, one interface per worker, coarse calls, cancellation by generation, errors as data, buffers transferred. Its contexts are now four: the main thread, the kernel worker (which runs the regen engine and, per the first amendment, the regen solver), the solver worker and the print-analysis worker (second amendment).
- **[ADR 0012](0012-3d-printing.md)**, the closest precedent: print setups in a typed top-level `print` section with their own `nextIds` (decision 1), print references that never block modelling (decision 2), a printer table as data with permanent ids and cited sources (decision 3), a pure `packages/print` (decision 4), and a lazily started print-analysis worker that is deliberately not shared with M5's CAM worker (decision 5, which leaves this ADR free to propose sharing).
- **[ADR 0013](0013-domain-packages.md)**: domain geometry is an `extension` feature with a translator (decision 2); plain domain settings go into the namespaced `domains` field, which holds "settings, not model": no face or edge names, no feature or body ids, no variable uses (decision 3); document-level domain state that holds references, ids or variable uses is a typed section with its own ADR and format bump, naming `print` and M5's `cam` (decision 9).
- **What the code has today.** At the time of writing, core's document has `print`, `drawings` and `domains` as top-level sections. Parts have bodies (M2), and regen reports a `bodyKey` per body: equal keys mean identical bodies. Core has a `hole` feature (simple, counterbore, countersink). Regen's drawing stage (`packages/regen/README.md`, "Drawings") already does on-demand work in the kernel worker: it takes the client's current generation and never starts a new one, runs on the regen chain through the cache, and caches its plain-data results by body keys and a stage version.
- **The [T5.0a spike](../spikes/T5.0a-clipper2.md)** (done 2026-10-02): clipper2-ts and clipper2-wasm compared on sketch regions, 10,000-vertex outlines and 50-ring pockets, with an integer scale, an arc refit method and measured errors.
- **The [T5.0b spike](../spikes/T5.0b-opencamlib.md)** (done 2026-10-02): OpenCAMLib (OCL) built by us as an ES module plus a separate `.wasm`, against a TypeScript drop-cutter written in the spike, on the M1 bracket and a 99,200-triangle filleted part. The TypeScript cutter agrees with OCL to 2.3e-12 mm for flat and ball cutters, is 2.5 to 6.9 times faster than the single-threaded OCL build, and uses about an eighth of the memory; OCL's V cutter gouges by up to 0.108 mm. The stricter reading of OCL's license is LGPL-3.0. It recommends the TypeScript drop-cutter, no OCL, no ADR 0006 amendment, and waterline out of M5.
- **The project owner's decisions for M5**: the target machines are the Shapeoko 5 Pro 4x4 and the Shapeoko 4 XXL in their default configurations, with the Shapeoko 5 Pro 4x4 as the default profile; 3D surfacing follows T5.0b: if it recommended OpenCAMLib, OCL would ship only as a separate `.wasm` whose source and build recipe we publish, with an ADR 0006 amendment for LGPL-3.0; otherwise our own TypeScript drop-cutter. T5.0b recommended the latter.

## Decision

1. **A new package, `packages/cam`**, pure TypeScript under GPL-3.0-or-later. It holds the CAM types, the toolpath intermediate representation (IR), WCS and stock math, the offset adapter, the operations, linking, the material-removal simulation, the machine and tool tables (decision 11), the post-processor engine and the posts (decision 10), a G-code verifier for tests, and the CAM worker's entry and client (decision 7).
   - **What it may depend on.** At run time: the polygon library of decision 12 (its one third-party computational dependency), `packages/units` for unit conversion (ADR 0005 decision 1: a post writing G20 converts through it, never on its own) and `comlink` for the worker entry, as `packages/print` does. Type-only devDependencies on `packages/core` (the `cam` section's types) and `@manufakture/kernel` (`MeshData`) are allowed, as in ADR 0012 decision 4 and ADR 0013 decision 1; type imports are erased.
   - **What it never depends on**: `packages/kernel` at run time, `packages/regen`, any domain package, the app or the DOM. Everything in it runs in Node tests with no kernel `.wasm` loaded.
   - **Plain inputs.** Its functions take evaluated numbers in internal units (millimetres, radians, and the time units of decision 3), 2D loops of lines and arcs, points with diameters, depths and meshes, all as plain data. The caller evaluates the document's expressions and resolves its references first (decisions 5 and 7), as `packages/print`'s callers evaluate thresholds. So `packages/cam` never sees a `StoredExpression`, a face name or a document.
   - **The client is a thin worker wrapper.** `@manufakture/cam/client` only starts the CAM worker and forwards calls with their generation, as `@manufakture/print/client` does. Expression evaluation, the calls to the regen geometry stage and the orchestration of a generation (decision 7) live in `apps/web`, as print's do in `apps/web/src/print/resolve.ts`. That keeps `packages/cam` free of core and regen at run time.
   - It is **not a domain package** in ADR 0013's sense: it registers no translator, owns no `domains` namespace, and is not named `domain-cam`. CAM produces toolpaths, not bodies.

2. **CAM state is a typed, top-level `cam` section of the document**, next to `parts` and `print`, holding `{ tools, setups, nextIds }`. A setup names the part (and optionally one body) it machines, its stock, WCS, heights, machine and post, and an ordered list of operations in cut order. Nothing derived is stored (ADR 0004 decision 1): no extracted loops, no toolpaths, no simulation results, no G-code. `diffDocuments` reports a CAM edit as `camChanged`, a boolean like `printChanged`, with no `firstAffectedIndex`; which setups and operations changed is for the app to find by diffing the two `cam` sections, which the cache keys of decision 9 already do. Regen never reads the section, so a CAM edit never dirties a regen.

   **Why a typed section and not `domains` (ADR 0013).** CAM state holds exactly what ADR 0013 decision 3 keeps out of `domains`: face references (a planar face as a geometry source, a face as the WCS up direction), feature ids (a sketch region, a hole feature), body ids (the setup's body) and expressions with variable uses (`#depth + 1mm` as a cut depth). Core's validation, variable rename and inline, and `diffDocuments` must reach all of them. That is ADR 0013 decision 9's test for a typed section, and decision 9 names `cam` as one. ADR 0012 made the same choice for `print` for the same reason.

   **Why CAM operations are not part features.** An operation changes no body. In the feature list it would sit in regen's chain, dirty a regen on every feed rate edit, and need a position in a part's history, while it must always act on the part's **final** body (decision 5) whatever features are added after it. Its order is cut order within a setup, not regen order, and a setup's operations share a stock, a WCS and a machine that no feature has.

   **Why not `extension` features.** ADR 0013 decision 2 makes `extension` the kind for domain **geometry**: an extension is translated into kernel `FeatureInput`s and takes part in regen's dependency graph, scope and cache. An operation has no kernel input to translate into and must not enter that graph. Using the kind anyway would put every toolpath parameter into opaque `params` or regen-visible `expressions`, make every operation a feature that blocks deletion of what it names (decision 6 forbids that), and make regen report each one. The M5 plan's decision 2 already ruled this out; ADR 0013 decision 9 confirms it.

   The shape, as a first cut for T5.1b (field names and the per-kind fields may still change in that task; the rules in this ADR may not):

   ```ts
   interface ManufaktureDocument {
     // ...as at the version T5.1b starts from
     cam: CamData; // the migration adds { tools: [], setups: [], nextIds: {} }
   }

   interface CamData {
     tools: CamTool[]; // the tools this document uses, copied from a library (decision 11)
     setups: CamSetup[];
     nextIds: Record<string, number>; // 'tool', 'setup', one per operation kind, 'r'; only ever increase
   }

   interface CamTool {
     id: string; // 'tool#1'
     name: string;
     kind: 'flat' | 'ball' | 'bull' | 'vbit' | 'drill' | 'engraver';
     number?: number; // the tool number a post writes with T and M6
     diameter: StoredExpression;
     fluteLength: StoredExpression;
     flutes: number;
     cornerRadius?: StoredExpression; // bull
     angle?: StoredExpression; // V-bit included angle, drill point angle
     tipDiameter?: StoredExpression; // V-bit flat tip
     presets: FeedPreset[]; // per material category: spindle speed, feed, plunge, stepdown, stepover
     source?: { library: string; id: string }; // where it was copied from
   }

   interface CamSetup {
     id: string; // 'setup#1'
     name: string;
     part: string; // part id
     body?: string; // body id; absent: the part's only body (an error on the setup if it has several)
     machine: string; // machine table id (decision 11); not checked by the schema
     post: string; // post id ('grbl', ...); not checked by the schema
     stock: CamStock; // a box from the body's bounds plus margins, or an explicit size and offset; a material category
     wcs: CamWcs; // up direction (a model axis or a planar FaceReference), origin (stock corner or centre, top or bottom)
     heights: { clearance: StoredExpression; retract: StoredExpression };
     operations: CamOperation[]; // cut order
   }

   interface CamOperation {
     id: string; // 'profile#1', 'pocket#2'
     kind: 'facing' | 'profile' | 'pocket' | 'drill' | 'vcarve' | 'surface3d';
     name: string;
     suppressed: boolean;
     tool: string; // a CamTool id
     geometry: CamGeometrySource[];
     // kind-specific fields: depths, stepdown, stepover, side, tabs, lead-in, ramp, ...
   }

   type CamGeometrySource =
     | { kind: 'face'; face: FaceReference } // a planar face; core's FaceReference, id 'r<n>'
     | { kind: 'region'; sketch: string; entities?: string[] } // a sketch region, the shape of a feature's `profile`
     | { kind: 'hole'; feature: string }; // a hole feature: its axis points and through-hole diameter (decision 5)
   // later: edges, whole-body silhouettes; surface3d machines the setup's body
   ```

   The differences from the plan's sketch: a setup names `{ part, body? }` rather than only `partId`, face sources and the WCS face are core's `FaceReference` (the face-only form of the `Reference` wrapper, as ADR 0012's `layFlat` uses), and a sketch region source is `kind: 'region'` (decision 4).

   **A setup machines one body.** The pair `{ part, body? }` is the one ADR 0012's `PrintItem` uses, but an absent `body` means something different here. For a print item it means every body of the part, since a printer can print several bodies side by side. A setup has one stock, one WCS and one set of heights, all derived from one body's bounds, and its toolpaths are checked against that body; several bodies in one stock would need a layout this ADR does not design. So an absent `body` means the part's only body, and is an error on the setup (naming the bodies) when the part has several; the user then picks one. Several bodies on one sheet is a later setup kind, not a reading of `body?`.

3. **Every number a user types is a `StoredExpression`**, feeds and speeds included, so `1/4"` is a valid tool diameter in a millimetre document and `#depth + 1mm` works in an operation (ADR 0005 decision 2). `packages/units` gains the time dimension first (T5.1a, landed): feed rates in `mm/min` and `in/min`, spindle speeds in `rpm`. Core's `variableUses`, `renameVariable` and `inlineVariable` reach every expression in `cam`.

4. **Ids are permanent, from `cam.nextIds`** (ADR 0004 decision 4): tools `tool#n`, setups `setup#n`, operations `<kind>#n` (`profile#2`, `pocket#1`), references `r<n>`. Because the counters are document-level, each id is unique across the whole `cam` section, not just within its setup: `pocket#2` names one operation in the document, and moving it to another setup keeps its id. Ids are never reused after deletion. They are a namespace separate from part feature ids and from `print`'s, so `r3` in a part and `r3` in `cam` are different references; a CAM id is only meaningful together with the section it lives in. Messages therefore always say it is CAM and name the setup an operation belongs to ("setup#1 / pocket#2").

   **"Profile" means one thing inside `cam`.** `profile` is the CAM operation kind (a contour cut around a shape) and also, in a part feature, the field naming its sketch region (`profile: { sketch, entities? }`). To keep them apart, the CAM geometry source for a sketch region is `kind: 'region'`, so no field inside `cam` is called `profile`; and code, messages and docs say "profile operation" for the cut and "sketch region" for the geometry. The operation keeps the name `profile`, the term the plan's tasks and most CAM documentation use.

5. **Geometry is referenced by name and resolved against the final body.** An operation's geometry is a list of sources (decision 2): a planar face, a sketch region, a hole feature, later edges and silhouettes. A planar face is resolved on the setup's body as it stands after the part's whole feature list, not at any point in its history, with regen's naming and vocabulary (`exact`, `descendant`, `ancestor`, `ends`, `ordinal`, `fragile`, `lost`, `ambiguous`) and the same re-pick UI. A sketch region comes from the sketch's solved geometry at the end of the regen, whether or not any body feature consumes the sketch (the plywood sign's V-carved text is a sketch no feature cuts). A WCS whose up direction names a face resolves the same way.
   - **A hole source gives the drill its through-hole diameter.** A hole feature yields its axis points, its depth and the diameter of the hole itself, for a `simple`, `counterbore` or `countersink` head alike. The head is not drilled: a counterbore is machined by a separate pocket (or, later, a bore) operation whose source is the counterbore's planar floor face, and a countersink is left to a later chamfer operation (M5 has none). One operation never cuts two diameters from one source.
   - **Expected outcomes are values on the operation** (ADR 0007 decision 5): `reference-lost` with the missing names, `reference-ambiguous` with the candidates, and warnings for `via` and `fragile`, as in ADR 0004 decision 6.
   - **Orientation is checked, not assumed.** A face, region or hole whose plane or axis is not parallel to the setup's XY plane (or axis) is an error on the operation, not a silently projected toolpath.
   - Sources resolve into exact lines and arcs where the geometry has them (T5.1e's planar face loops and plane sections, sketch regions), so arcs survive to G2/G3 through the tagged refit of decision 12.

6. **CAM references never block modelling.** Core's rule "a feature comes after everything it depends on" does not reach into `cam`: deleting, suppressing, reordering or editing a feature, sketch or body that an operation or a WCS names is always allowed, and the operation reports `reference-lost` the next time its toolpaths are generated. Core validation checks only that a setup's part id exists and that body ids, feature ids and references are well formed, never that a body, face, sketch or hole still exists, as ADR 0012 decision 2 does for print items. Blocking a feature edit because of a toolpath would invert the order people work in (model first, then machine). A machine or post id the app does not know (a document from a newer build) loads unchanged; the setup reports it and generates nothing for it.

   **Deleting a whole part is the one exception.** A setup without its part has nothing to resolve against and no stock, so `deletePart` refuses while any setup names the part, with a `dependency` error that names the setups, exactly as it does today for print items, assembly instances and drawing views (`packages/core/src/commands.ts`). The user deletes or retargets the setups first.

   Inside `cam` itself, consistency is kept like a feature list keeps its own: an operation's `tool` must name a tool in `cam.tools`, so deleting a tool still in use is refused with the operations named.

7. **Geometry extraction runs in the kernel worker; toolpaths run in a new CAM worker.**
   - **The CAM geometry stage** (T5.1f) is a regen-side stage modelled on the drawing stage. It runs in the kernel worker because extraction needs OCCT and the final body, which live there (ADR 0007 decision 1). It is called on demand (never by a regen), takes the client's current generation and never starts a new one, so it never cancels a regen; it runs on the regen chain, builds the part through the cache, and resolves to null when a newer regen supersedes it. Its reply is plain data per source: loops of lines and arcs, points with diameters, depths, and for `surface3d` the body's mesh, in model coordinates, with the resolved WCS frame, the body's bounds (from which `packages/cam` sizes a stock box from the bounds plus margins), and the reference outcomes of decision 5. Conversion into setup coordinates is `packages/cam`'s (T5.1c).
   - **The stage's cache key** holds the sources, the stage version, the body's `bodyKey` for face sources, the WCS face and `surface3d`, **and the regen result key of every feature a source names**: each sketch a `region` source names, and each hole feature a `hole` source names. The body key alone is not enough: a region source can name a sketch that no body feature consumes (the plywood sign's V-carved text is such a sketch), so editing it changes no body, and a hole's points come from the hole feature, not from the faces it cut. Regen already computes a cache key per feature (ADR 0004 decision 8); T5.1f exposes it on the feature's result, next to `bodyKey` on bodies, so the app can compare keys without asking the stage. An unchanged key sends no kernel op.
   - **The `surface3d` mesh** is tessellated by the stage at its own chordal and angular tolerance, finer than the export tolerance (0.02 mm chordal at `normal`, `packages/io`): by default 0.004 mm and 0.06 rad, the values T5.0b's 99,200-triangle part was measured at. The tolerance is a stage constant in the stage's cache key, so it reaches the toolpath key through the mesh; the mesh's chordal error adds to the finish's error budget, and the docs say so. T5.1f may change the constant with measurements and records it in `packages/regen/README.md`.
   - **The CAM worker** (T5.1g) runs toolpath generation, linking, the simulation and the TypeScript drop-cutter (decision 13), so a slow pocket or a 3D finish never delays a regen, a sketch drag or the printability overlay. It is a **fifth context** for ADR 0007, recorded there as an amendment, and follows that ADR's rules: one Comlink interface, `CamWorkerApi`, defined in `packages/cam`, with entries `@manufakture/cam/worker` and `@manufakture/cam/client`; one coarse call per setup (generate the stale operations of one setup and link them), never one per operation pass; a `generation` on every request, newer ones superseding older ones, with work in chunks that yield between them as in the print-analysis worker; expected failures (a tool too large for a region, an empty result, a cut below the stock) as values per operation; results as transferred typed arrays.
   - **Only plain data travels between the two**, through the main thread: loops, points and depths are small and are copied; meshes for 3D are transferred on both hops, since the main thread has no use for that copy (the viewport draws the regen meshes it already has).
   - **Orchestration lives in `apps/web`**, as print's does in `apps/web/src/print/resolve.ts`: the app evaluates the setup's expressions against the document variables through `packages/units`, asks the regen worker's geometry stage for the sources, computes the cache keys and stale flags (decisions 8 and 9), and calls the CAM worker through `@manufakture/cam/client` with numbers and geometry only. The client itself is a thin worker wrapper (decision 1).
   - **It is not shared with the print-analysis worker.** ADR 0012 decision 5 kept the two apart for different dependencies (a polygon library and a drop-cutter against a pure-TypeScript BVH; OCL's `.wasm`, which ADR 0012 also named, is not adopted in M5), lifetimes and costs, and left this ADR free to propose one shared worker. It does not: a 3D finish must not hold up the printability overlay, nor the reverse. The CAM worker starts lazily, when a CAM workspace or a G-code export first needs it, so a document with no setup never starts it.

8. **CAM is lazy.** A regen never generates toolpaths. The CAM workspace asks for the toolpaths of the setup on screen, and export generates whatever is stale for the setups it writes. After a model edit, an operation is shown as stale when a key its geometry depends on changed: its setup's body key, or the regen result key of a sketch or hole feature it names (decision 7). The app computes that flag from regen's reply, without asking the stage; stale operations regenerate when shown or exported. An edit inside `cam` marks only the operations whose own inputs changed, and linking for their setup.

   **A variable edit** changes no `cam` text, but it can change what an expression in `cam` evaluates to. The app re-evaluates the `cam` expressions after every variable change (cheap: no kernel and no worker involved), and an operation whose evaluated operation, tool or setup differs gets a different toolpath key (decision 9) and is stale. One whose values did not change keeps its key and its toolpaths. A variable that changes the model also changes body or feature keys, which the rule above covers.

9. **Toolpaths are cached like features** (ADR 0004 decision 8). An operation's key hashes:
   - the evaluated operation (every field in internal units);
   - the evaluated tool;
   - the setup's evaluated stock, WCS frame and heights, and the machine row it uses;
   - the **extracted geometry itself** (the stage's reply for its sources), not the body key: an edit elsewhere on the part changes the body key, re-runs the cheap stage, and still hits for an operation whose loops did not change;
   - a CAM implementation version, bumped with any change that can alter output;
   - the polygon library's exact version, and for `surface3d` the drop-cutter's version (the hash of the engine's `.wasm`, should a later milestone adopt one).

   Linking is cached per setup by its operations' keys, in order, and the setup's heights. The first cache lives in memory in the CAM worker; OPFS persistence follows the regen cache's `FeatureCache` shape later. Any entry may be dropped at any time; a miss regenerates, never errors. G-code is not cached: the post runs at export from the cached IR (an estimate that it is cheap next to generation, to be checked in T5.4a).

10. **The post-processor is data plus one engine** (T5.4a). A dialect is a record of what the controller accepts: the G and M codes allowed, the tool change style (none, `M0` pause, `M6 T<n>`), canned cycles or expanded drilling, arc format (IJK only; full circles as one arc or two halves), comment style, the maximum line length (80 characters for Grbl), number formats per word, and header, footer and tool change templates. The engine turns the IR into lines, tracks modal state, and **refuses** any move or code the dialect does not allow rather than writing it.
    - **Nothing is evaluated.** Templates substitute named variables from a fixed list (`{tool}`, `{tool_name}`, `{rpm}`, `{feed}`, ...), and an unknown variable is an error when the dialect is loaded, never text in the output. A user post is data a user may download and share, so no part of it is ever run as code; Kiri:Moto's JavaScript-evaluated macros (M5 plan Part 1) are the pattern not taken.
    - **Every arc passes Grbl's own checks, as Grbl will see it.** Two rules in Grbl 1.1 reject or change an arc (M5 plan Part 1, T5.0a "Tiny arcs"): the **radius check** (error 33, "invalid target": the end point's radius may differ from the start point's by at most 0.005 mm or 0.1% of the radius, and never by more than 0.5 mm), and the **angular travel** `mc_arc` computes from the written X, Y, I and J, which gains a full turn when start and end coincide within 5e-7 rad, so a tiny arc becomes a full circle. T5.0a's first refit wrote such an arc (`G2 X36.494 Y-6.533 I-2.832 J-0.991`, start equal to end), which Grbl would cut as a 6 mm circle.
    - **The check runs in Grbl's frame.** The engine checks every arc **after** it has been put into the coordinates the file is written in (the setup's work offset applied, converted to inches under G20) and rounded to the dialect's decimals, since that rounding is the one Grbl sees, and emulates Grbl's single-precision arithmetic as the spike's `grblArcCheck` does.
    - **What becomes a line.** The engine writes an arc as a `G1` to its end point when the rounded start and end coincide, when its chord is under ten steps of the dialect's output resolution (0.01 mm at 3 decimals), when its sagitta is at most a twentieth of the refit tolerance (tol / 20: 0.0001 mm at 0.002 mm, so the line stays within 0.0001 mm of the arc), or when it fails either Grbl check (or its travel is more than 0.5 rad off the intended sweep). Only an arc the IR marks as an intended full circle (a helical bore, a circular pocket ring) goes out as one IJK arc with equal start and end, or as two half arcs where the dialect asks for that. The thresholds are engine constants, documented in `packages/cam/README.md`, and the verifier of T5.4d checks that no unintended full circle and no error 33 survives.
    - **Comments are safe by construction.** The engine writes only parenthesised ASCII comments, strips nested parentheses and never writes `;`.
    - **GRBL first** (T5.4b): G21 and millimetres by default (G20 as an option, through `packages/units`), IJK arcs, no `M6` (Grbl 1.1 rejects it with error 20), drilling expanded to G0/G1 moves, every line under 80 characters, and for several tools one file per tool by default (or one file with an `M0` pause and a comment per change). Its output is the Grbl 1.1 subset that grblHAL also accepts. Carbide Motion, grblHAL, LinuxCNC and Mach3 posts follow as more dialect records (T5.4c). Every golden G-code file is checked by a parser we did not write (T5.4d).

11. **Target machines and tools are data with permanent ids**, kept like ADR 0012's printer table (`packages/cam`, T5.1d): ids are never renamed or reused, each row cites its source, the table grows without a format change, and a changed row cites its new source in review.
    - **Two machines in M5**, in their **default configurations** as sold (no upgrades or accessories assumed): `shapeoko-5-pro-4x4`, the **default** for a new setup, and `shapeoko-4-xxl`. A row holds the work area and travel the bounds checks use, the firmware family, and the default post.
    - **The default post is `grbl`** for both until T5.4c lands other posts; changing a machine's default post then is a data change.
    - **Tools are copied into the document** from a library (`source` records where from), so a document carries everything its toolpaths need and a library update never changes an existing job silently.

12. **The polygon library is clipper2-ts 2.0.1-18** (BSL-1.0), pinned exactly, behind an adapter in `packages/cam` (`src/offset/`, T5.2a) that owns scaling, tagging and range checks. From the [T5.0a spike](../spikes/T5.0a-clipper2.md):
    - **Why clipper2-ts over clipper2-wasm 0.4.0.** Both return the same vertices on every sketch case. clipper2-wasm is 1.1 to 3.4 times faster, but a 50-ring pocket takes 0.26 s in clipper2-ts, which the CAM worker and cache absorb. clipper2-ts has a Z callback (which the tagged refit needs at offset intersections; clipper2-wasm cannot fill those tags), synchronous calls on plain objects with nothing to free and nothing to load, and its own license file (clipper2-wasm ships none). The adapter keeps clipper2-wasm's `InflatePathsD` as a drop-in for offsets that need no Z callback if profiling ever shows offsets dominating.
    - **Integer scale: 1e4 units per mm** (0.1 micrometre), one constant. Rounding then adds about 0.00013 mm to the offset error, and 2 m is 2e7 units.
    - **The adapter range-checks coordinates.** clipper2-ts does not check `Path64` input (the spike got a silent result at 2e16), so the adapter rejects any coordinate whose magnitude exceeds 4.7e7 units (4.7 m), which also keeps every computation on clipper2-ts's fast `number` paths.
    - **Tolerances in one place**: arcs flattened at **0.001 mm** with vertices on the arc; round joins with an explicit **`arcTolerance` of 0.001 mm** (the default is `|delta| * 0.002`, 0.006 mm for a 6 mm tool); arc refit at **0.002 mm**. The spike measured 0.0011 to 0.0012 mm against the exact offset before refit, and after refit at most 0.0018 mm on the sketch cases and 0.0020 mm on every input (polygon outlines included), which is the budget this ADR states: **0.0020 mm after refit**.
    - **Z-tagged arc refit.** Every flattened input vertex carries a Z tag back to its source curve (sharp corners tagged separately), the Z callback tags intersections, and runs on one tagged circle become one arc on the exact circle; an untagged fit at 0.002 mm handles the rest and tagless input. At 0.001 mm flattening this cut segments 27 to 131 times on the sketch cases, and the largest radius difference after rounding to 3 decimals was 0.0008 mm (tagged) and 0.0015 mm (untagged), against Grbl's 0.005 mm radius check (error 33, decision 10). The post still runs that check as written (decision 10).
    - **The refit emits no degenerate arcs.** It caps every arc's sweep at half a turn (spike recommendation 6), which also keeps IJK arcs well conditioned. As its last step it applies the spike's rule: an arc becomes a line with the same ends when its sagitta is at most tol / 20 (0.0001 mm at the 0.002 mm refit tolerance), or when it fails decision 10's Grbl checks (the radius rule and the angular travel) at 3 decimals, the default output precision. In the spike that turned 65 arcs into lines, all on the 10,000-vertex polygons, kept every refit within 0.0020 mm of its polyline, and left no arc failing either check. A full circle in the IR is only ever an explicit one (decision 10), never a refit result. The post repeats decision 10's checks in Grbl's frame with its own decimals, since its rounding (work offset, inches, 3 or 4 decimals) is the one Grbl sees.
    - **Pocket rings are offset from the source**, never from the previous ring: offsetting each ring from the last gave 22 times the vertices, 41 times the time and a growing error.

13. **3D surfacing uses our own TypeScript drop-cutter; OpenCAMLib is not adopted.** The project owner decided to follow the [T5.0b spike](../spikes/T5.0b-opencamlib.md), and it recommends this.
    - **The drop-cutter** moves from `spikes/opencamlib/src/dropcutter.ts` into `packages/cam` with its tests (closed-form cases and the brute-force lower bound), under our own license and with no dependencies (T5.5a). It handles flat, ball and V cutters with exact vertex, facet and edge tests over a uniform grid index; T5.5a adds bull-nose cutters if the tool library has them, adaptive sampling, and the skip for triangles wholly below the current height. Reasons, from the spike: it agrees with OCL to 2.3e-12 mm for flat and ball cutters on 892,104 points; OCL's V cutter gouges by up to 0.108 mm where ours does not; ours is 2.5 to 6.9 times faster than the single-threaded OCL build; and it holds about 14 MB for the 99,200-triangle part where OCL needed 131 MiB of wasm memory.
    - **It runs in the CAM worker** on the mesh from the geometry stage (decision 7). A finish of the spike's large part took 2.2 to 6.7 s, so the worker reports progress and is cancellable between raster lines (ADR 0007 decision 4, as amended).
    - **Waterline is out of M5's scope.** Z-level roughing comes from plane sections and the pocket operation (T5.1e, T5.2c), and parallel finishing covers the plywood sign and a filleted block. A later milestone that needs steep-wall finishing decides between a TypeScript waterline on the same cutter geometry and OCL.
    - **ADR 0006 is not amended in M5**: nothing new ships, so no LGPL-3.0 is needed.
    - **Rules for a later milestone that adopts OCL** (for waterline, say), recorded here so they need not be re-decided: OCL ships **only** as a separate, replaceable `.wasm` plus ES module glue, built from a pinned upstream commit by a recipe in this repository (the spike's `spikes/opencamlib/build/` is where it starts), with that recipe and any C++ or bindings we add published under this repository's license (ADR 0002's rules for wasm we build, ADR 0006 decisions 4 and 5). It is loaded in the CAM worker only, lazily, and no OCL object or pointer crosses the worker boundary; embind objects are deleted as with OCCT, and an abort recycles the instance. The published `@opencamlib/opencamlib` browser bundle, which inlines its wasm into JavaScript, is never used. Because the stricter reading of OCL's license is LGPL-3.0, which ADR 0006 decision 2 does not admit, adopting it **requires an ADR 0006 amendment adding LGPL-3.0 to decision 2**, under the same separate-module condition, plus inventory rows, merged before or with the task that ships it; the spike drafts the wording. OCL's `ConeCutter` is not used without a fix to its edge test.
    - **The engine stays swappable.** Surfacing sits behind one interface in `packages/cam`, so such a later engine plugs in without touching the operations, and the toolpath key names the engine (decision 9).

14. **One format bump for `cam`** (T5.1b): the next free `FORMAT_VERSION` when it lands, with a migration that adds an empty `cam` and a fixture of the version before it (ADR 0004 decision 2). Nothing here assumes a number; it serialises with every other milestone's schema tasks. Adding rows to the machine, tool or post tables never bumps the format, since they are data in `packages/cam`, not in the document; any later change to the shape of `cam` (a new field, a new operation kind such as `surface3d`'s fields in T5.5a) takes its own bump, per ADR 0004 decision 2.

## Alternatives considered

- **CAM data under ADR 0013's generic `domains` field.** It would save a format bump. But `cam` holds face references, feature and body ids and expressions with variables, which ADR 0013 decision 3 forbids in `domains` and which core must reach. Rejected, as ADR 0013 decision 9 anticipates.
- **CAM operations as part features, or as `extension` features.** They would dirty regens, sit at a point in a part's history instead of acting on the final body, block deletion of what they name, and give regen work it cannot do (an extension must translate into kernel inputs). Rejected (decision 2).
- **CAM state per part** (`part.cam`). A setup's stock, machine and tool list are not a part's, and a later setup may hold several parts on one sheet. Rejected for a document-level section, as `print` is.
- **Blocking a model edit that would lose a CAM reference.** Inverts the order users think in. Rejected, as in ADR 0012 decision 2.
- **Extraction in the CAM worker with its own OCCT instance.** A second kernel's memory and load time, and a second copy of the naming layer that would have to agree with regen's. Rejected for a stage next to the kernel that already holds the body.
- **Toolpaths in the kernel worker.** A pocket or a 3D finish would queue behind regens and recycles and delay them. Rejected.
- **One worker for print analysis and CAM.** Rejected (decision 7); ADR 0012 decision 5 stands.
- **Keying toolpaths on the body key alone.** Every model edit would regenerate every operation of the part, including those whose loops did not change. Rejected for a key on the extracted geometry.
- **Storing toolpaths or G-code in the document.** Derived data (ADR 0004 decision 1); they would go stale with every edit. Rejected.
- **clipper2-wasm 0.4.0.** Faster, but no Z callback for intersections, embind objects to delete by hand (forgetting one result grew the heap by 29 MB over 1,000 calls in the spike), a wrong `types` path and no license file. Kept as a fallback behind the adapter. Rejected as the default.
- **Offsetting each pocket ring from the previous one, and untagged arc refit only.** The first multiplies vertices and error; the second collapses at coarse flattening and places arcs only as well as the polyline. Rejected (decision 12).
- **Post macros evaluated as JavaScript**, as Kiri:Moto does. A shared post file would be code run in the user's browser. Rejected for named variables only.
- **Renaming the `profile` operation** (to "contour" or "outline"). Removes the word clash entirely, but renames a term the plan's tasks and much CAM documentation use. Not adopted; the clash is removed inside `cam` by naming the source `region` instead (decision 4).
- **OpenCAMLib built by us as a separate `.wasm`, for 3D surfacing in M5.** It builds with a current Emscripten in seconds and its waterline works (T5.0b), but it would need an ADR 0006 amendment for LGPL-3.0, its single-threaded build is 2.5 to 6.9 times slower than our drop-cutter with several times the memory, and its V cutter gouges. Rejected for M5; decision 13 keeps the rules for a later milestone that wants its waterline.
- **OpenCAMLib's published npm browser build.** One 8.6 MB bundle with the wasm inlined into JavaScript, which would break ADR 0006 decision 4. Rejected in every case, in M5 and later.

## Consequences

- T5.1b (schema), T5.1c (package), T5.1d (machines and tools), T5.1f (geometry stage), T5.1g (CAM worker), T5.2a to T5.2g (offsets, operations, linking), T5.4a to T5.4e (posts and verification) and T5.5a (surfacing) cite this ADR.
- The document gains `cam` (T5.1b, one format bump). The persistence code must carry the new top-level key; T5.1b checks the persistence README for key filters, as T3.1a did for `print`. `diffDocuments` gains a boolean `camChanged`. `deletePart` gains a check that refuses while a setup names the part, next to its print, instance and drawing view checks (decision 6).
- A CAM edit never triggers a regen. Feature edits and deletions never fail because of CAM; the CAM workspace shows `reference-lost` operations with a re-pick instead. Deleting a part is refused while a setup uses it, naming the setups.
- Regen exposes a per-feature result key (T5.1f), so the app can tell when a sketch or hole feature a CAM source names has changed even though no body did (decisions 7 and 8).
- **ADR 0007** gains a fifth context, the CAM worker, and the geometry stage as a call on the kernel worker. That is recorded as an amendment in ADR 0007 itself, with its index line updated, by this task (T5.0c); T5.1g fills in the details in `packages/cam/README.md`.
- **ADR 0006**: T5.2a adds an inventory row for clipper2-ts 2.0.1-18, BSL-1.0, verified from its installed `package.json` and `LICENSE` when it is added to `packages/cam` (the spike verified the copy in `spikes/clipper2/node_modules`, which is not a shipped dependency). BSL-1.0 is already allowed (decision 2's permissive licenses; Boost inside planegcs is in the inventory), so no amendment is needed for it.
- **ADR 0006 gets no LGPL-3.0 amendment in M5**, since T5.0b recommended against OCL (decision 13), and the drop-cutter adds no dependency. ADR 0006's OFL amendment ends with a note that M5's LGPL-3.0 amendment is independent of the OFL one and that "whichever lands second extends the other"; in M5 there is no second amendment, so that note waits for a later milestone that adopts OCL. A later edit to ADR 0006 may say so; it is outside this task's footprint.
- **ADR 0012** is unchanged. Its decision 5 (the print and CAM workers kept apart) is confirmed; it names OpenCAMLib's `.wasm` as a possible CAM worker dependency, which in M5 it is not, since the CAM worker is pure TypeScript. **ADR 0013** is unchanged; `cam` is one of the typed sections its decision 9 names.
- The machine table must be revisited when Carbide 3D changes a machine's specifications; each row says where its numbers came from.
- Polygon offsets are accurate to 0.0020 mm after refit (decision 12), a budget set by the tolerances of decision 12; changing any of them, or the scale, is a change of the CAM implementation version and so a cache miss for every toolpath.
