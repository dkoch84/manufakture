## Summary

- **The fixtures are close to the plan's estimates.** The scripted shed has 147 members (Part 1: "on the order of 150"), the house 834 ("800 to 1,000"). 46 and 214 of them have cuts (rafters, gable studs, ties, ceiling joists); 26 and 138 have a birdsmouth.
- **A B-rep body per member (A) is too slow and too heavy for the house.** Through the real kernel service (a named `extrude` per member, a `tools` feature for its cuts, `tessellate` and `topology`), a cold regen of the house takes 5,483 ms after a 488 ms instance start, transfers 4.83 MiB of meshes and topology, and leaves about 42 MiB of wasm heap behind (T0.2 leak probe), so the 1 GiB recycle comes after 22 full regens. Moving one opening rebuilds its wall in 200 ms. The shed is fine (1,184 ms cold, 67 ms warm), but a two-storey house would take three times the house's numbers (estimate).
- **Member data with meshes computed in TypeScript (B) is about 400 times faster.** Generating the house's members, meshing its 108 distinct shapes and building the instance lists takes 12.9 ms cold in a fresh Node process; a warm regen after moving one opening takes 0.66 ms. The meshes of the whole house are 129 KiB, the transforms 64 bytes per member.
- **Manifold for the cut members (C) adds 39 ms cold and nothing warm.** Loading manifold-3d 3.5.4 takes 7.3 ms; meshing the house's 66 distinct cut shapes 32 ms (0.20 ms a shape against 0.0072 ms for our own convex planar clip). Its meshes are closed shells; ours are two convex pieces per notch with a shared inner face, fine on screen but not exportable as STL or 3MF without welding. All three meshers (kernel, own clip, Manifold) give the same volume for every cut shape, to 2e-8.
- **Manifold's `delete()` frees memory.** Meshing the house's cut shapes 10 and 60 times on a fresh module left 0.13 MiB in use both times (22,560 objects created and deleted); the control without `delete()` left 14.8 and 88.2 MiB.
- **Instancing cuts the draw calls by seven.** In headless Chromium with SwiftShader (software rendering) at 1280 x 800, the house drawn as an object (and an edge object) per member takes 996 draw calls and 34.1 ms a frame; `InstancedMesh` per role and shape 137 calls and 20.4 ms; `BatchedMesh` 15 calls and 20.6 ms. SwiftShader is bound by fill, not by calls, so the gap on a GPU depends on per-call cost (estimate below); edges are a quarter of the frame (15.5 ms without them).
- **Per-instance picking works.** A `pickId` instance attribute through the app's own pick shader includes picked the same member as three.js raycasting at 200 of 200 pixels on each fixture, in 0.7 ms (shed) and 2.8 ms (house) per pick in SwiftShader.
- **LOD is not needed at house scale.** Hiding blocking and cripples removes 85 members, 7 draw calls and 1,020 triangles and changes the frame time within noise (20.1 against 20.4 ms); merging each wall, floor and roof into one mesh gives 28 draw calls and 19.1 ms but more than doubles the GPU geometry (992 against 426 KiB). Turning edges off is the only large saving (24 %).
- **Member B-reps on demand are affordable as a batch.** All 834 house members as B-rep bodies take 4,072 ms, and one STEP file of them 825 ms more (16.2 MiB).

Recommendation: **B, with Manifold (C) for members that have cuts.** Members are data (decision 4, with the refinements below), meshed in the regen worker (boxes in TypeScript, cut members through Manifold), one mesh per distinct shape, drawn with `InstancedMesh` and picked through a per-instance id. B-reps of members are built only on demand, in batches. **Manifold is adopted** (Apache-2.0, re-read from the installed 3.5.4 package), loaded lazily in the regen worker. The heap probe now lives in `packages/kernel` (`heapInUse`, `@manufakture/kernel/testing`) for T6.5d. Details under [Recommendations](#recommendations).

**Fixtures.** Built by script in inches, converted to millimetres:

- **Shed**, 12' x 16': three 4x6 skids, 2x6 floor joists at 16" between two rims, 2x4 walls at 16" (precut 92-5/8" studs, one bottom plate, two top plates), a 36" door (rough opening 38" x 82-1/2") in the front gable wall, a 24" x 36" window in each long wall, a 6/12 gable roof of 2x6 rafters at 16" on a 2x8 ridge with 12" overhangs, rafter ties at every third pair, gable studs cut to the roof.
- **House**, 40' x 50' (2,000 sq ft) over a crawl space: 2x6 sill plates, a 3-ply 2x10 girder, 2x10 joists at 16" in two spans with a row of blocking over the girder; 2x6 exterior walls, a 2x4 bearing wall down the middle and six 2x4 partitions; two doors and eight windows (ten openings); 2x6 ceiling joists in two spans, cut to the roof; a 6/12 hip roof with 2x8 commons and jacks at 16", four 2x10 hips and a 2x10 ridge.

The generator does what framers do (Part 1, "Framing rules of thumb"): layout slots on 16" centres, slot 0 flush with the wall's start; kings, jacks, a header (2x6 to 36", 2x8 to 60", 2x10 to 96", two plies in a 2x4 wall, three in a 2x6 wall), a rough sill and cripples on the layout; a corner nailer at each outside corner; two studs where a wall tees in, unless a layout stud already stands there; plates spliced at 16' with the two top plates' splices 96" apart; the bottom plate stopped at doors. Rafters get a tail plumb cut, a birdsmouth (seat on the plate, heel at the wall's outside face) and their top cut: a plumb cut at the ridge for commons, a vertical cut along the hip's side face for jacks, a plumb cut at the ridge end for hips. It has no overrides and no warnings, and is not T6.2a's generator. A test checks that no two uncut members overlap; an earlier version put tee studs on top of layout studs, which showed up as picks that disagreed between the GPU and the raycaster (z-fighting), so T6.2a needs the same check.

**Representations.**

- **A**: each member a body through `KernelService`, as regen builds M1 bodies: an `extrude` feature of its cross-section along its length (named, with history), a `tools` feature (T4.2a) with one box tool per cut (a plane cut is a large box on the removed side; a birdsmouth, whose heel and seat are perpendicular, is one box in the corner they make), then `tessellate` and `topology` of the result, one batch per group. The mesh, topology and name table are copied with `structuredClone` and transfer, as the worker reply would. Old bodies of a group are released before it is rebuilt.
- **B**: members as data; uncut members are 12-triangle boxes, cut members are clipped as convex polyhedra by their plane cuts, and a notch splits a member into two convex pieces (outside the heel, and inside the heel but above the seat). One mesh per distinct shape key, an instance matrix per member.
- **C**: as B, but cut members are meshed by Manifold: `Manifold.cube`, `trimByPlane` per plane cut, and `subtract` of the notch wedge (the box trimmed by both notch planes); every object deleted after `getMesh`.

**Cold** regens run in a fresh Node process: A's includes creating the kernel service on a fresh instance (reported apart as "init"), C's includes loading Manifold. **Warm** regens move the window `opening#2` in the south wall by 12" and back, alternately, and rebuild that wall only. **Memory** of A uses the T0.2 leak probe (below); of B and C, the JS heap's growth and the mesh and matrix bytes. **Viewport** numbers come from a page built with Vite and loaded by Playwright through a route (no port), each case in a fresh page, 60 frames orbiting the building, each frame followed by a one-pixel `readPixels` so the time includes the rasterizer's work.

What the numbers say:

- **A's cost is the kernel, per member: about 6.5 ms each** (5,483 ms for 834), against the Part 1 probe's 0.64 ms for a bare box and mesh. ### Memory

**A: the T0.2 leak probe.** Run N full regens (build, mesh, release every body) on a fresh instance in a fresh process, then ask the allocator once how much it can still hand out in 64 KiB blocks before the memory must grow; bytes in use are the memory's size less that. The probe is now `heapInUse` in `packages/kernel` (see Recommendations).

| Fixture | N       | In use MiB    | Per full regen | Per member and regen |
| ------- | ------- | ------------- | -------------- | -------------------- |
| Shed    | 10 / 60 | 102.1 / 515.8 | 8.27 MiB       | about 58 KiB         |
| House   | 5 / 20  | 255.5 / 886.2 | 42.05 MiB      | about 52 KiB         |

The house runs N = 5 and 20, not 10 and 60: 60 full regens at about 42 MiB each would pass 2.5 GiB without recycling. The service's own recycling, left on, recycled after **22 full regens of the house** (heap 1,132 MiB at the recycle, since the memory grows in steps), in 350 ms (a new instance from the cached module; the document replay is not included). Released bodies keep most of their B-rep in libcascade's empty destructors (ADR 0002); a member is a small solid, but there are hundreds.

Calibrating the probe while porting it: on a fresh instance, leaking a known 8 MiB in pieces of 48 bytes to 16 KiB read as 0.70 to 0.85 of it with 64 KiB blocks, and 0.97 to 1.10 with 4 KiB blocks; a single large block goes through the allocator's mmap path and is seen even less exactly. The probe is linear in the leak (4, 8, 16 and 32 MiB of 1 KiB pieces read 3.4, 6.8, 13.6 and 27.1 MiB above the baseline), so comparisons of two N are sound, and the per-regen figures above are, if anything, low by up to 30 %. `heap-probe.test.ts` keeps the calibration as a test.

**B and C**: the member set holds 129 KiB (B) or 153 KiB (C) of meshes for the house and 64 bytes per member of matrices (53 KiB); the JS heap grew 2.6 MiB (B) and 3.8 MiB (C) over a cold regen, the members' data included. Nothing is kept in a wasm heap except Manifold's own, which stays at its initial 16 MiB:

| Manifold, house cut shapes meshed N times | Objects created | Deleted | Module memory | In use (probe) |
| ----------------------------------------- | --------------- | ------- | ------------- | -------------- |
| N = 10                                    | 3,760           | 3,760   | 16 MiB        | 0.13 MiB       |
| N = 60                                    | 22,560          | 22,560  | 16 MiB        | 0.13 MiB       |
| N = 10, no `delete()` (control)           | 3,760           | 0       | 16 MiB        | 14.81 MiB      |
| N = 60, no `delete()` (control)           | 22,560          | 0       | 99.7 MiB      | 88.19 MiB      |

So Manifold's destructors free what they own (unlike libcascade's), and the probe sees a leak there when there is one (1.47 MiB per run in the control). `delete()` is required: every `Manifold` and every intermediate (`trimByPlane` results, the wedge) must be deleted.

What the numbers say:

- **SwiftShader is bound by fill and lines, not by calls.** Going from 996 to 137 draw calls saved 13.7 ms a frame (about 16 microseconds a call in ANGLE and three.js on this CPU), and from 137 to 15 (`BatchedMesh`) saved nothing measurable. On a GPU the per-call cost is CPU time in three.js and the driver, typically 10 to 30 microseconds (estimate, not measured here): 1,000 calls alone would take 10 to 30 ms a frame and miss 60 fps, while 137 take 1.4 to 4 ms. A two-storey house has two to three times the members but not many more shapes (estimate: under 300 lists).

- **Representation C draws exactly like B:** the same lists, 552 fewer triangles for the house.

### Picking

The pick pass renders every `InstancedMesh` with the app's pick material approach (`apps/web/src/viewport/materials.ts`): a float `pickId` attribute, here an `InstancedBufferAttribute` (one id per instance, 24 bits), through `#include <begin_vertex>` and `#include <project_vertex>`, which apply `instanceMatrix` for instanced meshes, so the existing shader works unchanged. One pixel is rendered into a 1 x 1 target with `setViewOffset` and decoded to `<group>:<member id>`. Checked against three.js raycasting through the same pixel's centre at the projected centres of 200 random members:

| Fixture | Picks | Hit a member | Same member as the raycast | GPU pick (SwiftShader) | Raycast |
| ------- | ----- | ------------ | -------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------- |
| Shed    | 200   | 200          | 200                        | 0.7 ms                 | 0.0 ms  |
| House   | 200   | 200          | 200                        | 2.8 ms                 | 0.1 ms  |

Two causes of disagreement were found on the way, both fixed: casting the ray through a pixel's corner instead of its centre (with it, 20 of 200 house picks differed, most of them naming the neighbouring rafter), and members generated on top of each other (tee studs on layout studs, which z-fight in the pick pass; the last 4 differences). Raycasting instanced meshes is fast enough to be the fallback where the pick pass is unavailable.

## Recommendations

### 1. Representation: B, with Manifold for cut members

A is not affordable for the house: 5.5 s cold before any layer or opening body, 4.8 MiB transferred per regen, a recycle every 22 full regens, and 1,000 draw calls as the viewport draws bodies today. The plan's hypothesis holds, and T6.5b and T6.5c keep their scope. Members are data from the framing generator; the regen worker meshes them (boxes in TypeScript, members with cuts through Manifold), keeps one mesh per distinct shape, and sends instance lists; the viewport draws `InstancedMesh`es and picks per instance. Wall layers stay B-rep bodies (decision 7).

### 2. The member data shape (decision 4, as measured)

```ts
interface Member {
  id: string; // stable within its feature: `s12`, `top1:2`, `extension#7:king-l` (decision 5)
  role: Role; // stud, plate, king, jack, header, sill, cripple, blocking, joist, rim, rafter, hip, ...
  stock: StockRef; // catalog entry: nominal name, dressed width (thin face) and depth
  length: number; // blank length along local x, mm (what is cut from stock)
  placement: { origin: Vec3; x: Vec3; y: Vec3 }; // right-handed: x along the length, y across the thin face, z = x cross y
  cuts: Cut[]; // in the member's own frame
  group: string; // the feature id (wall, floor, roof)
}
type Cut =
  | { kind: 'plane'; n: Vec3; k: number } // removes dot(n, p) >= k: plumb, seat, bevel, side, cheek cuts
  | { kind: 'notch'; a: Plane; b: Plane }; // removes the intersection of two such half-spaces: a birdsmouth
```

Refinements to decision 4: **cuts are stored in the member's local frame**, so two members with the same stock, length and cuts are the same shape whatever their placement; the **placement is a rotation and a translation only** (no mirroring: a mirrored member is a different shape with its own key, since a negative-determinant instance matrix flips the triangles' winding); **`length` is the blank**, and the cut length a takeoff may want is derived; a **birdsmouth is one `notch`**, as a framer marks it. A generator must drop a cut that removes nothing (the kernel's `tools` feature rejects a tool that does not touch its body, and our gable studs first got one) and must never emit two members in the same space (the spike's test checks uncut members; T6.2a should check all).

### 3. Mesh sharing rules

- **Key**: stock name, blank length rounded to 0.001 mm, and the sorted list of local cuts with normals rounded to 1e-5 and offsets to 0.001 mm. Placement is never part of the key. Measured: 147 members to 33 shapes (shed), 834 to 108 (house).
- **One cache per regen worker**, shared by every group and kept across regens: a regen meshes only keys the cache lacks, and prunes keys no member uses at its end. A mesh crosses to the main thread once, when its key is new; a group's regen sends its instance list (ids, roles, a `Float32Array` of matrices, transferred).
- **Draw lists**: an `InstancedMesh` per (role, shape) (137 for the house), or per shape if T6.5c colours by an instance attribute instead of a material per role. `BatchedMesh` measured the same frame time with 15 calls and is the fallback if a GPU measurement shows the draw calls matter; its picking was not measured.
- **Edges**: per group, one `LineSegments` in world coordinates from the shapes' `EdgesGeometry` under the members' matrices, rebuilt with the group.

### 4. Budgets for T6.5d (revised)

The spike measured the framing share of a regen, not the whole construction regen (layer bodies are B-rep bodies of their own, not built here). The budgets therefore split:

| Budget                                                                                               | Shed         | House        | Basis                                                                        |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------ | ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Framing, cold (generate, mesh with Manifold load, instance lists), in the regen worker               | under 50 ms  | under 150 ms | measured (C): 22 ms, 52 ms                                                   |
| Framing, warm after moving one opening                                                               | under 10 ms  | under 10 ms  | measured (C): 0.14 ms, 0.63 ms                                               |
| Whole regen, cold (framing, layer bodies, transfer, viewport update; kernel instance start excluded) | under 1 s    | under 3 s    | the plan's 1 s and 5 s; the house's lowered, since framing leaves the kernel |
| Whole regen, warm after moving one opening                                                           | under 100 ms | under 150 ms | the plan's 100 ms and 300 ms; the house's lowered                            |
| Draw calls with members shown                                                                        | under 100    | under 250    | measured (B, C): 46, 137, plus layer bodies                                  |
| Frame time, SwiftShader, 1280 x 800 (a CI regression check, not a performance target)                | under 15 ms  | under 30 ms  | measured (C): 9.4 ms, 21.0 ms                                                |
| Frame rate on a mid-range laptop GPU, house                                                          |              | 60 fps       | the plan's; not measurable headless                                          |

The house's whole-regen budgets assume its 13 walls' sheathing and drywall (about 26 bodies, cut by 10 openings) cost on the order of 1 s cold and 100 ms for one wall (estimate from A's 2.4 to 6.5 ms per simple body; T6.5d measures it).

### 5. Memory rules for T6.5b

1. **Members never enter the kernel's arena in a regen.** A B-rep per member leaves about 50 KiB of wasm heap per member and regen (8.3 MiB a shed regen, 42 MiB a house regen).
2. **Member sets are plain data in the regen worker.** Matrices as one `Float32Array` per list (64 bytes a member), transferred; meshes by key, sent once; the cache pruned at the end of each regen (the house's whole cache is 153 KiB).
3. **Manifold**: one module per regen worker, loaded on the first member with a cut; every `Manifold` (intermediates included) deleted in a `finally` right after `getMesh`. Its memory does not grow with use when everything is deleted (16 MiB, 0.13 MiB in use after 22,560 objects), so it needs no recycling; it never shrinks either, so the regen worker carries those 16 MiB once loaded.
4. **On-demand member B-reps** (STEP export of framing, drawings that need hidden lines through members) are built in batches per group, used, and released at once, with cancellation between groups. A whole-house build leaves about 40 MiB behind; run it through the normal recycle check and never keep member B-reps alive between requests.
5. **T6.5d measures memory with `heapInUse`** (`@manufakture/kernel/testing`), N = 10 and 60 on fresh processes for anything that stays under 2 GiB at N = 60, otherwise two smaller N, and reports per-regen growth with the calibration above.

### 6. LOD

Not needed for a single-storey house: hiding blocking and cripples saved 0.3 ms of 20, merging groups 1.3 ms at twice the geometry. T6.5c should implement no LOD at first, and add **edges off beyond a distance** (24 % of the SwiftShader frame) if the GPU check asks for it. Role hiding and merged far meshes stay options for multi-storey buildings, measured again in T6.5d.

### 7. Manifold is adopted

- **License**: `"license": "Apache-2.0"` in the installed `manifold-3d` 3.5.4 `package.json` and the Apache License 2.0 text in its `LICENSE`, read from `spikes/framing/node_modules/manifold-3d` on 2026-10-03 (ADR 0006 decision 6). Apache-2.0 is allowed by ADR 0006 decision 2. The task that adds it to a shipped package (T6.5b) updates ADR 0006's inventory row ("manifold, none yet, unverified") and the notices.
- **Size and loading**: `manifold.wasm` 541,470 bytes and `manifold.js` 82,371 (the 3.5.4 files as installed; Part 1 measured 158,724 bytes brotli for the `.wasm`). It is loaded lazily inside the regen worker, as its own asset, so a document without cut members never downloads it. Loading took 7.3 ms in Node.
- **Install footprint**: the package declares runtime dependencies for its ManifoldCAD tooling (`@gltf-transform/*`, `commander`, `fast-xml-parser`, `@jscadui/3mf-export`, and, through `@gltf-transform/functions`, `sharp` with its prebuilt platform packages and `esbuild-wasm`), so adding it grew `pnpm-lock.yaml` by 554 lines. None of it is imported by `manifold.js` (it imports only Node built-ins inside Emscripten's environment checks), so nothing of it is bundled into the app. T6.5b pins the version exactly and decides whether that install footprint is acceptable or whether to vendor the two files with their license.
- **API used**: `Manifold.cube`, `trimByPlane`, `subtract`, `getMesh`, `delete`. `getMesh()` returns an indexed mesh; the spike expands it to flat-shaded triangles (one normal per triangle).

### 8. Where the leak probe lives

In **`packages/kernel`**, as a test utility: `packages/kernel/src/heap-probe.ts` exports `heapInUse(allocator, block?)`, `occtAllocator(oc)` and the `WasmAllocator` type through `@manufakture/kernel/testing`. It takes any Emscripten module's memory and allocator, so it measured Manifold here as well. `leaks.test.ts` now uses it instead of its private copy, and `heap-probe.test.ts` keeps the calibration. T6.5d and later benchmarks import it from there.

## Reproducing

From the repository root, after adding the spike to the workspace and running `pnpm install` (see [spikes/README.md](../../spikes/README.md)):

```bash
pnpm --filter @manufakture/spike-framing test       # the checks, about 3 s
pnpm --filter @manufakture/spike-framing measure    # every table in this report, about 15 min
```

The viewport part needs a Chromium for Playwright (`pnpm exec playwright install chromium`). In a container without Chromium's shared libraries, set `PLAYWRIGHT_BROWSER_LD_LIBRARY_PATH` to an unpacked copy and `FONTCONFIG_FILE` to a `fonts.conf`, as for the app's end-to-end tests. `ONLY=node`, `ONLY=kernel` or `ONLY=browser` runs one part. The spike's [README](../../spikes/framing/README.md) has the details.
