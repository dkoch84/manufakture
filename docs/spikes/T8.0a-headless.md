## Summary

- **The session model works on real documents as planned.** A prototype session (`src/session.ts`) reads Main's head from a library on disk, records it as a version, branches from it, locks the branch, loads it into a core `DocumentStore`, regenerates on `createNodeService` and the planegcs solver, and then applies batches, each one store command, one regen, one measurement of the bodies that changed and one saved revision with one log entry. The M1 bracket (with an engraved text in the bundled Inter Bold), the M4 bookshelf and the M6 shed all open, take their batches, save, close, and reopen from disk with a new library to exactly the same regen (mesh hashes, name tables, measurements). Main stays at revision 1 throughout.
- **Cold start is about one second, two thirds of it fixed cost.** In a fresh process: loading the code 0.4 s (under Vitest's on-the-fly transform; 1.2 s for the first file of a run, an upper bound for a bundled server), the kernel service 0.46 to 0.48 s (compiling the `.wasm` and the first 128 MiB instance), opening the session with its first full regen 0.17 s (bracket), 0.52 s (bookshelf) and 0.19 s (shed), of which the library's part (read Main, version, branch, lock, read the branch) is 8 to 23 ms.
- **Incremental regen per batch, median:** 152 ms for a wall thickness on the engraved bracket (everything rebuilds, the text cut most of it), 298 ms for the bookshelf's width (26 kernel features and 8 sketches rebuild), 36 ms for moving a shed window. A rename or 500 new variables regenerate in 1.5 to 4 ms from the cache. Saving a revision takes 1.6 to 6 ms.
- **Over 1,000 batches the kernel recycled 2 times on the bracket, 5 on the bookshelf, never on the shed**, at the 512 MiB threshold: its heap grows by about 1.0, 2.3 and 0.2 MiB per batch. Every recycle landed right after a regen, so the measurement that follows found its shapes gone and the session had to regenerate once more; it did, and every batch succeeded. A recycle costs 0.35 to 0.49 s (once 1.3 s).
- **Resident memory is the finding that matters most.** One session is about 0.55 GiB resident after opening and up to 1 GiB as its kernel heap grows toward the recycle (about 0.2 GiB of that is the test runner and the code). Each kernel instance costs its 128 MiB or more of wasm memory plus about 115 MB of V8 heap for libcascade's 5,298 bound classes. A recycled instance is collectable, but V8 does not collect it on its own soon: without forced collections the process grew to 2.1 GiB (bracket) and 3.7 GiB (bookshelf) over the 1,000 batches; with `gc()` every 100 batches the same bracket run stayed between 0.76 and 1.3 GiB. One collection right after a recycle did not give the memory back (the 1.3 GiB readings are exactly those), so whether a `gc()` after each recycle bounds memory is unverified.
- **Node and Chromium agree bit for bit on the bracket and the bookshelf**: mesh hashes, name tables, feature results and cache keys, and the kernel's exact volume, area, centre of mass and bounding box of every body, on 9 documents (each fixture as opened, after its story, and at two points of its long run), against the app's own regen worker in Chromium 153.
- **The shed differs in its framing, by at most 2.2 × 10⁻¹² mm.** 12 of its 36 member shape meshes have different bits, 2 of them with the same corners in another vertex order, and 2 sheathing bodies have different cache keys (their meshes and measurements are identical). The cause is JavaScript's `Math`, not the kernel: V8 14.6 (Node 26.10) and V8 15.3 (Chromium 153) differ by 1 or 2 units in the last place in `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `atan`, `asin`, `acos`, `atan2`, `exp`, `log` and `cbrt` for 4 to 19 % of inputs (`sqrt`, `hypot` and `pow` agree), and the construction domain computes roof and gable geometry with them. Every measurement the review compares (the plan's decision 4) is identical.
- **Two things a Node host must inject, and nothing else on the session path reaches for a browser API.** The bundled font is fetched from a `file:` URL, which Node's `fetch` refuses ("This font could not be read (inter-bold): fetch failed"): pass `fetchImpl`. And regen's default extension registry is empty until the app's worker entry fills it (29 of the bookshelf's 37 features fail, the boards as `unsupported`): pass a registry with stock, wood and construction. Manifold, `crypto.subtle` and `navigator.locks` exist in Node 26. No package on the path uses `OffscreenCanvas`. User fonts and scripts, which the browser runs under a watchdog in a worker, would run in the session's own thread in Node: see [Risks](#risks-browser-apis-and-how-to-inject-each).
- **Recommended starting limits:** 500 commands per batch, 2,000 batches per session and 512 MiB kernel recycle as planned; regen time per batch 30 s instead of 60; 4 sessions per process only with each session's kernel in a worker thread of its own, 2 if they share the main thread; images unchanged until T8.0b measures them. Details under [Limits](#limits-starting-values).

## Method

**The session.** `src/session.ts` is the least that runs every step of the plan's model. `open` takes Main's head from the library (a `NodeFsBackend`, the library's five-operation `StorageBackend` on `node:fs`), calls `createVersion` ("Agent session `<id>` start") and `createBranch` from that version, takes the branch's lock (a lock file created with `O_EXCL`, holding the pid), opens the branch into a `DocumentStore` and runs a full regen. `apply` refuses a batch over the command or session limit before running it, executes it as one undo step, regenerates through `engine.update` under the time limit (cancelling the kernel and rolling back with an undo when it runs over), measures every body whose shape changed with the kernel's `measure` op, and saves one revision of the branch with one log entry. `undo` saves the inverse as a new revision. `close` disposes the engine and releases the lock. `resume` opens an existing branch instead.

**The host** (`src/host.ts`) is what the app's regen worker entry provides, done for Node: the kernel service on the `.wasm` read from disk, the solver in the same thread, the text outliner with the bundled font read from disk, and a regen registry with the stock, woodworking and construction domains.

**The fixtures** (`src/fixtures.ts`), built from core commands:

| Fixture   | Source                                                                          | Features | Bodies | Members | Triangles | Names |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | ------ | ------- | --------- | ----- |
| Bracket   | The M1 bracket as the walkthrough leaves it, plus an engraved text (Inter Bold) | 7        | 1      | 0       | 784       | 325   |
| Bookshelf | `apps/web/e2e/m4-fixtures.ts`: 12 boards, 16 joints, configurations, assembly   | 37       | 12     | 0       | 1,312     | 452   |
| Shed      | `apps/web/e2e/shed-fixture.ts`: 4 walls, 3 openings, floor on skids, gable roof | 9        | 9      | 156     | 156       | 202   |

The bracket's volume checks against the hand formula of `apps/web/e2e/bracket.ts` (14,729.78 mm³ at 6 mm). Each fixture's **story** is a few batches an agent might send (engrave a part number, thicker walls, a fillet radius from a new variable; 36" wide and a rename; moving a window twice). The **long run** is 1,000 batches of one kind of edit whose value never repeats (thickness 5 to 9 mm, width 24" to 36", window 40" to 56" along the wall, along the golden-ratio sequence), so regen builds rather than serving its cache. The bracket's long run starts with the text engraved, so every batch cuts the text again.

**Node against Chromium** (`src/browser.test.ts`): 13 documents (each fixture as opened, after its story, and at long-run batches 7 and 500; the bracket also engraved alone) are serialized and parsed back as a library load would, regenerated twice in Node on fresh engines (to prove Node agrees with itself), and once in Chromium by the app's own regen worker (`apps/web/src/viewport/regen-spawn.ts`, which brings the same kernel build, solver, domains and text worker as the app), a fresh worker per document so every body carries its mesh. Both sides summarize with the same code (`src/summary.ts`): per feature its status, cache key, errors, warnings and how each reference resolved; the name table; per body its cache key, solid count, a SHA-256 of every mesh array and the names on its faces and edges; per framing set its key and a hash of its instance matrices; per member shape a hash of its mesh; per assembly its solved instances; and per body the kernel's `measure` with `body: true`. Anything that differs is listed.

What rebuilds: the bracket's thickness drives its first sketch, so all seven features rebuild every time, the text cut taking about 110 of the 150 ms (the bracket without text regenerates in 30 ms). Reopening a branch with 1,000 revisions costs no more than opening a new one: the head names its snapshot directly.

## Kernel recycles over 1,000 batches

One session per fixture, its own kernel service at the default threshold (512 MiB; the wasm memory grows in steps, so a recycle happens once it reaches 550.7 MiB).

| Fixture   | Recycles | At batches                    | Kernel heap growth per batch | Recycle (ms)      | Extra regens |
| --------- | -------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------ |
| Bracket   | 2        | 413, 826                      | about 1.0 MiB                | 382, 350          | 2            |
| Bookshelf | 5        | 187, 374, 561, 748, 935       | about 2.3 MiB                | 354 to 486, 1,322 | 5            |
| Shed      | 0        | (heap 319 MiB at batch 1,000) | about 0.2 MiB                | none              | 0            |

The service recycles at an idle point once the heap is over the threshold, which in a session is right after the regen's last kernel batch: the `measure` that follows then finds every shape unknown. The prototype detects that and regenerates once more (`lost` in `session.ts`), so the engine's regen count is exactly batches plus recycles (1,004, 1,006 and 1,001 with the open and the engraving). No batch failed, and each reopened branch regenerated to exactly what the session held.

**Memory across recycles** (`longrun-*.json` `trace`, `gc.json`). Without forced collections, resident memory rose all along the run: bracket 574 MiB at batch 1 to 2,133 MiB at 1,000, bookshelf 587 to 3,696 MiB, shed (no recycles) 558 MiB at batch 1, a peak of 912 MiB at batch 900 and 803 MiB at 1,000. The bookshelf's `memoryAtEnd`, read after a full collection, is still 2,895 MiB. The same bracket run with `gc()` every 100 batches stayed between 762 and 966 MiB, except at the first reading after each recycle (batch 500, after the recycle at 413: 1,295.8 MiB resident and 313.9 MB of V8 heap after the forced `gc()`; batch 900, after the recycle at 826: 1,267.3 MiB and 313.8 MB), and was back under 790 MiB only at the reading 100 batches later. A WeakRef per kernel instance already showed the old instance collected at that first reading (`instancesAlive`), yet that reading's collection did not give the memory back; the following one did. So a recycled instance is not leaked, and a `gc()` every 100 batches kept the bracket between 0.76 and 1.3 GiB, but nothing makes V8 free its 550 MiB of wasm memory and 110 MB of bindings promptly (estimate of the reason: the V8 heap itself stays small, so nothing triggers a full collection), and one collection right after a recycle was not enough. A `gc()` after each recycle (which needs `--expose-gc`) is therefore unverified as a bound; running the kernel in a worker thread and terminating the worker on a recycle should free everything at once (estimate). Verifying either is an open item for T8.1c. The app's regen worker recycles inside the worker in the same way, so a browser tab may hold old instances just as long; that was not measured here and is worth a look in the app.

Each further session costs 140 to 240 MiB at first, growing to about 550 MiB of wasm plus 115 MB of bindings before its first recycle. After all six were closed and dropped, the V8 heap went back to 197 MB but resident memory stayed at 1,218 MiB (freed pages kept by the allocator). Four shed sessions applying batches at the same time took turns on the one thread: a batch took 151 ms (median; 261 max) against 38 ms alone. Two sessions sharing one kernel service ran 20 interleaved batches without a failure, but a recycle drops every shape of every engine on the service, and its cancellation is by generation across all of them, so a session should not share its service (one per session, as the prototype does).

**The differences, all in the shed, all from JavaScript `Math`:**

| What differs                                   | Where                                                      | Size                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Member shape meshes (12 of 36), bits           | 2x4s cut by roof-pitch planes: gable studs and rafter ties | Every corner within 2.2 × 10⁻¹² mm; volumes equal within 2 × 10⁻⁸ mm³ |
| 2 of those, vertex order                       | Two 123.825 mm gable studs                                 | Same corners in another order (same set within 2.2 × 10⁻¹² mm)        |
| Body cache keys (2)                            | The two gable sheathing bodies                             | Their meshes, names and measurements identical                        |
| Measurements, names, feature results, statuses | none                                                       | identical everywhere                                                  |

Cause: the construction domain computes roof and gable geometry in TypeScript with `Math.atan`, `Math.cos`, `Math.sin` and friends, and Node 26.10 (V8 14.6) and Chromium 153 (V8 15.3) implement them differently. The spike's probe (`src/mathprobe.ts`, 856 inputs each, raw bits):

| Function  | `sin` | `cos` | `tan` | `atan` | `asin` | `acos` | `atan2` | `exp` | `log` | `cbrt` | `sqrt`, `hypot`, `pow` |
| --------- | ----- | ----- | ----- | ------ | ------ | ------ | ------- | ----- | ----- | ------ | ---------------------- |
| Differ    | 43    | 34    | 48    | 71     | 63     | 76     | 158     | 87    | 62    | 61     | 0                      |
| Max (ulp) | 1     | 1     | 2     | 1      | 1      | 1      | 1       | 1     | 2     | 1      | 0                      |

A last-bit difference in a plane's normal moves a cut corner by 10⁻¹² mm and changes a sort among near-equal vertices; it changes a cache key wherever the value is hashed. The kernel and the solver are wasm and gave identical bits for identical inputs throughout. T7.0c found the same split between the two V8 builds (632 differing values). The bracket and the bookshelf compute nothing of the kind in JavaScript on their path, and agree bit for bit.

What this means for M8: the review gate must compare measurements (with a tolerance) and names, never cache keys or mesh hashes, between a bundle made in Node and the app's regen; ADR 0016 should say so. Node and the browser will drift apart at least whenever one of them updates V8, so "identical" can only be promised for the kernel's outputs given identical inputs.

**A harness finding.** The first comparison served the page through a Playwright route on `https://spike.test`, as other spikes do. The bracket's text then failed in Chromium only ("the text worker could not be started"): the route serves the page and the regen worker, but not the text worker the regen worker starts (its request never reached the route; the recorded error is "The worker stopped (out of memory, or a crash)", so a worker started by a worker bypassing the route is the likely cause, not a shown one). Serving from a server on 127.0.0.1 on a port the system picks fixed it; `routeHarness` in `browser.json` keeps the evidence. Specs that need text from a worker inside a worker cannot use a route.

Worker threads give parallel regens, the hard timeouts the browser has for scripts and fonts, and memory freed on terminate.                 |

## Recommendations

For T8.0c (ADR 0016) and T8.1c (`packages/session`):

1. **Build the session as the prototype does**: library, `DocumentStore`, `RegenEngine` with an injected host (kernel service, solver, text outliner with `fetchImpl`, a domain registry of its own, scripts), one batch per `store.execute`, one revision and one log entry per batch. It needed no change to core, regen or the kernel.
2. **One kernel service per session, in a worker thread per session.** It is what makes 4 sessions per process work in time and memory, gives fonts and scripts the hard limits the browser has, and frees a kernel instance at once. In the main thread, limit to 2 sessions and collect with `gc()` periodically (every 100 batches kept the bracket between 0.76 and 1.3 GiB; one collection right after a recycle did not free it, so a `gc()` after each recycle is unverified). Verifying that, or that terminating the worker frees the instance, is an open item for T8.1c.
3. **Handle the recycle that lands after a regen**: when the measurement after a regen finds its shapes unknown (the `recycled` status since the regen started), regenerate once more before answering. Expect it every 190 to 410 batches on heavy documents.
4. **Regen limit 30 s**, cancelled through the kernel service's generation and rolled back with the store's undo plus a cached regen.
5. **Compare measurements and names, not cache keys or mesh bits, at review time.** Node and the browser agree on the kernel's results for identical inputs, but JavaScript `Math` differs between V8 builds, and domains feed it into geometry. ADR 0016 should list the shed's case as the known difference and its cause.
6. **For T8.1a**: `BackendKind` needs a Node kind (`'fs'`); a cross-process branch lock belongs in the Node backend (`navigator.locks` is per process); branch provenance (`origin`, session id, review state) is not in `Branch` yet, so the prototype encoded the session in the branch's name.
7. **Do not serve test pages that start nested workers through a Playwright route**; serve them from 127.0.0.1.
