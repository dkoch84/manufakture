## Summary

- **Both ports compute the same offsets.** On every sketch case (14 runs over 4 fixtures and 2 flattening tolerances) clipper2-ts 2.0.1-18 and clipper2-wasm 0.4.0 return identical vertex sets. On the 10,000-vertex polygons 2 to 16 of 11,346 to 14,473 vertices differ, with area differences below 1e-5 mm2.
- **clipper2-wasm is 1.1 to 3.4 times faster; in absolute terms both are fast.** Medians: the bracket in 0.64 to 3.1 ms (TS) against 0.30 to 1.6 ms (WASM); a 10,000-vertex outline in 35 to 126 ms against 23 to 90 ms; 50 pocket rings in 264 ms against 130 ms. Both load cold in a fresh worker in about 8 ms.
- **clipper2-ts has the better API for this job.** It exposes `ClipperOffset` with a Z callback, which tagged every intersection point the offset's own Z rule leaves at 0 (2 to 198 per offset), plain objects and synchronous calls, with nothing to free. clipper2-wasm exposes only `InflatePaths64`/`InflatePathsD`. Z values do pass through, but with no callback those intersection points stay untagged (2 to 201 per offset). Every embind object must be deleted: with everything deleted, 1,000 calls left the heap exactly where it was; forgetting only the result grew it by 29 MB. Its `types` path names a file the package does not ship, and it ships no license file.
- **Integer scale: 1e4 units per mm (0.1 micrometre).** Integer rounding then adds about 0.00013 mm to the offset error (1e3 adds 0.0017, 1e5 and above add nothing measurable). 2 m is 2e7 units, inside clipper2-ts's fast integer paths. clipper2-ts does not check `Path64` input against 2^53 (it returned a result at 2e16 without a word), so our adapter must check.
- **Offsets are as accurate as the flattening and the joins.** With arcs flattened at 0.001 mm and joins at `arcTol` 0.001 mm, the Hausdorff distance to the exact offset is 0.0011 to 0.0012 mm on every sketch case. At 0.01 mm flattening it is 0.01 mm. Slots narrower than the tool vanish cleanly, and a 0.2 mm sliver survives with the right area (error 0.0007 mm2); the dumbbell splits in two.
- **Never offset an offset.** Fifty pocket rings, each from the previous ring instead of from the source, gave 22 times the vertices (413,695 against 18,522), took 41 times as long (10.7 s against 0.26 s), and grew the worst ring's error from 0.0013 to 0.0019 mm.
- **Arc refit works best when it is tagged.** Tag each flattened vertex in Z with its source vertex, and the tags name the exact circle each run of output lies on: a source arc's concentric offset, or the round join around a sharp corner. A greedy fit handles the rest. At 0.001 mm flattening this cuts segments 27 to 131 times on the sketch cases (77.8 times over the 50 pocket rings) with a maximum deviation of 0.0018 mm from the exact offset. The tagged arcs themselves sit on the exact circles (to 1e-14 mm on the frame). An untagged fit gets similar counts at 0.001 mm (25 to 131 times) but places arcs only as well as the polyline (0.0018 mm), and it collapses to 1.2 to 4.7 times when flattening is coarser than its tolerance.
- **Tiny arcs must become lines, or Grbl cuts full circles.** Grbl's radius check (error 33) is not the only trap. Grbl 1.1 computes an arc's angular travel from the written X, Y, I and J, and when start and end coincide (within 5e-7 rad) it adds a full turn. The first version of the tagged refit emitted such arcs on dense polygons: on flower-10k +3 (tagged + Z callback) an arc of 1.1e-5 rad was written as `G2 X36.494 Y-6.533 I-2.832 J-0.991`, start equal to end, which Grbl cuts as a 6 mm circle. The refit now turns an arc into a G1 when its sagitta is at most a twentieth of the tolerance (0.0001 mm) or when a 3-decimal Grbl check (radius rule and angular travel, emulated in single precision) fails. That turned 65 arcs into lines over all refit runs, all of them on the 10,000-vertex polygons, and no refit got further than 0.0020 mm from its polyline because of it. After it, no arc fails either check. The largest start-to-end radius difference is 0.0015 mm (untagged) and 0.0008 mm (tagged), against GRBL's 0.005 mm.

Recommendation: `packages/cam` uses **clipper2-ts 2.0.1-18** behind a small adapter, at a scale of **1e4 units per mm**. It flattens arcs at **0.001 mm** with round-join `arcTol` **0.001 mm**, tags every input vertex in Z, uses the TS Z callback to tag intersections, offsets every ring from the source, and rebuilds G2/G3 with the **tagged refit** (untagged fit at 0.002 mm for the rest). Details under [Recommendations](#recommendations).

Cases (all millimetres; a 6 mm tool, so offsets of plus and minus 3):

| Case         | What                                                                                                   | Exact area known |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------- |
| bracket      | 100 x 60 rounded rectangle (r8, lines and tangent arcs) with two r6 holes and a 30 x 10 slot           | yes              |
| frame        | 100 mm square frame with a 60 mm square hole (sharp corners), and an r15 island region inside the hole | yes              |
| dumbbell     | two r10 lobes joined by a 4 mm neck; the inward offset splits it                                       | no               |
| narrow-slots | slots 5 mm (must vanish) and 6.2 mm wide (a 0.2 mm sliver survives), inward 3                          | yes              |
| circle-10k   | a 10,000-vertex polygon on an r50 circle, measured against the exact circle                            | yes              |
| flower-10k   | a 10,000-vertex wavy outline, r = 40 + 6 sin 9t (concave and convex)                                   | no               |
| pocket       | 200 x 160 rounded pocket with an r12 island; 50 rings from -3 to -76.5, 1.5 apart (31 are not empty)   | no               |

The sketch cases are sketch entities run through `detectRegions`, converted from its `RegionCurve`s to exact lines and arcs, and flattened at 0.001 and 0.01 mm (vertices on the arc). Each flattened vertex gets its own Z tag, its index plus one, because Clipper treats Z = 0 as "no tag". A table maps each tag back to its point and its source curves.

**Correctness** is measured against the exact offset of the exact line/arc source, not against the flattened input. Forward: for vertices and segment midpoints of the result, `| dist(p, boundary) - |d| |`. Backward: samples of the exact offset (every curve moved along its normal, plus the round join at every corner on the offset side), keeping only samples really at distance `|d|` from the whole boundary, and their distance to the result. The Hausdorff distance is the larger of the two. Areas are compared with closed forms where they exist.

At 0.01 mm flattening every sketch case is 2 to 9 times faster (the pocket's 50 rings: 36 ms TS, 17 ms WASM), and the error is the flattening's: 0.0096 to 0.0101 mm, with area errors up to 0.7 mm2.

What the numbers say:

- **The error budget is additive and predictable.** Flattening puts vertices on the arcs, so the polyline lies inside each arc by up to the chord error. The round joins add their own chord error (`arcTol`), and integer rounding adds about 0.0001 mm. 0.001 + 0.001 gave 0.0011 to 0.0012 mm everywhere. The area errors are the same thing integrated: perimeter times mean deviation.
- **Degenerate cases behave.** The 5 mm slot under a 6 mm tool returns nothing (no self-intersecting leftover), the 6.2 mm slot returns one 0.2 mm sliver whose area is right to 0.0007 mm2, and the dumbbell returns two loops. Both ports agree vertex for vertex on all of it.
- **The WASM advantage is largest on small inputs** (1.8 to 3.4 times, where the per-call conversion and engine overhead dominate in TS) and smallest on large ones (1.1 to 1.8 times on 10,000 vertices). `PathD` (Float64Array in, library scales) is slightly faster than `Path64` (BigInt64Array, our own scaling), because it avoids BigInt conversions.
- **Offsetting the previous ring is a trap.** Each pass offsets every vertex of the previous ring's flattened joins, so vertices multiply (22 times), time goes from 0.26 s to 10.7 s, and the flattening errors compound. Every ring must be offset from the source loops.

### Z tags

Clipper2's offset copies the Z of the vertex it offsets into every point it emits for it (both ends of the adjacent offset edges and the whole round join). Its cleanup union then gives an intersection point the Z that its two edges share, if they share one. That rule is the same in both ports (`ClipperOffset::ZCB`); only clipper2-ts lets us add a callback for the rest.

### Arc refit

Two methods, both in `src/refit.ts`, refit tolerance 0.002 mm:

- **Untagged**: start at the sharpest vertex; extend a run as long as one circle (through the run's first, middle and last point) holds every vertex and every segment midpoint within the tolerance, turning one way and by at most half a turn, or as long as one line does; take the longer. Exponential then binary search keeps it near linear.
- **Tagged**: a segment whose two ends carry the same sharp-corner tag lies on the round join around that corner (centre: the corner, radius `|d|`). A segment whose two ends come from the same source arc lies on that arc's concentric offset (radius `r + d` inside a counter-clockwise arc, `r - d` outside a clockwise one). A segment accepts its circle if its ends and midpoint are within `tagTol` (flatten + `arcTol` + 0.0005 mm). Runs on one circle become one arc on the exact circle, ends projected onto it. Runs the tags do not explain go through the untagged fitter. Adjacent arcs meet at their circles' intersection, and lines move to meet arcs.
- **Both, last step**: an arc becomes a line (same ends) when its sagitta is at most tol / 20 (0.0001 mm), or when Grbl would not cut it as meant once written with 3 decimals (`grblArcCheck`: error 33's radius rule, and `mc_arc`'s angular travel with its 5e-7 rad full-circle rule, in single precision; a travel more than 0.5 rad off the intended sweep fails).

#### Degenerate arcs

Tagged refit with the Z callback on the 10,000-vertex outlines, before and after the arc-to-line step ("Degenerate arcs" in the JSON):

| Case          | Arcs to G1 | Arcs | Smallest written chord | Smallest sweep | Grbl full circles |
| ------------- | ---------- | ---- | ---------------------- | -------------- | ----------------- |
| flower-10k +3 | no         | 146  | 0 mm                   | 3.0e-6 rad     | 1                 |
| flower-10k +3 | yes        | 140  | 0.223 mm               | 8.7e-4 rad     | 0                 |
| flower-10k -3 | no         | 194  | 0.0014 mm              | 5.5e-4 rad     | 0                 |
| flower-10k -3 | yes        | 165  | 0.213 mm               | 4.2e-3 rad     | 0                 |
| circle-10k +3 | no         | 5    | 0.0125 mm              | 4.3e-3 rad     | 0                 |
| circle-10k +3 | yes        | 3    | 49.5 mm                | 0.97 rad       | 0                 |

Tag tolerances of 0.0015 mm (the default for polygon input) and 0.0025 mm gave the same rows. Where these arcs come from: on a dense polygon every vertex is a sharp corner with a round join of a few micrometres, and a corner join of two or more segments becomes its own arc. Those arcs are harmless as geometry but not as G-code: rounded to 3 decimals, start and end can coincide (flower +3), and Grbl then runs a full turn. The circle's 0.0125 mm, 4e-3 rad arcs are not wrong in Grbl, but a line there is within 1e-5 mm of them and is the safer word.

What the numbers say:

- **Tags make arcs exact; geometry alone makes them only as good as the polyline.** Wherever the tags explain a run, the arc is placed on the true circle, so its error is zero whatever the flattening tolerance (the frame: 1e-14 mm at both 0.001 and 0.01). The untagged fit inherits the polyline's error, up to 0.0018 mm here, and cannot be better than the flattening.
- **Coarse flattening breaks the untagged fit.** At 0.01 mm the polyline is farther from its circles than the 0.002 mm tolerance, so the fit falls back to short pieces (1.2 to 4.7 times reduction, 3,267 tiny arcs in the pocket). The tagged fit still rebuilds the arcs (5 to 62 times), but the parts it hands to the untagged fitter (lines near untagged intersections, joins that merge with other rings) keep the coarse error (0.0081 mm in the pocket). So flatten at 0.001 mm; the input is small anyway (472 vertices for the pocket).
- **The Z callback matters where offsets intersect.** On the dumbbell's outward offset the callback tags the 4 neck intersections, and the refit drops from 10 to 6 elements, with the error from 0.00037 to 0.00014 mm. In the pocket it brings the tagged count from 288 to 244 elements. clipper2-wasm cannot do this.
- **Grbl's error 33 has margin; its full-circle rule does not.** Arc ends are projected onto their circle, so the start and end radius agree to float precision, and rounding X, Y, I and J to 3 decimals leaves at most 0.0015 mm against Grbl's 0.005 mm (and 0.1% of the radius) limit; at 4 decimals it would be ten times smaller. The radius check does not catch the degenerate arcs above, though: their radius is right, only their written ends coincide. Hence the angular travel check and the arc-to-line step. Arcs are capped at half a turn, which also keeps IJK arcs well conditioned.
- **The cost is small.** Tagged refit takes 11 ms for all 50 pocket rings, against 264 ms to compute them.

### Integer scale

The bracket offset inward by 3 mm, near the origin and moved to (1900, 1900) mm (a corner of a large bed), flatten and `arcTol` 0.001 mm:

| Scale (units per mm) | Largest coordinate at 2 m | Hausdorff mm | clipper2-ts ms | Notes                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------ | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1e2                  | 2e5                       | 0.0168       | 0.51           | 1,095 vertices against 1,216: joins and small edges are lost             |
| 1e3                  | 2e6                       | 0.0027       | 0.72           | rounding adds 0.0017 mm                                                  |
| **1e4**              | **2e7**                   | **0.00112**  | **0.77**       | rounding adds about 0.00013 mm                                           |
| 1e5                  | 2e8                       | 0.00100      | 0.80           | past clipper2-ts's fast area path (4.7e7) for parts more than 0.47 m out |
| 1e6 to 1e8           | 2e9 to 2e11               | 0.00099      | 0.81 to 0.83   |                                                                          |
| 1e10 to 1e13         | 2e13 to 2e16              | 0.00099      | 1.0 to 1.2     | BigInt paths; at 2e16 past 2^53, no error raised                         |

Results were the same near the origin and at 1.9 m, and the same for both ports, up to the ports' limits. clipper2-wasm is int64 inside, and its `PathD` API stops at 8 decimals.

- **1e4 is the right scale.** At 1e4 the integer grid adds about 0.00013 mm to an error budget of about 0.002 mm, and larger scales gain at most that. 2 m is 2e7 units. clipper2-ts takes plain `number` fast paths while coordinate differences stay below 9.5e7 (cross products) and coordinates stay below 4.7e7 (areas). At 1e4 that covers 4.7 m, every supported machine and then some. At 1e5 a part placed beyond 0.47 m would drop into the BigInt paths.
- **clipper2-ts's "throws on overflow" applies to its own scaling helpers only** (`inflatePathsD`, `scalePath64`, `Point64Utils.fromPointD`). Given `Path64` points directly, it computed silently at 2e16, where doubles can no longer hold every integer. The adapter must reject coordinates beyond a limit (4.7e7 units, 4.7 m, keeps every computation on the fast path).

### Load and bundle cost

| What                                                              | Raw     | gzip    | brotli  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ------- | ------- |
| clipper2-ts, offset + booleans, tree-shaken and minified (Vite 8) | 82.0 KB | 19.6 KB | 17.0 KB |
| clipper2-wasm glue `dist/es/clipper2z.js` (shipped minified)      | 54.1 KB | 13.9 KB | 12.6 KB |
| clipper2-wasm `clipper2z.wasm`                                    | 214 KB  | 81.5 KB | 64.1 KB |

Cold load in a fresh Node worker thread, median of 7: clipper2-ts imports in 6.1 ms, and its first offset takes 2.0 ms (8.1 ms total). clipper2-wasm reads its glue and `.wasm` in 2.3 ms, compiles in 0.3 ms, then instantiates and runs a first offset in 4.6 ms (7.3 ms total). In a browser the `.wasm` adds a 64 KB download (brotli) and an asynchronous instantiation before the first call; clipper2-ts rides in the CAM worker's own chunk. Estimate: neither matters next to OCCT's load.

Two packaging notes for clipper2-wasm: the glue finds its `.wasm` with `new URL('clipper2z.wasm', import.meta.url)`, which Vite's library mode inlines as base64 (the glue then bundles to 322 KB). An app would pass `locateFile` or `instantiateWasm` with a `?url` import. Also, its `package.json` `types` points at `dist/es/clipper2z.d.ts`, which is not in the package; the declarations are at `dist/clipper2z.d.ts` (the spike maps them in `src/clipper2z.d.ts`).

"Heap top" is the address `malloc` returns for a 1 MiB block, captured from the instance's exports: it moves up when anything is left allocated. Unlike libcascade, clipper2-wasm's destructors work. But every `Paths64.get(i)` returns a new object that needs its own `delete()`, and `view()` is a view into the WASM heap that must be copied before the object is deleted or the memory grows. clipper2-ts needs none of this.

## Recommendations

For T5.0c (architecture ADR) and T5.2a (offset engine):

1. **Use clipper2-ts 2.0.1-18**, pinned exactly, as `packages/cam`'s one polygon library, behind an adapter (`src/offset/`) that owns scaling, tagging and range checks. Reasons, in order:
   - same results as the WASM build (vertex for vertex on sketch input);
   - a Z callback, which the tagged refit needs at intersections, and the full `ClipperOffset` (per-path join and end types, `PolyTree64` output, `deltaCallback` for variable offsets);
   - synchronous calls on plain objects, nothing to free, nothing to load, runs in Node tests unchanged;
   - its own license file;
   - it is slower, 1.1 to 3.4 times, but a 50-ring pocket in 0.26 s in the CAM worker, cached (plan decision 9), is not the bottleneck.

   The adapter keeps the engine swappable: if profiling of real jobs (T5.2c) ever shows offsets dominating, clipper2-wasm's `InflatePathsD` drops in for the offsets that need no Z callback.

2. **Scale 1e4 units per mm**, one constant. The adapter rejects any coordinate whose magnitude exceeds 4.7e7 units (4.7 m), since clipper2-ts will not.
3. **Tolerances in one place**: flatten arcs at 0.001 mm (vertices on the arc), round joins at `arcTol` 0.001 mm (pass `arcTolerance` explicitly: the default is `|delta| * 0.002`, 0.006 mm for a 6 mm tool), refit 0.002 mm, tag acceptance flatten + `arcTol` + 0.0005 mm. Expected error: about 0.0012 mm before refit, at most 0.002 mm after, with tagged arcs exact.
4. **Tag every input vertex** (Z = vertex index + 1) with a table back to the source curves (`curves`, and `corner` for sharp junctions only, so tangent junctions do not break arcs). Set `zCallback` to give intersections the tag of the nearest tagged edge end.
5. **Offset every ring from the source**, never from the previous ring. For pocket clearing, offset the source by each ring's distance; the cleanup union already handles islands and splits.
6. **Rebuild arcs with the tagged refit**, untagged fit at 0.002 mm for what tags do not explain and for tagless input (imported outlines). Project arc ends onto their circle, meet adjacent arcs at their circles' intersection, cap sweeps at half a turn. As the last step, **turn an arc into a G1 (same ends) when its sagitta is at most tol / 20, or when it fails a Grbl check at the post's precision**: the radius rule (end radius within 0.005 mm or 0.1% of the start radius, never more than 0.5 mm), and the angular travel Grbl's `mc_arc` computes from the rounded X, Y, I, J (atan2 of the two radius vectors, plus or minus a full turn when within 5e-7 rad of zero), which must match the intended sweep (the spike fails anything more than 0.5 rad off). The spike's `grblArcCheck` and `demoteArcs` are the reference.

   **The post (T5.4a) runs the same check on every arc as it formats it**, with its own decimals (3 or 4, inch or mm), since its rounding is the one Grbl sees, and writes an arc that fails as a G1 to its end point (the arc is then within the sagitta of the line, which the refit has kept tiny). It must never emit an IJK arc whose rounded start equals its rounded end unless a full circle is meant. The spike's worst radius difference is 0.0015 mm at 3 decimals.

7. **Analytic fast path** (plan T5.2a): **not measured in this spike**; no analytic offset of line/arc loops was built or timed. The tagged refit already returns exact geometry for every arc the offset keeps (frame: 1e-14 mm), so an analytic path would mostly save the Clipper call, which costs 0.2 to 4 ms per sketch case here (estimate: it buys little). T5.2a can skip it unless profiling says otherwise.
8. **ADR 0006 inventory**: add clipper2-ts 2.0.1-18, BSL-1.0, verified from its installed `package.json` and `LICENSE`.

## Open questions

- Browser timings were not measured; Node's V8 is the same engine as Chrome's, so the relative numbers should hold (estimate). The CAM worker (T5.1g) is where to confirm them.
- The tagged refit was prototyped on round joins only. Profile tool compensation with miter or square joins (not planned for M5) would need corner keys for those shapes.
- Open-path offsets (engraving along a line, lead-ins) were not measured; `EndType.Round`/`Butt` go through the same `ClipperOffset`, so the same tagging applies (estimate).
