## Summary

- **Exact HLR (`HLRBRep_Algo`) is correct and fast enough to be the only default.** On the box with a hole, the cylinder and the M1 bracket it matches views drawn by hand, edge by edge, in every view checked (11 views; front, top, right, and isometric for the first two), with no missing and no extra line. The 2D output is in the view frame exactly as a plain TypeScript projection computes it (to 1e-9 mm), so dimensions never need the kernel to be projected. A 100-board cabinet run takes 42 to 96 ms per view, a 954-face STEP import 96 to 178 ms, the bookshelf 14 to 50 ms, the bracket about 3 ms (Node 26, medians).
- **The poly algorithm is not a safe fallback for woodworking.** It drew 1,429 mm of hidden edges as visible on the bookshelf's front view (the back panel's edges in the rabbets, where boards touch), and it is as slow as exact or slower on every single-body fixture (up to 7 times). It agreed with exact on everything without board contact.
- **Edge classes**: sharp, smooth (`Rg1Line`, tangent edges), sewn (`RgNLine`, in practice the seams of cylinders) and outline (silhouettes), each visible or hidden. Two surprises: a circle seen edge on comes back as a `BSplineCurve`, not a line (the extractor detects straight B-splines); and where a seam lies on a silhouette, HLR reports one edge, as sharp, and drops the outline. That explains the Part 1 count of "10 hidden silhouettes where 20 were expected": the other 10 were the seams, reported as sharp.
- **Coincident lines**: hidden edges lying exactly under visible ones are returned too (280 mm of the box's front view, 228 mm of the bracket's). Harmless on screen, but a drawing should drop them before DXF.
- **Memory**: `HLRBRep_Algo` frees on `delete()`, but `HLRBRep_HLRToShape` keeps a handle to it and its own destructor is empty (as are 58 of the 71 bound HLR classes). Removing every loaded shape from the algorithm (`Remove(i)`) before `delete()` cuts the leak per projection from 640 KiB to 70 KiB on the 40-hole board; reading points with `D0` into one reused `gp_Pnt` instead of `Value()` halved what was left. No embind object is left behind (tracker). One projection of the 100-board run fits in the initial 128 MiB heap.
- **Bodies must be projected together.** Per-body layers from one run (`CompoundOfEdges(S, ...)`) are exact; projecting each body alone and merging draws up to 72 % of the visible length wrong in a cabinet run (edges another board hides). Reusing a body's projection for a translated instance (shift the 2D output) is exact.
- **Sections work by cutting first**: the bracket cut by a half space at Y = 0 gives exactly the hand-computed section (3 faces, 422.6336 mm2) and every section edge is drawn visible.
- **There is a way back from HLR edges to model edges after all**: reading the same classes in 3D (`CompoundOfEdges(..., In3d = true)`) gives the 3D pieces, and every sharp, smooth and sewn piece lies on a model edge (2,263 pieces on 6 fixtures, none without). But the 2D and 3D readings are not one to one, it costs 1.0 to 4.0 times the plain projection, and picking does not need it (next point), so it is not recommended for M4.
- **Picking by the nearest projected model edge, as the plan states it, fails in principal views**: 78.3 % of 25,102 clicks overall and 0 % in several principal views, because front and back edges project onto the same line. With a depth tie-break (among edges within 0.15 mm of the nearest, take the one nearest the viewer) it picks the right edge on 25,100 of 25,102 clicks (the 2 misses are near the ends of edges, where two edges meet).
- **The dimension model works**: references picked in a view, stored as names (`VertexRef`, `EdgeRef`, `FaceRef`), resolved with the kernel's naming, measured exactly and projected in TypeScript. On the bracket, the foot thickness follows 6 to 8 mm with every reference `exact`, the hole spacing and diameters stay right, and the fillet radius becomes `lost` when the fillet is deleted. Values format through `packages/units` (`2' 11-7/16"`, `3' 4-1/2"`, `23/32"`).

Recommendation: T4.4b builds `project` on the exact algorithm only, with all of a view's bodies in one run, per-item layers, the release rules below, and a straight-B-spline test; poly stays out of M4 (or behind an explicit option with a warning). T4.4c stores dimension references as the kernel's existing reference types, plus an instance path for assembly views. T4.4e sends picking data and the app picks with the depth tie-break. Details under [Recommendations](#recommendations).

## What the spike does

`spikes/hlr/src/hlr.ts` is the prototype of T4.4b's op: `projectExact(oc, shapes, view, options)` runs `HLRBRep_Algo` with an `HLRAlgo_Projector` built from the view frame, reads each class with `HLRBRep_HLRToShape.CompoundOfEdges(type, visible, In3d)` (or its per-shape overload), and turns each edge into a line, circular arc, elliptical arc or polyline in view coordinates; `projectPoly` does the same with `HLRBRep_PolyAlgo` on a triangulation made for it. Views (`views.ts`) are third angle for a Z-up model: front looks along +Y, top down -Z, right along -X, isometric from (1, -1, 1); paper x is right, y up, both in model millimetres.

Fixtures (`fixtures.ts`): a 100 x 50 x 20 box with a 10 mm through hole; a cylinder r 20 h 50; a 600 x 300 x 18 board with 40 holes; an 80 x 60 x 30 box with filleted vertical edges and chamfered top edges; a vase (a cubic Bezier revolved, so B-spline faces); the M1 bracket built through the feature layer (so it is named); a side board with a dado and the shelf in it; a bookshelf of 7 boards (two sides with four dadoes, a back rabbet and 20 shelf-pin holes each, four shelves, a back); a run of 20 base cabinets, 100 boards; and a "large imported STEP": the cabinet run with the bookshelf and four vases in front of it, written to STEP (57,242 entities) and read back as one compound of 954 faces. No real-world STEP file is in the repository, so this stands in for one.

## Findings

### What HLR returns

Exact HLR returns few, exact edges. Per OCCT curve type, the isometric views came back as lines and ellipses (`Ellipse` for every circle seen obliquely), top views as lines and circles; freeform faces gave `BezierCurve` and `BSplineCurve`. The extractor turns `Line` into a line, `Circle` into an arc (centre, radius, start and end angles, counter-clockwise in view coordinates), `Ellipse` into an elliptical arc (centre, semi-axes, rotation, eccentric-anomaly range), and anything else into a polyline within a chord deflection (0.05 mm), unless it is straight. Every analytic record was checked against points sampled from the OCCT curve (`expected.test.ts`).

Three things to know:

- **A circle seen edge on is a `BSplineCurve`.** In the front view of the box, the hole's circles come back as four B-splines (two per circle) whose points all lie on a line. The extractor tests B-splines for straightness (every sample within 1e-6 of the chord, relative) and emits a line. Without that, every hole in a principal view would be a polyline.
- **Coincident edges are reported once in 2D.** Where the seam of a cylinder lies on a silhouette (the box's hole seen from the front: the seam is at +X), the 2D reading has one edge, classed sharp, and no outline there. The 3D reading has both. Where a fillet's tangent edge projects onto a sharp edge (the bracket's top and right views), only the sharp edge is reported.
- **Seams are `sewn`, not `sharp`, when they are not on a silhouette** (the cylinder seen at 45 degrees, or from the right: one visible `RgNLine` down the middle). Drawings leave them out by default.

Smooth edges (`Rg1Line`) are reported apart: the filleted box's front view has 4 visible and 4 hidden smooth edges. Drawings usually omit them or draw them thin, so the op returns them as a class and the drawing decides.

### Correctness against views drawn by hand

`expected.test.ts` describes each view in 3D (segments and circle arcs, with the visibility worked out by hand; the reasoning is in the comments) and compares geometrically: every expected visible (hidden) curve must be covered by actual visible (hidden) sharp or outline edges within 0.08 mm, every actual visible edge must lie on an expected visible curve, and every actual hidden edge on an expected hidden curve or under an expected visible one. Smooth edges were compared too for the bracket.

| Shape                 | Views                  | Exact: mismatches | Poly (mesh 0.01 mm): mismatches | Hidden length under visible lines (exact) |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Box with a 10 mm hole | front, top, right, iso | 0                 | 0                               | 280, 331, 180, 0 mm                       |
| Cylinder r 20, h 50   | front, top, right, iso | 0                 | 0                               | 80, 126, 80, 0 mm                         |
| M1 bracket, t = 6     | front, top, right      | 0                 | 0                               | 228, 268, 220 mm                          |

Nothing else needed changing. The same test checks, for every view, that each box corner projected by `views.ts` is an end point of an HLR line and that `HLRAlgo_Projector.Project` puts it at the same place (both within 1e-9 mm).

### Exact against poly

`timing.test.ts` also measures, per fixture and view, the length drawn by one algorithm and not by the other (sharp and outline edges, per visibility, 0.15 mm tolerance). They agree on everything except:

| Fixture, view             | Disagreement                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| bookshelf, front          | poly draws 1,429 mm visible that exact hides: the vertical edges at X = 12 and 788 where the back panel sits in the rabbets |
| step import, front        | the same 1,429 mm (the bookshelf is in it)                                                                                  |
| filleted box, front/right | 60.5 mm exact calls hidden sharp, poly another class (same geometry)                                                        |
| step import, iso          | 14.5 mm hidden, classed differently (same geometry)                                                                         |

Projected alone, the bookshelf's left side gives the same result in both algorithms; the side with the back panel sitting in its rabbet gives 714.5 mm of the error, half of the whole (`bodies.test.ts`, `polyContact`). Exact is right (the side's front face hides X = 12 in front view; checked by hand). Woodworking models are made of touching boards, so this is the case that matters. With interpenetrating bodies (an earlier version of the STEP fixture overlapped the bookshelf with the first cabinet by mistake; that version is not kept) both algorithms disagreed by short pieces at the overlap, and exact drew a 12 mm hidden piece as visible; with touching, non-overlapping boards exact was right every time it was checked.

### Memory

Destructor audit (`audit.ts`, the method of `spikes/kernel-wrapper/scripts/destructor-audit.ts`): of the 71 HLR classes libcascade binds, 58 have an empty raw destructor. For the ones the op uses:

| Class                                                                       | `delete()` frees |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| `HLRBRep_Algo`, `HLRBRep_PolyAlgo`, `HLRTopoBRep_OutLiner`                  | yes              |
| `HLRBRep_HLRToShape`, `HLRBRep_PolyHLRToShape`                              | no               |
| `HLRAlgo_Projector`, `HLRAppli_ReflectLines`                                | no               |
| `BRepAdaptor_Curve`, `BRepMesh_IncrementalMesh`, indexed maps               | yes              |
| `gp_Ax2`, `gp_Dir`, `gp_Pnt`, `gp_Pnt2d`, `gp_Circ`, `gp_Elips`, `TopoDS_*` | no               |

Heap growth per projection, measured on fresh instances (heap in use after 12 runs less after 2, divided by 10; one probe per instance, the kernel README's method), board with 40 holes, isometric:

| Variant                                                        | Per projection |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| exact, no release rule (only `delete()` and `Nullify()`)       | 640 KiB        |
| exact, `Remove(i)` for every loaded shape before `delete()`    | 70 KiB         |
| exact with the 3D reading and per-item layers                  | 90 KiB         |
| poly, no release rule                                          | 966 KiB        |
| poly, `Remove(i)` and `BRepTools.Clean` on the meshed compound | 205 KiB        |

- **Why `Remove(i)` matters**: `HLRBRep_HLRToShape` holds a handle to the algorithm and is never destroyed (empty destructor), so the algorithm, its data structure and the B-rep of every loaded shape stay alive after `delete()`. Removing the shapes first releases all but the small algorithm object.
- **What is left** (70 KiB for 372 output edges, about 0.2 KB per edge) is the small embind wrappers whose `delete()` frees nothing: the `TopoDS_Edge` read from each result compound, and the `gp_Circ` / `gp_Elips` / `gp_Ax2` / `gp_Pnt` / `gp_Dir` of each arc's geometry. An earlier version that sampled curves with `Value(u)` (a new `gp_Pnt` per point) leaked 150 KiB; reading with `D0(u, p)` into one reused point halved it. Instance recycling bounds the rest (estimate: about 1,000 board-sized views per 70 MiB).
- The embind tracker finds no live object after either algorithm (1,147 objects created by an exact projection with the 3D reading and layers, 2,510 by poly, 0 live).
- One projection of the bookshelf or of the 100-board run, exact or poly, did not grow the wasm memory past its initial 128 MiB.

### Several bodies in one view

`bodies.test.ts`:

- **Per-body layers from one run are exact.** Reading each class per shape (`CompoundOfEdges(S, type, visible, false)`) and taking the union gives the whole-view result with 0 mm missing or extra, on the dado pair, the bookshelf and the cabinet run, all four views. The cost is about the same as one whole-view reading (bookshelf: 15 to 52 ms against 17 to 53 ms; the reading is not the expensive part).
- **Projecting bodies alone and merging is wrong.** Length drawn visible that another body hides: dado pair iso 999 of 3,753 mm, bookshelf iso 10,523 of 13,163 mm, cabinet run iso 125,434 of 175,220 mm (72 %), cabinet run right view 59,390 mm against 2,560 mm that is really visible. The plan's open question (T4.4b, T4.4e risks: "project per item and merge?") has the answer no: every body of a view goes into one HLR run.
- **A translated instance can reuse a projection**: the bracket projected once and its 2D output shifted by the projected offset equals the bracket projected after `transform`, 0 mm difference, all four views. This only holds for a body seen alone (no other body to hide it or be hidden), so it is a cache for single-part views, not for assemblies.

### Section views

The bracket cut with a large box at Y = 0 (`BRepAlgoAPI_Cut` through `Kernel.boolean`, 10.3 ms), then projected from the front (2.9 ms): the planar faces on Y = 0 facing the viewer are the section, 3 faces (the holes split the foot) of 422.6336 mm2 in total, which is the hand-computed value (L of 504 plus the fillet corner 16 (1 - pi/4), less two holes of 4.5 x 1.6 + 8 x 4.4). All 23 edges of those faces are drawn visible. The spike did not chain them into hatch loops; that is plain work for T4.4b (the section faces' wires, read with the same extractor).

### From HLR edges back to model edges

Each class can also be read in 3D (`CompoundOfEdges(type, visible, In3d = true)`): the visible or hidden pieces of model edges, and the silhouette generator lines on faces. Measured on 6 fixtures and 4 views (`assoc.test.ts`):

- The 2D and 3D readings are **not one to one** (the 2D reading merges coincident edges, the 3D one does not), so pieces are matched to 2D edges by geometry: a piece belongs to an edge when its projection lies on it or the other way round. Every 2D edge got at least one piece; many got several (front and back edges, circles seen edge on that lie on an outline).
- Every sharp, smooth and sewn piece lies on a model edge in 3D (2,263 pieces, all matched within 0.01 mm of an edge meshed at 0.001 mm); no outline piece lies on one.
- Cost: the plain projection plus the 3D reading takes 1.0 to 4.0 times the plain projection (bookshelf right view: 15.4 against 52.8 ms; leaving out the first projection of a fresh instance); the matching was done in plain JavaScript here and is not a cost estimate for a real implementation.

So the plan's statement "the HLR result carries no link to the model" is true of the API but not of the data: a link can be computed. It is not needed for M4 (picking below works without it), so the recommendation is to leave it out of T4.4b and keep it in mind for features that must say which model edge a drawn line is (for example highlighting a model edge in a drawing).

### Picking

Clicks every 2 mm along every visible sharp and smooth HLR edge (at least 0.5 mm from its ends), on the box, the bracket, the filleted box, a 10-hole board, the dado pair and the bookshelf, all four views. The right answer is the model edge the clicked line is the image of (from the 3D reading). The candidates are the bodies' mesh edge polylines at display tolerance (0.1 mm), projected with `views.ts`.

| Rule                                                                            | Right edge picked                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| nearest projected edge in 2D (the plan's rule)                                  | 19,666 of 25,102 clicks (78.3 %)  |
| same, and among edges within 0.15 mm of the nearest, the one nearest the viewer | 25,100 of 25,102 clicks (99.99 %) |

The plain rule fails wherever a front and a back edge project onto the same line, which in a principal view is most of the outline: 0 % on the right views of the box, the 10-hole board and the bookshelf and on the bracket's front view, 18 % on the bookshelf's top view. The 2 misses of the depth rule are on the filleted box's isometric view, near the ends of edges, where two edges meet and run within 0.15 mm of each other in the view. A click costs about 0.1 ms in JavaScript on the bookshelf (6,572 clicks in 626 ms, a linear scan over every projected segment).

Clicks on silhouettes (outline edges) were counted but not scored: a silhouette is not a model edge, so the nearest-edge rule cannot be right there. Picking a silhouette must pick the face (see the dimension model); not prototyped.

### Dimensions

`dimension.ts` and `dimension.test.ts` prototype decision 7 on the M1 bracket:

1. References are picked in a view: a vertex by the nearest projected vertex (nearest the viewer on ties) and `pickVertex`; an edge by the depth-rule pick above and `pickEdge`; a face by its name (`pickFace`). They are stored as the kernel's own reference types: `{ vertex: VertexRef }`, `{ edge: EdgeRef }`, `{ face: FaceRef }`.
2. To place a dimension, each reference is resolved on the current model (`resolveVertex`, `resolve`), its exact geometry read with `Kernel.measure` (vertex point; line end points or circle centre, radius and axis; plane or cylinder axis and radius), and projected with the view's frame in TypeScript.
3. The value is the projected distance for linear dimensions (|dx| horizontal, |dy| vertical, the 2D distance for aligned, with a warning when the two points differ in depth, that is, foreshortened), and the model radius for radius and diameter (true size), with the anchors the drawing lays out from: a circle when the axis is along the view direction, the two silhouette lines when it is across, and a warning when it is oblique.

| Dimension (stored references)                                      | t = 6      | t = 8      | t = 8, fillet deleted |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- | ---------- | --------------------- |
| foot thickness, vertical, two vertices picked in front view        | 6, exact   | 8, exact   | 8, exact              |
| height, vertical, two vertices                                     | 40, exact  | 40, exact  | 40, exact             |
| hole spacing, horizontal, two counterbore edges picked in top view | 15, exact  | 15, exact  | 15, exact             |
| hole diameter, `hole#1:wall:e7`, top view (circle)                 | 4.5, exact | 4.5, exact | 4.5, exact            |
| hole diameter, same face, front view (silhouettes, hidden)         | 4.5, exact | 4.5, exact | 4.5, exact            |
| fillet radius, `fillet#1:round:r2`, front view (circle)            | 4, exact   | 4, exact   | lost                  |

Formatting through `packages/units` `formatLength`: 900 mm as `ft-in` /16 gives `2' 11-7/16"`, 1028.7 mm gives `3' 4-1/2"` (the plan's example), 18 mm as `in-fraction` /32 gives `23/32"`, all as computed by hand.

What cannot be dimensioned associatively (by reasoning, not measured):

- **Silhouettes of faces other than cylinders seen across their axis**: a cone seen obliquely, a sphere (its outline is a circle of the sphere's radius, so a diameter works, but not a linear dimension "to the silhouette"), a torus, any B-spline face. The outline depends on the view and is not a named entity, and there is no closed form to recompute it from a reference. Such dimensions need the user to dimension a real edge or vertex instead.
- **Apparent intersections**: points that exist only in the drawing (where two edges cross in projection, an edge meets a silhouette, edges of two different bodies meet in a view).
- **Anything picked on an HLR result line itself** (it carries no name); picks must always go through model edges, vertices and faces.
- **Values in an oblique view** are foreshortened; they can be associative but are not true lengths, so a drawing should warn (the prototype does) or refuse them.

## Recommendations

### T4.4b: the `project` op

Exact algorithm only, every item of a view in one run, results per item.

```ts
interface ProjectItem {
  shape: ShapeId;
  /** Where the body sits in the view's model space (an assembly instance's pose). */
  transform?: Placement;
  /** Echoed back so the caller can map layers to bodies and instances. */
  key: string;
}

interface ProjectView {
  /** From the viewer into the scene. Orthographic only in M4. */
  direction: Vec3;
  /** Up on paper; not parallel to `direction`. */
  up: Vec3;
  /** Frame origin (default the world origin); only shifts the output. */
  origin?: Vec3;
}

interface ProjectOptions {
  /** Default true. Hidden edges are always computed; this only drops them from the reply. */
  hidden?: boolean;
  /** Tangent edges (Rg1Line). Default true: the drawing decides how to draw them. */
  smooth?: boolean;
  /** Seams (RgNLine). Default false. */
  sewn?: boolean;
  /** Chord tolerance for polylines (freeform curves), model mm. Default 0.05. */
  deflection?: number;
  /** Cut every item by the half space behind this plane first and return the section loops. */
  section?: { origin: Vec3; normal: Vec3 };
}

type EdgeClass = 'sharp' | 'smooth' | 'sewn' | 'outline';

/** In view coordinates, model mm: x right, y up on paper. Arcs run counter-clockwise from start to end. */
type Curve2 =
  | { kind: 'line'; a: Vec2; b: Vec2 }
  | { kind: 'arc'; center: Vec2; radius: number; start: number; end: number }
  | {
      kind: 'ellipseArc';
      center: Vec2;
      major: number;
      minor: number;
      rotation: number;
      start: number;
      end: number;
    }
  | { kind: 'polyline'; points: Vec2[] };

interface ProjectedEdge {
  /** Index into `items`. */
  item: number;
  cls: EdgeClass;
  visible: boolean;
  curve: Curve2;
}

interface ProjectResult {
  edges: ProjectedEdge[];
  /** 2D bounding box of every returned edge. */
  bounds: { min: Vec2; max: Vec2 };
  /** With `section`: per item, the closed loops of its section faces (outer first), for hatching. */
  sections?: { item: number; loops: Curve2[][] }[];
}

// Kernel.project(items, view, options): ProjectResult
```

Rules the implementation should follow, all from this spike:

- **One run per view**: `Add` every item (placed by `transform` first) to one `HLRBRep_Algo`, then read each class per item with `CompoundOfEdges(S, type, visible, false)`, which is exact and costs no more. Never project items alone and merge.
- **View frame**: `gp_Ax2(origin, -direction, normalize(up x -direction))`; the output is then (dot(p - o, x), dot(p - o, y)) with y = z x x, which `packages/drawing` and regen can reproduce in TypeScript (`views.ts` is the reference; tested equal to `HLRAlgo_Projector.Project` within 1e-9).
- **Curves**: map `GeomAbs_Line`, `Circle`, `Ellipse` as `hlr.ts` does (orientation from the sign of the conic's axis Z); test every other curve for straightness before making it a polyline (circles seen edge on are B-splines).
- **Coincident hidden lines**: return them; `packages/drawing` drops hidden segments that lie under visible ones before DXF (they are 0 to 330 mm per view on the fixtures). A pure 2D step is easier to test there than in the kernel.
- **Memory**, in `releaseOwned` or the op's scope: call `Remove(i)` for every loaded shape (`NbShapes()` down to 1) on `HLRBRep_Algo` before `delete()`; never sample curves with `Value(u)`, use `D0(u, p)` into one owned `gp_Pnt`; `Nullify()` every result compound and edge; `HLRBRep_HLRToShape` and `HLRAlgo_Projector` are deleted but free nothing, so do not create them in loops. Add a leak test like `memory.test.ts` (tracker plus heap per projection on fresh instances) to `leaks.test.ts`.
- **Poly**: leave `HLRBRep_PolyAlgo` out of M4. It was slower on every single-body fixture, it drew hidden edges visible where boards touch, and its output (thousands of chords) is worse for DXF. If a fallback is wanted later for very large imports, gate it on size (estimate: above about 5,000 faces, where exact extrapolates to about 1 s per view from the STEP fixture's 954 faces at 100 to 180 ms) and mark the view as approximate.
- **No 3D reading by default**: it costs up to 4 times the projection and nothing in M4 needs it. If later needed, match pieces by geometry, not by order.
- **Caching** (T4.4e): a view is one unit, keyed by every item's shape key and pose, the view and the options. A single-part view of a translated instance can reuse another instance's view shifted in 2D; an assembly view cannot.

Expected costs for the bookshelf drawing (estimate from the table): four views of the bookshelf take about 122 ms of exact HLR in Node, once per edit of the model, in the regen worker; T0.2 measured browser-worker times within about 10 % of Node for the M1 pipeline (73.6 against 68.3 ms), so a similar figure in the browser is expected but was not measured here.

### T4.4c, T4.4d, T4.4e: the dimension model

A dimension stores model references only, as the kernel's existing types:

```ts
type DimensionRef = (
  | { vertex: VertexRef } // the faces around the vertex (+ ordinal when needed)
  | { edge: EdgeRef } // the faces along the edge (+ ends, ordinal)
  | { face: FaceRef } // a face name: planes, and cylinders for radius, diameter and silhouettes
) & {
  /** Which body in the view's source (a part studio body id). */
  body: string;
  /** Assembly views: the instance path from the view's assembly to the part. */
  instance?: string[];
};

interface Dimension {
  id: string; // 'dim#n'
  view: string; // 'view#n'
  kind: 'horizontal' | 'vertical' | 'aligned' | 'radius' | 'diameter' | 'angle';
  /** Two for linear and angle dimensions, one for radius and diameter. */
  refs: DimensionRef[];
  /** Where the dimension line sits, in paper mm from the anchors (dragged by the user). */
  offset: number;
  text?: string;
  precision?: number;
}
```

How regen (T4.4e) resolves one, as prototyped:

1. Resolve each reference on its body's names with the same functions features use (`resolveVertex`, `resolve`), reporting `exact`, `fragile`, `ambiguous` or `lost` per dimension (the worst of its references). A deleted target makes the dimension `lost`, never moves it.
2. Read the exact geometry with `measure` (no mesh, no HLR): a vertex point; a line edge's end points or a circle's centre, radius and axis; a plane, or a cylinder's axis and radius.
3. Apply the instance's pose, project with the view frame in TypeScript, and hand `packages/drawing` only projected anchors: two points for linear dimensions (a circle edge anchors at its centre, a line edge at its midpoint unless the kind says otherwise), a circle, or two silhouette lines. The value is computed from the projected anchors (linear) or the model radius (radius, diameter), and formatted with `packages/units` in the document's display units.
4. Warn, do not fail, when a value is foreshortened (aligned across depth, a circle or cylinder oblique to the view).

Picking (T4.4e and T4.4g): regen sends, per view, the pickable model entities with their references: edge polylines (the mesh's `edgePositions` and `edgeRanges`, already per edge with names), vertex points with their `VertexRef`, and cylinder faces with axis and radius (from `topology()`), all in model space with the item's pose. The app projects them with the view frame and picks the nearest within the pick radius, breaking ties within 0.15 mm (paper-scaled in the app) by depth, nearest the viewer first. That is 99.99 % right on the fixtures and needs no kernel call per click. Clicks near a silhouette pick the cylinder face whose projected silhouette is nearest (not prototyped).

## Where this differs from the plan

- **Part 1 and decision 7 say picking takes the nearest projected named edge.** Measured: 78.3 % right overall and 0 % on most right views. A depth tie-break is required (99.99 %). The data regen sends must therefore keep depth (3D points, not pre-projected 2D polylines), and vertices and cylinder faces need to be pickable too.
- **Part 1 says the poly algorithm is the fallback.** It is wrong where boards touch (bookshelf front, 1,429 mm), so it should not be a silent fallback for woodworking models. Exact handled every fixture, including a 954-face STEP compound in under 0.2 s.
- **Part 1 says there is no bound path from a result edge back to the model edge.** True of the API, but the 3D reading (`In3d`) gives a geometric path that worked on every piece tested. Not needed for M4.
- **T4.4b's risk "project per item and merge only for hidden-line classification"**: merging separate projections is wrong (up to 72 % of the visible length), and per-item layers from one run are exact at no extra cost, so there is nothing to trade off.
- **Part 1's "10 hidden silhouettes where 20 were expected"** is explained: the seam lies on one silhouette of each hole and is reported once, as sharp.
- **Exact output is not only lines, arcs and ellipse arcs**: circles seen edge on arrive as B-splines and need the straightness test; freeform faces give B-spline and Bezier edges, returned as polylines.
- **T4.4b's memory rule**: the plan asks whether the HLR classes need a `releaseOwned` rule. They do: `Remove(i)` on the algorithm before `delete()` (640 to 70 KiB per projection), and point sampling through `D0`.

## Not measured

- **Time in a module worker in Chromium.** Not run. Estimate: close to Node, as T0.2 measured for the M1 pipeline (within about 10 %).
- **The bracket's isometric view against a hand-drawn view**: the bracket was checked in front, top and right; box and cylinder in all four.
- **A real-world STEP file**: none in the repository; the STEP fixture is generated.
- **Very large imports** (thousands of faces and up): the 5,000-face threshold for a poly fallback is an extrapolation.
- **Section hatch loops**: the section faces and their edges were verified, the loops were not built.
- **Picking silhouettes and vertices at scale**: the vertex pick was used for the dimension prototype (4 clicks, all right) but not measured over many clicks; silhouette picks were not scored.
- **`HLRAppli_ReflectLines`**: bound, not used. It wraps the same algorithm with no per-shape output, so it adds nothing here.
