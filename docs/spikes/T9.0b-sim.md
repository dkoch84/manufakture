## Summary

- **The lumped model gives believable numbers, and its bookkeeping is exact.** Every reported run closes its energy ledger to a relative residual under 10⁻⁸ (target 1 %), and its thermal ledger to rounding. Built from two published motor datasheets (five windings), it reproduces their speed-torque lines within 1.4 % of no-load speed and their efficiency curves within 1.4 points.
- **The cable trainer at 200 lbf (890 N), direct drive, Kt 0.5 N·m/A, 25 mm spool:** 22.2 N·m holding (23.4 N·m peak on the return stroke), 44.5 A peak phase current (31.5 A rms), 119 W of copper loss when cold and 126 W after 30 s. Each pull takes 534 J from the user. 434 J of it reaches the DC bus, peaking at 1,156 W (690 W mean over the pull). At 50 % charge the pack accepts 115 J of that (its 2C limit) and the braking resistor burns 319 J. On a full pack the pack accepts 8 J and the resistor burns 426 J.
- **Winding temperature from 25 °C:** 35.7 °C after 10 reps, 39.6 °C after a 30 s hold, 45.0 °C at the end of a 5 × 10 session. Across a 3 × 3 grid of winding heat capacity and thermal resistance (half to twice the assumed values), the hold ends between 32 and 55 °C. The belt variant's smaller motor reaches 74.7 °C after the hold and 93.7 °C over the session.
- **Energy per session:** 10.9 Wh from the pack for 5 sets of 10 reps at 200 lbf (466 s, starting at 50 %). 41 % of it goes to the resistor, 33 % to copper and 12 % to the pack's own resistance. A full 97.9 Wh pack gives 8 such sessions before the cell voltage under load falls below 2.8 V. A pack that accepted every regenerated watt would need 7.0 Wh a session: the charge limit costs 36 %.
- **Three of the plan's illustrative values change.** The plan's 160 W hold loss is a DC formula. Read as a datasheet would quote it (0.08 Ω line to line, Kt per peak amp, sinusoidal currents), the loss is 119 W; with 0.08 Ω per phase it would be 237 W. The plan's 530 J per pull is the user's work; 434 J of it reaches the bus. The 4.8 kJ of a 30 s hold is 3.7 kJ, which warms the winding by 15 K.
- **New: the return stroke is as hard on the pack as regeneration is.** Driving the cable back in at 200 lbf draws up to 20 A from the pack (12C on 1.7 Ah cells) and sags it to 51 V at 50 % charge. Below about 15 % charge the cells fall under 2.8 V during each return. The pack needs high discharge capability too, or the firmware must cap the force or speed at low charge.
- **Integrator and step:** use the implicit midpoint rule. With every power evaluated at the midpoint, it conserves the quadratic storages (inductance, inertia, heat) exactly, so the ledger becomes a check on the bookkeeping rather than on the step. For T9.4b, simulate the currents quasi-statically at 1 ms: this agrees with the full current-loop model at 10 µs to within 1 × 10⁻⁴ on every ledger entry and on the temperature rise, and a session takes about 50 ms instead of 1.5 s.
- **Error against the datasheets** (maxon EC 90 flat and FAULHABER 4221 BXT H): no-load speed within 1.1 %, stall torque 0.6 %, slope 0.5 %, maximum efficiency within 0.1 points (maxon) and 2.1 points (FAULHABER), mechanical time constant within 0.4 % (maxon). Larger errors trace to the datasheets, not to the model. At the rated point the model's current is 8 to 13 % above the published one, because the makers' rated torque implies a torque constant 2 to 7 % above their listed one. maxon's thermal continuous current comes out 7 % and 14 % high from its own thermal resistances.

## The model

One degree of freedom along the drivetrain, as decision 6 says. The user is a prescribed motion: cable position and speed against time, with a target cable force. The controller is ideal force control. It feeds forward the inertia and the loss torques, so the cable carries the target force, unless the current or the voltage limit intervenes. The force the user actually feels comes out of the shaft's torque balance, `J·α = Te + F·r·η±/G − T_loss`, where the efficiencies divide or multiply according to which way power flows.

- **Motor:** a surface-magnet PMSM in the rotor frame (Ld = Lq, amplitude-invariant dq). `v_d = R·i_d + L·di_d/dt − ω_e·L·i_q` and `v_q = R·i_q + L·di_q/dt + ω_e·(L·i_d + λ)`, with `T = 1.5·p·λ·i_q = Kt·i_q` and `i_d = 0`. Copper loss is `1.5·R(T_w)·|i|²`. Friction is Coulomb plus viscous, and iron loss is hysteresis (∝ ω) plus eddy (∝ ω²), all at the shaft. For the datasheets the same code runs in six-step mode: the DC equivalent of block commutation, with R and L line to line and k = (3√3/π)·p·λ, which is how both makers rate their motors.
- **Controller:** two modes. _Full_ runs a PI current loop with decoupling (bandwidth 1 kHz, anti-windup by conditional integration), clamps the voltage vector to `m·Vbus/√3`, and integrates the RL plant. _Quasi-static_ lets the currents follow their references within the step and takes the voltages from the steady equations. Losses are fixed, plus conduction (`1.5·R_on·|i|²`) and switching (∝ Vbus·|i|).
- **Pack and chopper:** `P = (OCV(SoC) − R·I)·I` is solved for I. Charging is capped by the acceptance limit, and the chopper sends the surplus to the resistor, flagging any step where the surplus exceeds `V²/R`. State of charge counts coulombs. The pack's chemical energy is the exact integral of the piecewise-linear OCV curve, so the ledger takes the pack term from the state rather than from a quadrature.
- **Thermal:** two nodes. Copper loss goes into the winding; iron and friction go into the stator and housing; the housing goes to ambient.

### Integrator and step size

Every state (currents, temperatures, the drivetrain's prescribed speed) advances by the theta method. With θ = 0.5 this is the implicit midpoint rule, which is second order and A-stable, so the stiff RL plant (τ = L/R = 2.5 ms against a 2 s rep) and the slow thermal nodes need no step-size care for stability. Its decisive property here: for a linear storage `½·L·i²`, `½·J·ω²` or `C·T`, the midpoint rule's discrete power `L·(i₁ − i₀)/dt · (i₀ + i₁)/2` is exactly the change of stored energy. So when every power in the ledger is evaluated at the midpoint, the ledger closes to rounding whatever the step. The residual that remains comes from the pack's OCV, which is taken at the start of each step: about 6 × 10⁻⁹ at 50 µs, falling linearly with the step. Backward Euler (θ = 1) dissipates `½·L·Δi²` and `½·J·Δω²` per step. That residual is still far below 1 % (2 × 10⁻⁶ at 50 µs, 4 × 10⁻⁴ at 10 ms), but it is spurious loss with no physical cause.

Accuracy is a separate question from the ledger, and was measured against a 10 µs midpoint run of 10 reps (`convergence.json`):

**Recommendation for T9.4b:** the implicit midpoint rule, quasi-static currents and a 1 ms step for sessions and envelopes. The full current-loop mode at 50 µs is worth keeping for the control specification (T9.9d), where transients such as force steps, the reversal and a voltage limit are the point. A 466 s session costs 1.5 s in full mode and about 50 ms quasi-static, so T9.4b's 2 s budget is met with room for a sizing study's many runs.

The plan's 160 W comes from `I²·R` with 45 A and 0.08 Ω, a DC formula. A FOC motor's copper loss is `1.5·R_phase·i_peak²`, which is `3·R_phase·i_rms²`: here 1.5 × 0.04 × 44.5² = 119 W. A session takes 12.6 Wh, and the pack lasts 7 sessions. With its smaller thermal mass, the belt motor's winding is the tighter limit: 94 °C after one session, with the housing still rising.

## Against two published datasheets

Both datasheets are DC-equivalent ratings of block-commutated brushless motors, so the model runs in six-step mode with each sheet's terminal resistance and inductance (line to line), torque constant, rotor inertia and pole pairs. Its friction and iron loss come from the published no-load current at the published no-load speed: the loss torque `k·I0` is split half constant (bearings, hysteresis) and half proportional to speed (eddy, windage). For maxon, its two thermal resistances are used as well. Everything else is predicted and compared (`datasheets.json`). The published curves are drawn the way the makers draw their catalog curves: a straight speed-torque line through the no-load speed with the published slope, and efficiency from current `I0 + M/k`.

Consistency checks, which are not predictions: starting current U/R within 0.15 %; the no-load current the loss model is fitted to within 0.5 %; speed constant times torque constant within 0.33 % of 30/π on every winding; and with inductance and loss removed, the time-stepped spin-up gives the datasheets' `J·R/k²` within 0.36 %, which checks the integrator.

**Model error, stated:** on the curves a datasheet publishes (the speed-torque line and the efficiency curve), the model is within 1.4 % of no-load speed and 1.4 efficiency points; on maximum efficiency, within 0.1 points for maxon and 2.1 points for FAULHABER. The larger errors trace to the datasheets rather than to the model:

- **Rated points** are not consistent with the same sheet's torque constant. Every rated torque is 2 to 7 % more than `k × rated current` (maxon 24 V: 0.387 N·m against 0.0705 × 5.39 = 0.380), so the makers do not deduct the no-load loss torque. The model does deduct it, adding `k·I0` (6 to 10 % of maxon's rated torques), and so needs 8 to 13 % more current. FAULHABER's rated speeds come from a thermal operating area with an unstated flange, not from the nominal-voltage line, so they are not compared.
- **FAULHABER 48 V's 88 %** cannot be reached with its own no-load and starting currents. The classical bound `(1 − √(I0/I_A))²` gives 85.6 %, and the model gives 85.9 %.
- **FAULHABER's mechanical time constants** are the definition `J·R/k²`, which ignores inductance. On these motors the electrical time constant (0.86 to 0.98 ms) is a quarter of the mechanical one, so a real spin-up is 7 to 11 % slower. The model includes the inductance. On maxon's motor, whose electrical time constant is 3 to 7 % of the mechanical one, the two agree within 0.4 %.
- **maxon's continuous current** comes out 7 % (24 V) and 14 % (48 V) above the published one from its own thermal resistances, with the copper's temperature coefficient and the no-load loss heating the housing. maxon's thermal resistances are for a stated mounting that the model does not know. A 7 to 14 % overestimate of continuous current is the uncertainty to carry into the thermal check (T9.5): allow 15 %.
- **Loss split.** Moving the split between constant and speed-proportional loss changes nothing compared here at the fitted speed, but it moves the no-load loss predicted at other speeds by tens of percent. A second published point (a no-load current at another voltage, or a loss curve) would pin it. For the cable trainer, iron and friction are 0.8 % of a session's energy, so this does not move its results.

## Cross-check against the Voltra's published sessions per charge

T9.0c found that the Voltra's help centre gives about 6 sessions per charge at 100 lb and 14 at 60 lb. Whatever the session length, that is an energy ratio of 2.33 between the two forces. The modelled ratio for the same rep at 100 and 60 lbf (`forceSweep`) is 2.48 for the direct drive and 2.30 for the belt variant, so the shape of losses against force matches. The absolute value does not match yet. The model gives 243 J a rep at 100 lbf (direct drive), against T9.0c's estimate of about 500 J. That estimate assumed 117 reps a session, a figure the help centre does not publish. Either the Voltra's sessions are about twice that long, or it recovers less of the pull than the modelled 2C acceptance allows, or its losses are higher. T9.4b should fit these two published points, as T9.0c suggests, once the session definition is known.

## What this means for M9

- **Decision 6 holds.** One degree of freedom, datasheet constants, a two-node thermal model and a pack curve give numbers that land on published datasheet curves within about 1.5 %. They also turn up effects nobody finds by drawing the box: 12C discharge on the return stroke, a 36 % energy cost from charge acceptance, and a resistor that takes 41 % of the pack's energy.
- **T9.4b** can lift `src/model.ts` nearly as it is: the theta method at θ = 0.5, quasi-static currents at 1 ms, and the ledger entries above as calc records (decision 4). The ledger test (closure below 10⁻⁶, not just 1 %) is a cheap guard against a forgotten loss term.
- **T9.2b (motor catalog)** must record the resistance and inductance convention (line to line or phase) and the torque-constant convention (peak or rms phase current, or a DC constant for six-step), or the same numbers give copper losses a factor of two apart. The six-step factor 3√3/π is how a DC-rated datasheet becomes a FOC model.
- **T9.2c (cells) and T9.7c** need discharge pulse capability as well as charge acceptance: the return stroke at 200 lbf draws 12C.
- **T9.5 (checks)** should carry a 15 % allowance on thermally limited continuous current derived from datasheet thermal resistances.
- **Still to test:** the thermal parameters of a real candidate motor (T9.0c), the cells' own OCV curve and pulse resistance, the other resistance modes (eccentric, chains, isokinetic), and the field weakening the model clamps but does not yet do. At 200 lbf and 1.5 m/s the voltage vector stays at 0.56 of what the bus offers, so field weakening is not needed for this rep.
